Why do agents need a world model?

to pick an action that pays off later, you have to know what it will do before you do it, and trying everything for real is slow, expensive or fatal. A world model lets an agent try actions in imagination and keep the best. A 2025 theorem says any agent that generalises across multi-step goals already contains one. The live question is which kind: one that paints pixels, one that builds 3D, or one that predicts in a compact latent space.

Beat 1

Why isn't a good policy enough?

Because a policy is a lookup from situations to actions for the goals it was trained on. Give it a new goal and it has nothing to say. A model of how the world responds can be reused for any goal: search it for the actions that reach the new one.

Richens, Abel, Bellot and Everitt (DeepMind, ICML 2025) turned the intuition into a theorem: any agent that generalises to multi-step goal-directed tasks with bounded regret must contain a predictive model of its environment, and that model can be extracted from its policy. Model-free agents that generalise are not model-free; their model is just implicit. So the question moved from "whether" to "which kind, and made explicit how".

The idea is old. Schmidhuber trained recurrent world models for controllers in 1990. Ha and Schmidhuber's World Models (2018) made it vivid with three parts, and trained the controller entirely inside the model's own dream before running it in the real game.

Beat 2

How does an agent plan inside its model?

By guessing many action sequences, imagining where each one leads, keeping the best few, and guessing again near them. After a few rounds it executes only the first action, looks at the world again, and replans. That is CEM inside MPC, and it is how V-JEPA 2-AC, DINO-WM and LeWorldModel control robots.

The cross-entropy method needs no gradients through the model, only the ability to roll it forward and score the end state. Model predictive control wraps it: plan H steps ahead, act once, observe, repeat. In a JEPA world model the score is the distance between the predicted embedding and the embedding of a goal image, so the agent never renders a pixel.

V-JEPA 2-AC pretrained on more than 1M hours of video, then post-trained on under 62 hours of DROID robot data, and did zero-shot pick-and-place on Franka arms by planning toward image goals. It reported about 16 s per action against about 4 minutes for the pixel-generative Cosmos baseline check. DINO-WM (2024) plans the same way on frozen DINOv2 patch features. PLDM (2025) showed that latent dynamics with VICReg-style regularisation, learned from reward-free, suboptimal offline data, generalise better than model-free RL. LeWorldModel (2026) encodes each frame as one 192-d vector instead of a grid of patches and plans in 0.98 s against 47 s for DINO-WM.

planning cost per action ≈ samples K × iterations I × horizon H × cost of one predictor call The edge-efficiency notes' rule of thumb. LeWM's speedup comes mostly from the last factor: one vector per frame instead of a patch grid makes each predictor call cheap.
Beat 3

Why do imagined futures drift?

Because the model's output becomes its next input. A small one-step error is fed back and grows, slowly in a calm world, exponentially in a world where small differences get amplified. Looking at the real world again resets the error, which is why MPC replans every step.

This is LeCun's compounding-error argument against autoregressive generation, applied to dynamics. It is also why video world models drift: the project's notes put Genie-class generators at "drifts after about a minute", and Genie 3's visual memory is about one minute. Dreamer trains its policy on imagined rollouts only about 15 steps long. MuZero sidesteps the problem by never predicting observations at all, only the quantities planning needs.

The lab runs a real billiard simulation twice: the true one, and a "learned model" whose only flaw is that it turns the ball's velocity by a tiny angle ε each step. Round pegs make the system chaotic: a slightly different angle of approach to a peg leaves at a very different angle.

Beat 4

Why are there three camps?

Because "predict the world" can mean predicting what you would see, what is there, or only what matters for acting. Each choice buys something different: pixels are checkable and playable, 3D is persistent and editable, latents are fast and small. Each pays for it somewhere else.

Camp 1 · generate pixels

Paint the next frame

  • Genie 3 (DeepMind, 2025-08): real-time 720p at 24 fps, about a minute of visual memory, text-promptable world events.
  • Project Genie (2026-01): the first productised world model, 60-second sessions.
  • Veo 3, NVIDIA Cosmos, Odyssey-2 (720p at 22 fps), Wayve GAIA-3 (15B parameters, used to validate driving policies).

Buys: you can watch it, play it, check it. Pays: compute per frame, and drift after about a minute.

Camp 2 · explicit 3D

Build the scene

  • Marble (World Labs, Fei-Fei Li, 2025-11): persistent, editable, exportable 3D worlds as Gaussian splats and meshes, from text, images or video.
  • DeepMind's D4RT: a query-able 4D scene representation from video.

Buys: persistence and editing. Pays: no dynamics or physics yet.

Camp 3 · latent prediction

Predict only what matters

  • V-JEPA 2-AC, DINO-WM, PLDM, LeWorldModel.
  • AMI Labs (LeCun, Paris): $1.03B seed at $3.5B on 2026-03-10, aiming at non-generative world models with open publication.
  • Dreamer sits in between: latent dynamics, plus a pixel decoder for training.

Buys: fast planning, small models, robustness. Pays: hard to inspect, and fragile on some tasks.

Picture it

A pixel world model is a film director who must shoot every frame before deciding whether the plot works. A 3D world model is a set builder whose sets never move. A latent world model is a chess player who imagines "knight there, then pressure on the king" without picturing the wood grain of the pieces.

What would settle itHead-to-head planning success and cost on shared robot tasks (the project's fault-line table). As of October 2026 nobody has run that comparison at scale, and Gemini Robotics 2 (2026-07) is still a vision-language-action model, not a world-model controller.
Beat 5

Why can a latent be decodable but not plannable?

Because a planner uses distances, not information. A latent can contain every bit of the state, so a probe decodes it perfectly, and still measure distance so badly that "closer in latent space" points the wrong way. SCALE (2026) calls this: decodable is not plannable.

Latent planners minimise ‖z(sT) − z(goal)‖². Under that squared-Euclidean cost, each latent direction counts in proportion to its variance (SCALE: its weight is twice its variance). If the encoder spreads some irrelevant feature (texture, lighting, a periodic pattern) over a high-variance direction, the planner chases it. If it squashes a relevant direction, the planner ignores it. Hu et al. add a regulariser that aligns latent distances with state distances, and report beating LeWM on all 15 task-solver averages.

The lab builds a latent that is perfectly decodable: it contains x and y exactly, plus a wavy "texture" feature. A linear probe recovers the position with R² = 1. Then it plans greedily by latent distance.

For this projectThe project's edge notes draw the same line: shrinking the state to one vector (LeWM) cuts planning cost, but "a compact state with the wrong metric plans badly". A related 2026 preprint finds that when little of the frame moves, the static background drowns the moving part in the embedding distance check. Build idea #3 (a pocket world model with a latent-size sweep) can test the Cui et al. (2026) bound, where latent dimension trades approximation against sample complexity.
Beat 6

Why do Sutton, LeCun and Chollet disagree?

Because they agree a world model is needed and disagree on where it comes from: from an agent's own stream of experience (Sutton), from self-supervised prediction in latent space (LeCun), or from efficient program-like abstraction tested on brand-new worlds (Chollet).

Richard Sutton

"Learn from your own experience."

The Bitter Lesson (2019): general methods that use computation, search and learning, win. LLMs imitate text without goals or ground truth, so they are a dead end for intelligence. The Oak architecture (2025 keynote) builds features, subproblems, options and models at runtime from experience alone. With Silver: the Era of Experience.

Yann LeCun

"Predict in representation space, then plan."

Autoregressive LLMs lack persistent memory, planning and grounded world models, and token-level generation compounds errors. World models should be trained by self-supervision in an abstract space (JEPA), energy-based and non-generative, and used for model-predictive planning under objectives and guardrails. Left Meta in November 2025 to found AMI Labs.

François Chollet

"Measure how fast you learn a new world."

ARC-AGI-3 (2026-03): 135 interactive games, no instructions, scored on action efficiency. Agents must explore, infer the goal, build a world model and plan. Frontier models scored under 1% at launch; humans 100%. The early winners write code world models. See chapter 09.

Genie 3 team

"Make the world playable."

Jack Parker-Holder and Shlomi Fruchter (MLST, 2025-08): generate pixels in real time, so agents and people can step inside. DeepMind uses the result as an unlimited environment generator for agents such as SIMA 2.

For this projectARC-AGI-3 is effectively a world-model-learning benchmark. A small action-conditioned latent world model (LeWM-style) on ARC-AGI-3 games is a laptop-sized JEPA experiment nobody seems to have tried; the 2026 winners used code world models. The map of who stands where: Why Atlas and chapter 15. Why the latent should be small enough for a robot: chapter 12.

Three things to remember

  1. Imagine many, act once.Sample futures in the model, keep the best, take one step, look again. A theorem says generalising agents carry such a model anyway.
  2. Small errors compound; looking resets them.Imagined rollouts drift, exponentially in chaotic worlds. Short horizons and frequent re-observation beat a slightly better model.
  3. Decodable is not plannable.A latent can hold every bit of the state and still point the planner the wrong way. Planning needs the right geometry, not just the right information.
