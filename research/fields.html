Why do 23 fields belong on one map?

fourteen AI fields and nine artificial-life fields are built from the same five mechanisms: prediction, search, emergence, compression and energy. A cellular automaton and a world model differ in substrate, not in kind. Once you see the shared mechanism, a result in one field becomes an experiment in the other, and most of those experiments fit on a laptop.

Beat 1

Why put AI and artificial life on the same map?

Because both ask how structure appears without anyone writing it down. AI gets it from a loss and gradient descent; artificial life gets it from local rules and selection. The questions (what is learned, in what order, how to find the interesting parts) are the same questions.

The map below comes from the project's field survey. Part A is fourteen frontier AI fields, from world models (A1) to robot foundation models (A14). Part B is nine artificial-life fields, from neural cellular automata (B1) to body-brain co-evolution (B9). For each field: the core idea, a landmark, how it touches JEPA, and whether an Apple M5 laptop with 16 GB can run a meaningful experiment.

Three things happened in 2024 to 2026 that made the two halves impossible to keep apart. Foundation models became the judges of "interesting" in artificial-life search (ASAL, 2024). Cellular-automaton dynamics became pretraining data for language models: 164M tokens of NCA beat 1.6B tokens of real text as a warm-up (Kumar et al., 2026). And the FER hypothesis argued that an evolved network and an SGD network with identical outputs can have opposite insides, which made the process that builds a network a first-class question for ML. See chapter 13 and chapter 14 for the mechanics.

Picture it

Think of the 23 fields as 23 kitchens and the five mechanisms as five cooking techniques: roasting, braising, fermenting, reducing, curing. Recipes look nothing alike from kitchen to kitchen, but a cook who knows fermentation can walk into any of them. Learn the technique once and you can read the whole map.

ProvenanceThe field survey (fields/ai-alife-field-map.md) was written from model knowledge current to about mid-2026, with no fresh literature search. Landmark dates are well established; the "Link to JEPA" column and the five bridges are proposals, not results. The landscape dossiers (chapter 15) add 2025-26 evidence where it exists.
Beat 2

What are the 23 fields, and why does each one matter here?

Because each field is a different answer to "where does useful structure come from?", and each has a concrete hook into the JEPA project: a feature dictionary to read, a predictor to swap, a judge to replace.

Beat 3

Why does "runs on a laptop" belong on a research map?

Because in 2025-26 a large share of the results that changed minds were laptop-sized: a 7M-parameter TRM on ARC, a 76K-parameter CompressARC, a 15M-parameter LeWorldModel planning 48× faster than DINO-WM. Feasibility decides which questions one person can actually answer.

The survey's legend: ✅ meaningful experiments on an M5 with 16 GB, 🟡 small-scale toy versions only, ❌ needs a cluster (or, for basal cognition, a wet lab). Seventeen fields get ✅, five get 🟡, one gets ❌. The landscape synthesis gives the reason this is not a consolation prize: compute spent on the problem at hand (iteration, search, test-time training) is now often worth more than compute spent memorizing in advance. Sara Hooker's hardware lottery adds the other half: ideas that do not fit big GPUs got less attention, so non-transformer designs (SSMs, liquid nets, spiking nets, hyperdimensional computing) are under-explored, and edge hardware is where they get a second chance.

Beat 4

Why do fields that never cite each other share machinery?

Because there are only a few ways to get structure for free: predict what comes next, search a space and keep what scores, let local rules interact, compress, or roll downhill on an energy. Tag each field with the mechanisms it relies on and the hidden neighbours appear.

The tags below are this chapter's own reading of the survey, one line of justification per tag. They are binary and debatable on purpose: change which mechanisms count and watch the matrix rewire. Two fields are connected when they share at least one active mechanism; the cell's darkness is how many they share.

Beat 5

Why is the new work on the bridges?

Because inside a field the easy experiments are done, while between two fields a well-known tool meets a question nobody has pointed it at. The five bridges below each move one tool across one gap, and each one fits on a laptop.

Three things to remember

  1. Five techniques, 23 kitchens.Prediction, search, emergence, compression and energy cover the whole map. Learn the mechanism once and every field becomes readable.
  2. Artificial life is the cheap corner.Seven of nine ALife fields run meaningfully on a laptop, and foundation models just gave them new judges and new uses as pretraining data.
  3. New work lives on bridges.Move one tool across one gap: SAEs onto a JEPA, a JEPA into ASAL, a latent loss into an NCA. A bridge is as cheap as its most expensive end.
