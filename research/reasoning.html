Why does search plus a verifier keep winning?

a model that proposes is cheap and often wrong, a checker that disposes is slow but reliable, and together they turn raw compute into answers you can trust. Every accepted step is correct, so errors stop compounding, and whatever survives the check can be fed back to train a better proposer. Pretraining built the proposer. Reinforcement learning, test-time sampling and evolutionary search are three ways of wiring it to a checker.

Beat 1

Why does next-token prediction get so far?

Because the cheapest way to predict text written by people who reason is to compress some of that reasoning. And the loss falls as a smooth power law in parameters, data and compute, so every extra order of magnitude buys a predictable improvement.

A language model is trained to put high probability on the next token. To do that on the internet it has to compress grammar, facts, code, arithmetic and the habits of people explaining things. Prediction is compression: a model that assigns probability q to the real next token can encode it in −log₂ q bits, so lowering the loss is literally shrinking the file. (If you built the tokenizer and attention by hand in Build ML From Scratch, this is the same objective, run on a trillion tokens; see the attention explorable for the core block.)

Kaplan et al. (OpenAI, 2020) found that test loss follows power laws over seven orders of magnitude: L ∝ N−0.076 in parameters, D−0.095 in tokens, with architecture details mattering far less than size. They concluded that a bigger compute budget should go mostly into a bigger model. Hoffmann et al. (DeepMind, 2022) refitted with learning-rate schedules matched to each run length and got a different answer: grow parameters and tokens equally. Their 70B-parameter Chinchilla, trained on 1.4T tokens, beat the 280B Gopher trained on 300B tokens at about the same compute. The rule of thumb that came out of it: about 20 training tokens per parameter.

L(N, D) = E + A / Nα + B / Dβ    E = 1.69, A = 406.4, B = 410.7, α = 0.34, β = 0.28    C ≈ 6 N D Hoffmann et al.'s parametric fit (their "Approach 3"). E is the irreducible entropy of text; the two other terms are what a finite model and finite data cost you. Training compute C is about 6 FLOPs per parameter per token.
Beat 2

Why does prediction alone stall on reasoning?

Because imitation has no ground truth. The loss rewards text that looks like a good derivation whether or not it is right, and in a long chain one wrong token poisons everything after it, with nothing in the loop to notice.

Next-token loss scores how typical a continuation is. A confident wrong proof and a correct one can be equally typical. Nothing in pretraining checks the answer, and nothing teaches the model to back up when a step fails.

Errors also compound. If each step of a derivation is right with probability 1 − ε and steps fail independently, an n-step chain is right with probability (1 − ε)n. LeCun's long-standing objection to autoregressive models is this exponential: the longer the answer, the likelier it has left the set of correct answers.

Three critics put it three ways. Subbarao Kambhampati calls LLMs "approximate retrieval engines" that cannot verify their own plans, and proposes LLM-Modulo: let the model generate, let sound external checkers decide. Richard Sutton says imitation of human text has no goal and no ground truth, so it is a dead end for intelligence. François Chollet says scale buys memorised skill, not the ability to acquire new skill (Beat 6).

P(chain correct) = (1 − ε)n
ε = 1%, n = 10  → 0.90
ε = 1%, n = 100 → 0.37
ε = 1%, n = 500 → 0.007 The independence assumption is crude, but the shape is the point: per-step accuracy has to rise as answers get longer, or something has to catch errors.

Picture it

Pretraining makes a brilliant improviser: fluent, fast, never stops to check. A verifier is the editor who reads every line and sends back what doesn't hold. The improviser alone gets worse as the piece gets longer. The pair gets better the more drafts you can afford.

ProposeA learned modelPretrained LLM, policy network or code generator. Cheap, broad, unreliable.
SearchMany proposalsSampling, tree search, evolution, longer chains of thought.
VerifyAn automatic checkUnit tests, a math answer key, a Lean kernel, a game result, a simulator.
LearnTrain on survivorsRL updates, fine-tuning on accepted traces, a new parent population.

The recipe of Q6 in the project's landscape notes. Sutton's Bitter Lesson (2019) names the two general methods that keep scaling with compute: search and learning. The verifier is what lets them run unattended.

Beat 3

Why does RL on checkable answers produce reasoning?

Because a reward from an answer checker pays only for being right. Every behaviour that raises the hit rate (writing out steps, checking, backtracking, trying again) gets reinforced, and none of it has to be shown by a human first.

OpenAI's o1 (September 2024) was the first widely used model trained this way, and showed accuracy rising with both RL compute and the length of its hidden chain of thought. DeepSeek made the recipe public. DeepSeek-R1-Zero (January 2025) started from the V3 base model and used only reinforcement learning with rule-based rewards: is the final answer right, is the format respected. Responses grew longer over training and started to include re-checking and "wait, let me reconsider" moves no one had written for it. The full R1 paper went through peer review and appeared in Nature in September 2025, disclosing roughly US$294k of RL compute on top of the base model check.

The algorithm is GRPO. For each question, sample a group of G answers, score each with the checker, and use the group itself as the baseline. Answers better than their siblings get pushed up, worse ones pushed down. No value network is trained, which halves the memory bill compared with PPO.

Ai = (ri − mean(r1..G)) / std(r1..G)     θ ← θ + η · Σi Ai ∇ log πθ(answeri) The group-relative advantage. If every answer in a group gets the same reward (all right or all wrong), every Ai is zero and that question teaches nothing. Real GRPO adds PPO-style clipping and a KL penalty to a reference model; the toy below keeps only the core.

Beat 4

Why does a verifier beat majority voting?

Because voting can only amplify whatever answer is most common, and when the model has a favourite mistake, more samples make the mistake win harder. A verifier checks each answer on its merits, so more samples only add chances to be right.

Test-time compute is the second scaling axis: spend more inference per question instead of more training. There are two ways to spend it. Think longer: one long chain of thought with self-checks, which is what RL-trained models learn to do. Sample wider: draw N answers and pick one. Picking needs a rule. Self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022) takes the majority answer. Best-of-N with a verifier scores each answer: a trained outcome verifier (Cobbe et al., 2021, on GSM8K), a process reward model that grades each step (Lightman et al., 2023, "Let's Verify Step by Step"), or an exact checker such as unit tests. Snell et al. (2024) showed that spending test-time compute well can beat a model about 14 times larger at matched FLOPs, on problems of moderate difficulty.

The lab below computes, by simulation, how accuracy grows with N under each rule. Each sample is right with probability p. Wrong answers are not uniform: a share of them land on one popular wrong answer, the way a model has a favourite misconception.

CarefulKambhampati (MLST, "Do o1 models search?", 2025-01) warns against reading the intermediate tokens of a reasoning model as a faithful trace of its reasoning: their content often does not track whether the final answer is correct. Thinking longer helps, but the tokens are not proof that it reasoned the way the text says.
Beat 5

Why does search plus a verifier make discoveries?

Because when a score is cheap and trustworthy, you can try millions of candidates no human would think of and keep only what scores. The model supplies good guesses, the verifier supplies the truth, and evolution keeps the best ideas alive long enough to be improved.

    AlphaGo, AlphaZero. A policy network proposes moves, Monte Carlo tree search explores, the game result verifies, and self-play trains the next network. AlphaZero dropped human games entirely.
  • 2019 · 2020

  • 2023-06

  • 2023-12

  • 2024-07
    AlphaProof + AlphaGeometry 2. RL in Lean, with the proof checker as the reward and test-time RL on generated variants: IMO silver, 28/42.
  • 2025-05

  • 2025-07

  • 2025-11

  • 2026-02
    Aletheia. A Deep Think research agent (generate, verify, revise, admit failure) run on 700 open Erdős problems; 4 solved autonomously. The authors grade it no higher than level 2 of 4 on their own autonomy scale.
  • 2026-05

FunSearch's loop is simple enough to run in a browser. Below, the "LLM" is replaced by random edits to small expression trees, which is weaker but honest: the point is the loop. The task is the one FunSearch used: online bin packing. Items arrive one at a time; a heuristic gives each open bin a score and the item goes into the highest-scoring bin that fits. The verifier packs a set of instances and counts bins against the lower bound ⌈Σ sizes / capacity⌉. Programs that crash or return NaN or infinity (say, a division by zero) are rejected.

Open clones put the same loop on a laptop. OpenEvolve and ShinkaEvolve (state-of-the-art circle packing in about 150 evaluations) run with small models; ThetaEvolve adds test-time RL to an 8B open model. Self-modifying agents apply it to their own code: the Darwin Gödel Machine raised SWE-bench from 20% to 50%, and the Huxley-Gödel Machine (Schmidhuber's lab) selects agents by how good their descendants are, reaching 61.4% on SWE-bench Verified. Zhengyao Jiang (MLST, 2026-09) points out that this "recursive self-improvement" still runs inside search spaces humans designed. The open-endedness side of the story is chapter 14.

For this projectA JEPA objective could be discovered this way: evolve the anti-collapse term with an LLM, scored by linear-probe accuracy plus quantization robustness. The verifier is an experiment the project already runs (see chapter 03 and chapter 17).
Where it stopsNo verifier, no loop. Taste, research synthesis and open questions have no cheap checker. Kosmos reached 79% statement accuracy but 58% on synthesis, and the AI Scientist paper in Nature still hallucinated citations. DeepSeekMath-V2 tries to move the line by learning a proof verifier.
Beat 6

Why is skill not the same as intelligence?

Because skill can be bought with data: memorise enough solved tasks and you look clever on anything similar. Intelligence, in Chollet's definition, is how efficiently you turn a few examples and some priors into a skill you did not have. ARC was built so that only the second kind scores.

Chollet's On the Measure of Intelligence (2019) defines intelligence as skill-acquisition efficiency and introduces ARC: grid puzzles that need only "core knowledge" priors (objects, counting, symmetry, contact), each shown with two to five input-output examples. A child-level rule, never seen before, inferred from a handful of examples. Big frozen models did badly on it for years.

What lifted the scores was not bigger frozen models. It was adapting per task at test time, which is search plus a verifier in disguise: the task's own examples are the verifier. Test-time fine-tuning (MindsAI, the ARChitects), program synthesis guided by a model, and in 2025 the "refinement loop": propose an answer or program, check it against the examples, refine, repeat. The ARC team's ablation of HRM found the outer refinement loop gave about +13 points, while the brain-inspired hierarchy added little over a plain transformer of the same size.

Skill

What you can do now

Measured by accuracy on a fixed task set. Grows with training data and model size. Can be "knowledge-overfit": the ARC Prize 2025 report warns frontier models are starting to cover ARC-AGI-1 and -2 by sheer exposure.

Intelligence (Chollet)

How fast you get new skill

Measured per unit of experience and prior. ARC-AGI-3 scores action efficiency: how few moves an agent needs to work out an unfamiliar game. That is a world-model-learning test (chapter 11).

The other voices

Not everyone agrees

Melanie Mitchell: accuracy overstates abstraction. Laura Ruis: LLMs do use generalisable procedures. Christopher Summerfield: that text alone teaches rich world knowledge is a real scientific surprise. Marcus and Saba: no compositional semantics without symbols.

Where the evidence stands (October 2026)Abstraction on ARC comes from per-task adaptation (refinement, test-time training, program search), not from bigger frozen models, and small models with deep iteration are competitive. Chollet's lab Ndea bets on deep-learning-guided program synthesis. Final ARC Prize 2026 results are due 2026-12-04. The fault line, in the project's map: Why Atlas and chapter 15.

Three things to remember

  1. A checker turns compute into truth.Propose cheaply, verify strictly, train on what survives. Every accepted step is correct, so errors stop compounding.
  2. Votes amplify the popular mistake.Majority voting converges to the most common answer, right or wrong. A verifier converges to the right one, as long as it lets the truth through more often than any single mistake.
  3. Skill is the output, intelligence is the rate.Memorised skill scales with data. ARC rewards adapting to a new task from a few examples, and so far per-task refinement loops win it.
