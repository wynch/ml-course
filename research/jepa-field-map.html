JEPA Field Map

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures, from LeCun's 2022 position paper to the 2026 wave of theory. Theory comes first on this page: what is proven about collapse, about what JEPAs learn, and about when they recover the true state of a world. The paper map and an experiment agenda follow.

The objective in one line

Every paper below is a variation on this energy. The interesting part is always the second term, or whatever replaces it.

E(x, y, z) = ‖ gφ( fθ(x), z ) − sg[ fξ(y) ] ‖²   +   λ · R( fθ(X) )
x, y context and target (visible patches and masked patches, frame t and frame t+k, text and code) fθ, fξ context and target encoders; ξ is often an EMA of θ gφ predictor; z is the mask position, the action, or a latent for uncertainty sg stop-gradient; R anti-collapse term (none in I-JEPA, VICReg in PLDM, SIGReg in LeJEPA)
How the field moved

Lineage

Each year shifted the open problem.

2021–22

Non-contrastive SSL (BYOL, SimSiam, VICReg) shows collapse can be avoided without negatives; Tian et al. explain why in the linear case. LeCun's position paper names the JEPA and frames it as an energy-based model with a latent.

2023

I-JEPA makes masked latent prediction work on images without hand-crafted augmentations. MC-JEPA, A-JEPA and Graph-JEPA carry the idea to motion, audio and graphs.

2024

V-JEPA scales to video. IWM shows the predictor decides invariance versus equivariance. Littwin et al. give the first clean implicit-bias result: JEPA prefers predictive features, MAE prefers high-variance ones. DINO-WM plans in a frozen latent space.

2025

V-JEPA 2 plans robot actions zero-shot; intuitive-physics surprise emerges from V-JEPA. LLM-JEPA brings the objective to language models. LeJEPA replaces the heuristics with one provable regularizer, SIGReg.

2026

A theory wave: identifiability (LeJEPA learns the world up to a linear map, and only for Gaussian latents), training-dynamics phase boundaries, generalization bounds for JEPA world models, variational and active-inference readings. On the applied side, end-to-end pixel world models (LeWorldModel) and debates about the right latent geometry for planning.

Theory first

Key theoretical insights

Nine results that, taken together, explain most of what JEPAs do. Each cites the papers it rests on; click a citation to jump to it in the map.

I-1

Predicting in latent space buys abstraction and costs you collapse

Because the target is an embedding the model also learns, the encoder can drop unpredictable detail (exact textures, leaf motion) instead of spending capacity on it. The same freedom admits the trivial solution where every input maps to a constant. Every JEPA is therefore defined as much by how it prevents collapse as by what it predicts.

Why it matters: the anti-collapse mechanism is the main design choice, and most theory papers are about it.

[lecun2022] [dawid2023] [recon]

I-2

Collapse escape is a per-mode instability, and the heuristics all move one ratio

Linearized at the collapsed point, each representation mode has a driving force from cross-view prediction and a decay force from EMA and weight shrinkage. A mode escapes collapse when their ratio μ exceeds 1, and μ factors into a data term (set by masking) and a predictor term. The number of unstable modes bounds the effective rank. Masking ratio, predictor scale and EMA rate are three knobs on the same quantity. This extends Tian et al.'s 2021 linear account of BYOL and SimSiam to JEPAs.

Why it matters: it turns hyperparameter folklore into a phase diagram you can predict and test.

[drivedecay] [tian2021] [cjepa_mo]

I-3

JEPA learns predictive features first; reconstruction learns high-variance features first

In deep linear networks, MAE-style objectives pick up features in order of their covariance, while JEPA picks them up in order of their regression coefficient, meaning how well the context predicts them. Encoder depth strengthens this bias. Pixel reconstruction, by contrast, spends capacity on high-variance subspaces that matter little for perception.

Why it matters: it is the cleanest formal reason JEPA features are more semantic and why they degrade more gracefully under pixel corruption.

[littwin2024] [recon] [latvid]

I-4

The predictor's parameterization chooses which optimum you get

Many encoders achieve the same prediction loss. In time series, a near-identity linear predictor selects the invariant subspace of the Koopman operator (regime indicators), while unconstrained predictors give entangled equivalents. In images, conditioning and capacity of the predictor decide invariance versus equivariance. Identity-biased predictors also help escape collapse. The flip side is the slow-feature trap: JEPAs latch onto whatever varies slowly, including slow noise.

Why it matters: the predictor is an inductive-bias dial, not just a head you throw away.

[koopman] [iwm] [drivedecay] [sobal2022]

I-5

JEPA is a spectral method on a co-occurrence matrix

Joint-embedding objectives perform spectral embedding of a view-similarity graph. For world models, JEPA pretraining is equivalent to low-rank factorization of an action-conditioned co-occurrence matrix, which yields a finite-sample generalization bound linking pretraining error to planning regret. Latent dimension trades approximation error against sample error.

Why it matters: it gives a principled way to choose latent dimension and to compare latent-level with input-level prediction.

[spectral] [duality] [gentheory]

I-6

Three independent routes point to the isotropic Gaussian

LeJEPA proves an isotropic Gaussian embedding minimizes worst-case downstream risk and enforces it with SIGReg (random 1-D projections plus a characteristic-function test). Klindt, LeCun and Balestriero then show the Gaussian is the only latent law for which LeJEPA provably recovers the world's latents up to a linear map. An active-inference reading shows SIGReg closes the entropy gap that VICReg leaves open, making the objective a valid variational free energy.

Why it matters: it is the closest the field has to a canonical answer for R, and it predicts measurable signatures such as an embedding condition number near 1.

[lejepa] [lejepawm] [sigregvfe]

I-7

Good prediction does not imply the right latents

Identifiability needs more than low loss. With actions, causal states are recoverable (up to component-wise transforms and permutation) only if actions vary the transition mechanisms enough. Without action conditioning, LeJEPA's guarantee needs stationary additive-noise dynamics.

Why it matters: it tells you what data a world model must see, and gives metrics beyond probing accuracy.

[iact] [lejepawm]

I-8

JEPA is a deterministic special case of a variational model

The encoder and predictor match the variational posterior and learned conditional prior of a coupled latent-variable model. Training the full ELBO removes the need for an ad hoc anti-collapse term and gives calibrated latent uncertainty, which the original position paper's latent z was meant to supply but most implementations drop.

Why it matters: it blurs the "non-generative" line and offers a route to multimodal futures.

[varjepa] [lecun2022] [inforeview] [vicreginfo]

I-9

For planning, geometry matters more than decodability

Planners score candidates by Euclidean distance in latent space, so a direction's weight in the cost is proportional to its variance. Task state can be perfectly decodable yet barely affect planning if it lives in low-variance directions, and small moving objects get drowned by static background. This is in tension with I-6: isotropy treats all directions equally, which is optimal for unknown downstream tasks but not for a known control task.

Why it matters: it is the sharpest current conflict between two theory threads, and an easy one to probe.

[scale] [plannable] [lewm] [dinowm]

Unsolved

Open questions

Where the theory is thin or the threads disagree.

Isotropy versus task geometry

SIGReg's isotropy is optimal for unknown tasks; planning wants task-relevant directions to carry more variance. Is there a principled middle, such as isotropy within a task-weighted metric?

Beyond linear and local

Collapse dynamics and implicit-bias results are for linear networks near initialization. Do the phase boundary and feature ordering survive in ViTs with real masking and long training?

Masks as the latent variable

The mask distribution decides which invariances get learned, yet there is no theory of masking comparable to the theory of augmentations in contrastive learning.

Uncertainty and multimodal futures

A deterministic predictor regresses to the conditional mean in latent space. When does that mean stay meaningful, and is Var-JEPA's ELBO enough to represent branching futures?

Is EMA ever necessary?

LeJEPA drops EMA and stop-gradient, SALT uses a frozen teacher, and Drive vs. Decay treats EMA as one knob on μ. Which regimes, if any, require a co-adapting target?

Hierarchy

H-JEPA, the multi-timescale core of the position paper, has almost no theory and few working systems. Variable-stride prediction is a first step.

Non-Gaussian worlds

If the Gaussian is the only identifiable latent law for LeJEPA, what should a model do in worlds with heavy-tailed or discrete factors? Should the regularizer's target distribution be learned?

Language

When does a latent-prediction term help a next-token model, and what does it change in the representation? LLM-JEPA shows gains, but there is no theory yet.

For later

Theory-driven experiment ideas

Each one tests a specific claim above at small scale. Costs assume one GPU or less.

Map the collapse phase boundary

CPU to 1 GPU-day
Tests
I-2: escape happens when μ > 1, and the number of unstable modes predicts effective rank.
Setup
Linear and small-ViT JEPAs on synthetic Gaussian data and CIFAR-10. Sweep masking ratio, EMA rate and predictor init scale; compute predicted μ per mode and compare with measured effective rank. Add a SIGReg arm to see whether the boundary disappears.

Variance versus predictiveness race

CPU to hours
Tests
I-3 beyond the deep linear setting.
Setup
Synthetic inputs whose features vary variance and cross-view predictability independently. Train JEPA, MAE and LeJEPA with nonlinear encoders of several depths; record the order in which each feature becomes linearly decodable.

Break the Gaussian assumption

Hours
Tests
I-6 and I-7: linear identifiability holds only for Gaussian latents.
Setup
Latent dynamics with Gaussian, Laplace, uniform and mixture innovations, rendered through a random nonlinear mixer. Measure linear R² and MCC between learned and true latents. Then swap SIGReg's target for the true latent law to see whether identifiability returns.

Isotropy against plannability

1–3 GPU-days
Tests
The I-6 versus I-9 tension.
Setup
LeWorldModel-style training on Two-Room and Push-T. Interpolate between pure SIGReg and a SCALE-style distance-alignment term. Track condition number, Kendall τ of candidate rankings, and planning success.

The predictor as a selector

CPU to hours
Tests
I-4: predictor parameterization picks the optimum; slow noise traps JEPAs.
Setup
Switching dynamical systems with a slowly drifting noise channel. Compare near-identity linear, low-rank and deep MLP predictors on disentanglement of regime indicators and on how much slow noise they encode.

How much action diversity is enough?

Hours
Tests
I-7: identifiability requires actions to vary the transition mechanisms.
Setup
Synthetic action-conditioned environments where the number of distinct mechanisms is a dial. Plot latent recovery against that dial to see whether there is a sharp threshold as the theory suggests.

Latent dimension against data size

Hours
Tests
I-5: the generalization bound's approximation versus sample trade-off.
Setup
Grid of latent dimension by dataset size in a small gridworld JEPA world model. Check whether the best latent dimension grows with data at the rate the bound implies.

Branching futures

CPU
Tests
I-8 and the uncertainty open question.
Setup
Toy worlds where the future is bimodal (a ball goes left or right). Compare a deterministic JEPA, a mixture-output predictor and a Var-JEPA-style ELBO on whether the latent prediction collapses to an implausible mean.
