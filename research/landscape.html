Who is building what, and why do they disagree?

every serious programme agrees that prediction, search and compute matter, and each one bets on a different place to put the structure: in the objective, in the path of learning, in the search, in the architecture or in the data. Those bets make different predictions, so the disagreements are not noise. Most of them could be settled by experiments that have not been run yet.

Beat 1

Why did one story split into seven?

Because "scale a transformer on human data, then fine-tune it" ran into three walls at once: human data is finite, outputs that look right can hide bad insides, and frozen models cannot learn after training. Each wall spawned its own line of attack.

In 2023 the field had one dominant recipe. By October 2026 it had split into several stories that disagree with each other in productive ways. Here they are in seven moves, each tagged with the thread from the Why Atlas that it belongs to.

Thread 4 · search + verifier

Reasoning came from RL on checkable answers

DeepSeek-R1 (peer-reviewed in Nature, 2025-09), IMO gold for OpenAI and Gemini Deep Think (2025-07), Aletheia on 4 open Erdős problems (2026-02). Whether RL creates reasoning or only surfaces it is disputed.

Thread 4 · search + verifier

Search plus a verifier became a discovery engine

FunSearch (2023), AlphaEvolve (2025-05) and its 2026 impact report, open clones (OpenEvolve, ShinkaEvolve, ThetaEvolve), self-rewriting agents (Darwin Gödel Machine, Huxley-Gödel Machine, Hyperagents).

Thread 3 · right space to predict

World models became an industry

Genie 3, Dreamer 4, V-JEPA 2, LeWorldModel, AMI Labs' $1.03B seed (2026-03), World Labs Marble, NVIDIA Cosmos, Wayve GAIA-3. Three camps: generate pixels, build explicit 3D, or predict in a latent space.

Thread 7 · small, local, cheap

Small recursive models embarrassed big ones

TRM (7M parameters) and CompressARC (76K) on ARC, "refinement loops" as ARC Prize 2025's theme, ARC-AGI-3 (2026-03) where frontier models scored under 1% at launch.

Thread 2 · outside vs inside

Representations converge, less than claimed

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (2024) was narrowed by 2026 critiques to relational, local convergence. Kumar's FER hypothesis (2025): identical outputs can hide fractured insides.

Thread 2 · outside vs inside

Interpretability moved to mechanisms

DeepMind deprioritised sparse autoencoders (2025-03). Anthropic found attribution graphs, feature manifolds, introspection, emotion vectors (2025-26).

Thread 6 · learning never stops

Continual learning is the declared next frontier

Titans, Nested Learning/HOPE, TTT-E2E, sparse memory finetuning, DeepMind's EVE Online partnership (2026-08), ARC-AGI-3's in-environment learning.

The deepest current runs under all seven: what a system learns depends on how it learned it (the path, the objective, the verifier, the curriculum), and matching outputs says little about the quality of the inside. Beat 2

Why do the same names keep showing up under different questions?

Because the 14 why-questions are not independent. A person who studies how networks generalize (Q1) ends up studying what is inside them (Q2) and what to predict (Q3). The people who connect the most questions are the ones who move the field.

Beat 3

Why does it matter who learned from whom?

Because a research lineage carries a default answer to "where should the structure go?". Students inherit the question their advisor thought was central, so you can often predict a paper's bet from its family tree.

Akarsh Kumar is the clean example. His undergraduate mentor at UT Austin was Risto Miikkulainen, who was also Kenneth Stanley's PhD advisor; his PhD advisor is Phillip Isola. So he sits where the NEAT lineage (structure comes from the path of search) meets the representation lineage (structure shows up in what the embedding agrees on). FER is what you get when you ask the second lineage's question with the first lineage's tools.

Beat 4

Why can't they just agree?

Because most debates are about things nobody can measure yet: the quality of an inside, whether RL adds abilities or reveals them, what counts as reasoning. Each fault line has a specific missing experiment, and naming it turns an argument into a research plan.

Ten live debates, each with the people on both sides as the sources list them. Pick a side on any card you have an opinion on; the panel at the end computes whose positions you match most often. Then open "what would settle it" to see why the debate is still open.

Beat 5

What is DeepMind betting on, and why no JEPA?

Because DeepMind's winning recipe since AlphaGo is a learned prior plus search plus a verifier, and pixels are the one representation a verifier (or a human) can check. Its world-model stack is latent in practice, but its public representation bet is pixel MAE.

1

Search plus learning, generalised

AlphaGo's recipe moved onto LLMs: FunSearch (2023-12) → AlphaEvolve (2025-05: a 48-multiplication 4×4 complex matmul, 0.7% of Google's fleet compute recovered) → cross-industry optimiser (2026-05 impact report: TPU design, quantum circuits with 10× lower error, Ramsey bounds). Maths: AlphaProof (formal, IMO silver 2024) → Deep Think IMO gold (informal, 35/42, 2025-07) → Aletheia on open Erdős problems (2026). DiscoRL (Nature 2025) applies it to RL update rules.

2

From human data to experience

Silver and Sutton's "Era of Experience" (2025) is the manifesto. SIMA 2 (2025-11) learns in Genie 3 worlds from tasks Gemini proposes; Dreamer 4 got Minecraft diamonds offline; the EVE Online partnership (2026-08) targets continual learning in a persistent world.

3

World models as the training substrate

Genie 1 (2024-02: 2D, about 1 fps, latent actions) → Genie 2 (2024-12: 10 to 20 s of 3D) → Genie 3 (2025-08: real-time 720p, 24 fps, about a minute of memory) → Project Genie product (2026-01) and Street View grounding (2026-05). No Genie 4 as of 2026-10-10.

4

AI improving AI

AlphaEvolve tunes Gemini kernels and TPU design; Gemini 4 Argon teams migrated an 800K-line kernel to Rust; Decoupled DiLoCo trains across distant data centres. Safety moves in parallel to "AI control" (2026-06 roadmap).

5

Science as flagship

AlphaFold 3 → AlphaGenome → AlphaGenome Atlas (about 9 billion variants, 2026-09); GraphCast → WeatherNext 3 (2026-09), which learns from raw observations with no physics model in the loop.

The JEPA angleDeepMind has no JEPA-named work. Its video representation bet, 4DS, is a pixel-reconstruction MAE that keeps improving up to 22B parameters. Yet Genie's latent actions, Dreamer's latent dynamics, MooG and D4RT are all latent in practice. An independent 2026 study (2605.15618) found V-JEPA 2 degrades more gracefully than VideoMAEv2 and VideoPrism under corruption and occlusion. The latent-vs-pixel fault line runs through DeepMind's own stack.
Beat 6

Why use a podcast as a lens on research?

Because Machine Learning Street Talk interviews the people on both sides of almost every fault line, at length, often years before their ideas are mainstream. Long-form conversation exposes the bet behind a paper, which an abstract hides.

MLST hosted LeCun and Balestriero together in January 2022, Chollet four times between 2024 and 2025, Friston five times, and Kumar with Stanley on FER three months after the paper. Not found as guests in the project's search: Hinton, Chris Olah, David Ha, Albert Gu, Richard Sutton. Three things to remember

  1. Every camp is a bet on where structure goes.Objective (JEPA), path (open-endedness), search (verifiers), architecture (symmetry, recurrence) or data (synthetic, experience). Name the bet and you can predict the paper.
  2. Same outputs, different insides.The deepest current of 2025-26: how a system learned shapes what it learned, and output metrics cannot see the difference.
  3. A debate is a missing experiment.Each fault line names its settling test. Most need a measuring tool nobody has built, which makes tools the best place to contribute.
