Q1 · MECHANICS

What happens, mechanically, when a machine learns?

A model is a function with millions of adjustable numbers, called parameters. A loss scores how wrong the function is on the training data. Training computes, for every parameter at once, which direction would reduce the loss — the gradient — and nudges each parameter a small step downhill. Then it repeats, millions of times. That is the whole of training; everything else is refinement.

The honest picture is a ball rolling on a landscape whose shape the data sets. The step size (learning rate) decides everything about the ride: too small and you crawl, too large and the ball oscillates or flies off the chart. The course measures that cliff exactly — the same code diverges just above a threshold set by the landscape's curvature.

CONCRETEThe smallest model has one parameter: price = w × surface, for apartments. Start with w = 10 000 €/m². The loss measures how far your prices land from real sales; the gradient says "raise w" or "lower w"; the learning rate says by how much. A billion-parameter model does nothing deeper — it turns more knobs at once.

Q2 · GENERALIZATION

Why does it learn the pattern instead of memorizing the data?

Large networks have enough parameters to memorize their training data outright — and classical theory says they should, and then fail on anything new. They mostly don't. The modern answer: among the many parameter settings that fit the data perfectly, training reliably finds the simplest one — smallest weights, smoothest function. Networks also learn broad strokes (low frequencies) before fine detail, so stopping training early keeps the pattern and leaves the noise behind.

The deep version of this answer: learning is compression. A model generalizes exactly when it has found a description of the data shorter than the data itself. Your course reaches the same point from experiment:

"It is not the error that is fundamental, it is the norm."— ml-course, module 00c, on double descent

Seen from the other end, training is an inverse problem: the outputs are given, the parameters are unknown, and many settings fit. A problem with many solutions is called ill-posed, and the classical cure is a preference for simple solutions. Implicit bias is that cure, unprogrammed.

CONCRETEPredict the next number of 2, 4, 6, 8, … Memorizing the list says nothing about the next item; the rule "add 2" is shorter than the list and predicts forever. That is the whole claim: a model generalizes when it has found the rule, not the list — "shortest perfect fit" and "learned the pattern" are the same statement.

Q3 · REPRESENTATION

What is a representation?

The middle of the network: the data re-expressed as points in a vector space where geometry is meaning — similar things sit near each other, and concepts point along directions. In your course, token families (numbers, months, countries) already separate on a 2-D projection before a single layer runs.

Two hard-won lessons from your own gridjepa experiments. First, a representation can satisfy its training loss perfectly while being useless: with no safeguard, the model drove its loss to ~0 by mapping every input to the same vector — collapse. So the loss cannot certify the representation; you need independent instruments (spread of the vectors, effective rank, small probes). Second, a representation keeps only what its objective demands: restoring the full input through a 2,250-number reconstruction head changed planning ability not at all, while a 2-number supervised readout quadrupled it. Organization beats information.

And the geometry has a measured boundary: one point per meaning runs out. A meaning composed of several constraints — "every category satisfied, each by any of its alternatives" — provably cannot be ranked by a single vector of sane size; it needs a set of points, one per aspect, each finding its best match on the other side. This is the perceptron's XOR wall (module 00a's origin story) reappearing at retrieval scale in 2026: a single linear score cannot express interaction structure, however it is learned.

Two claims about the shape of the space. The manifold hypothesis: real data fills only a thin surface inside its raw coordinates, and a representation is good when it unrolls that surface. And a network with ReLU units cuts its input into flat pieces, one linear map per piece. How many pieces, and where, is measurable.

CONCRETERGB is a representation of color: "tomato red" becomes three numbers (220, 60, 40), and nearby triples are similar colors. Word embeddings do the same for meaning — which is why "king − man + woman" lands near "queen". And collapse is the student who summarizes every book as "things happen": the task is technically satisfied, and the summary space has shrunk to one point.

Q4 · TRANSFORMERS

Why do transformers work so well?

Three answers, at three depths, all true at once.

1. They fit the hardware. Attention is parallel matrix arithmetic; GPUs eat it. The older recurrent networks processed words one at a time and lost on speed, not on smarts.

2. Their core operation is retrieval. Attention is a soft dictionary lookup: every word asks a question, scores every other word's key, and blends their values by match quality. That formula is, term for term, the recall step of an associative memory (a Hopfield network) with enormous capacity. A language model is mostly a memory machine — your course's logit-lens figure shows a model spending 27 of its 32 layers retrieving, and committing to its answer late.

3. At scale, improvement is a law. Loss falls along smooth power laws in model size, data, and compute — progress you can purchase and predict. Architecture details matter surprisingly little once size dominates.

4. Depth composes. Each layer builds its features out of the previous layer's — edges become textures become parts become objects; letters become words become clauses become meaning. Composition is what lets a fixed budget of parts cover an unbounded world, and it is the deep link to Q2: an abstraction pays rent by compressing, and composition is what makes compression reusable. Two honest caveats. Measured from inside, the layers look less like a clean ladder and more like many small corrections to a shared draft — partly an ensemble, not a strict pipeline. And depth is fixed, so a transformer's true recursion happens outside the weights: each generated word is fed back in, and a chain of thought is the model composing with itself through the page. The context window is its call stack.

If the corrections shrink with depth, the draft is settling on a fixed point, a state the next layer leaves unchanged. Some architectures make that literal and run one layer until it stops changing. Experiment 4 below asked whether the course's own model does this: it does not. Its late layers write more, not less, and the last one writes most.

One bridge worth keeping: the softmax that turns scores into weights is, formula for formula, the Boltzmann distribution from physics — and the "temperature" knob in sampling is the same T. Not an analogy; the same equation.

CONCRETEAttention is asking a question to a full room. Every word in the sentence wears a badge (its key) and holds a card (its value). Your word whispers a question (its query); whoever's badge matches answers, louder for a better match, and you blend what you hear. And temperature: at low T you always order your favorite dish; at high T you throw a dart at the menu.

Q5 · ALTERNATIVES

What are the alternatives, and what do they trade?

Not a zoo of rivals — a design space where each alternative moves one resource.

Recurrence and state-space models (Mamba and family): constant memory per step instead of remembering everything, at the price of no direct look-back. It turned out attention and these models are two ends of one mathematical family — a dial, not a fork.

Latent prediction (JEPA, LeCun's program): predict a summary of the future instead of every pixel or token, so capacity goes to what is predictable. Your gridjepa results sharpen this: the summary keeps only what its objective demands, so what you supervise matters far more than how much.

Sparsity (mixture-of-experts and cousins): stop spending every parameter on every input — route by width (which expert), depth (which layers), or memory (which stored facts).

Fixed substrates (wavelets, Mallat's program): install structure you already know by hand — vision's invariances, for instance — and learn only the rest. The design question becomes: where is learning placed?

Diffusion (image generators, and lately text): generate by undoing noise, one small step at a time. The hierarchy here is not in the network — the noise enforces it. Heavy noise erases fine detail first, so generation must settle global shape before texture, coarse to fine, with the same network reused at every level. Experiment 1 below measured the same order in learning. So this section's closing question is really twofold: where is learning placed — and where is composition placed: in the weights, in the process, or in the architecture?

CONCRETEReading a novel with a fixed-size notepad instead of a photographic memory of every page — that is the state-space trade. Predicting "the glass will fall and shatter" without predicting where each shard lands — that is latent prediction. Sending the patient to one specialist instead of through every department — that is mixture-of-experts.

Q6 · KNOWING

How do we know what a model knows?

Two instruments. Interpretability opens the box: concepts live as directions in the representation, and small networks have been decoded completely — one trained on modular arithmetic was found to have invented the Fourier transform on its own. Calibration checks from outside: when the model says 80%, is it right 80% of the time?

The trap your own projects keep rediscovering deserves a name: self-certification. Any system scored on a quantity it controls will perfect the score, not the truth. Gridjepa's collapse (loss ~0, representation dead). The pétanque verifier that derived its check from the very hypothesis it was checking. And LLM hallucination is the same loop one level up: fluent confidence, certified by the same model that produced the claim.

The antidotes all have teeth: score only predictions made before the outcome is seen; use instruments the model cannot touch; make refusal an allowed answer.

CONCRETESelf-certification is grading your own exam — or checking a ruler by measuring it with itself. Calibration is the forecaster's audit: gather every day they said "80% chance of rain" and count — did it rain on about 80 of each 100?

Q7 · METHOD

What makes a question proper?

The skill this whole page serves. A proper question names, before looking: what you expect, what would change your mind, and what you will measure. One variable at a time. Cheapest test first. "No answer yet" allowed.

You already run this discipline at full strength in the pétanque ledger — pre-registered entries, everything measured rather than quoted. The move now is simply to point the same discipline at ML notions, through the experiments below.

CONCRETE"Does the app measure well?" is a wish. "Across 30 taped distances, is the average error under 5 mm, with no single error over 10?" is a question: it names the data, the number, and the failure line — before looking. (Those are the pétanque ledger's real acceptance numbers.)

Experiments

Small, laptop-sized, one question each. Rule from Q7: expectation and measurement written here before the code ran. Misses reported too.

Watch the frequencies arrive ~15 min · tests Q2DONE · PARTLY MET

Pre-registered: a small network fits a slow wave, a fast wave and noise; the slow wave should be learned first, the fast one later, noise last. Both at once, or fast first, refutes it.

Result: the order held. The slow wave's error halved at step 7, the fast wave's at step 2,152, the noise band's at step 5,500. Training past the best point cost 1.23× on the true function, so the early-stopping half was weak.

experiments/01-spectral-bias/RESULTS.md

Temperature is physics ~30 min · tests Q4DONE · MET

Pre-registered: sample thousands of next characters from a small language model at seven temperatures; the pick-frequencies should match Boltzmann's formula on the model's own scores. A gap wider than sampling noise refutes it.

Result: met at six of seven temperatures; the seventh missed by 0.0001. At T = 0.2 the spread stays far from zero, because the top two scores nearly tie: low temperature is not deterministic.

experiments/02-temperature/RESULTS.md

Watch a network invent Fourier 4.4 min · tests Q2 + Q6DONE · MET

Pre-registered: a one-layer transformer learns a + b mod 97 from 30% of the pairs and keeps training; training accuracy should be perfect early, test accuracy near chance, then jump, with sharp periodic structure in its number-embeddings.

Result: memorized by step 130, generalized around step 4,500. Four of 48 frequencies hold 95% of the embedding energy and carry the algorithm: rebuild from them and test accuracy stays 100%; delete them and it falls to chance.

experiments/03-grokking/RESULTS.md

Does the stream converge? ~20 min · tests Q4DONE · FAILED

Pre-registered: read the residual stream after every layer of a 360-million-parameter model on 64 prompts; corrections should shrink with depth and agreement with the final state should rise. A random-weight control that shrinks as much refutes it.

Result: all three falsifiers fired. The last third of layers corrects 1.19× the first, agreement rose for 44% of prompts, not 90%, and the random-weight control passed both checks — so the checks measured arithmetic, not learning.

experiments/04-fixed-point/RESULTS.md

The crystallizer V0–V2 DONE · PARTLY MET

A project that came out of this map, now its own repo. One small language model, frozen: it never trains. All learning sits in the program around it, which keeps a library of cards — short written rules, dug out of solved episodes: small tasks whose answer the task itself can check. Three pre-registered builds scored 0.23, then 0.38, then 0.40 on tasks held back from mining, against the bare model's 0.29 throughout; the first build lost to the model it was meant to help. One lever did the work: letting the program refuse to load a card when none fits. A wrong card is worse than no card — it scored zero where the bare model got 28% right. Open problem: which solved examples teach a good card. Numbers: ../ml-lab/crystallizer, files RESULTS.md, RESULTS_V1.md, RESULTS_V2.md.
