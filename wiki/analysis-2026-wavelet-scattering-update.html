2026 Wavelet Scattering Update

analysis created 2026-05-14 updated 2026-05-14 analysis wavelets wavelet-scattering edge-ai representation-learning benchmark-design 2026

This update answers the question: what do the most recent 2026 papers change about the proposed wavelet-substrate experiment?

Main Update

The 2026 literature strengthens the substrate idea, but not in the naive form "fixed scattering plus classifier always wins."

The stronger direction is:

fixed or structured wavelet geometry + budgeted coefficient/path use + probabilistic or residual learner + shift-aware evaluation.

Relevant 2026 Signals

  1. Mallat's 2026 work moved toward moments and generation. source-mallat-2026-moment-generative-models uses scattering moments as constraints or feature maps inside maximum-entropy generation and training-free stochastic interpolants. This matters more for residual prediction and generative world modeling than for the first classifier experiment.
  2. Scattering cost is now an explicit research target. source-scrapl-random-path-scattering makes scattering cheaper by sampling paths. For the local experiment, this suggests measuring sparse path selection before inventing a hand-tuned event extractor.
  3. Uncertainty is the cleanest 2026 benchmark addition. source-bayesian-scattering-uncertainty uses scattering plus a probabilistic head as a distribution-shift baseline. This maps directly to edge intelligence: the device should know when the substrate no longer explains the signal.
  4. Applications are moving toward sensors and edge cases. source-2026-wavelet-edge-applications shows relevant uses in speech deepfake detection, battery thermal prediction, industrial anomaly detection, lightweight dehazing, and dynamical-system forecasting.

Consequence For The Experiment

The first milestone should change slightly:

  • Keep image classification, but add calibration and distribution-shift reporting from the start.
  • Add a tiny probabilistic head baseline, not only linear and multilayer-perceptron heads.
  • Record coefficient count and path count, not only trainable parameter count.
  • Treat sparse coefficient/path selection as a first-class ablation.
  • Plan the second domain as sensor or audio data, not another image dataset.

Better First Research Question

Old:

Can a multiscale, stable, mostly fixed signal substrate reduce the amount of learned neural machinery needed for robust edge intelligence?

Sharper after the 2026 pass:

Can a fixed multiscale substrate plus a small probabilistic or residual learner improve sample efficiency, robustness, and uncertainty under edge-like distribution shift?

Connection To Strange Loops

The strange-loop idea should not be forced into the first milestone. It becomes meaningful after the substrate has:

  • a calibrated uncertainty signal
  • sparse scale/path selection
  • residual surprise

Then a higher-level rule can feed back into lower-level perception by changing which paths, scales, or residual channels are sampled. That gives a measurable loop:

substrate events -> learned rule -> changed substrate sampling -> better prediction or uncertainty
