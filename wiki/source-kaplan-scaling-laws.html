Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models

source created 2026-05-06 updated 2026-05-06 scaling-laws training compute foundational 2020

Kaplan, McCandlish, Henighan, Brown, Chess, Child, Gray, Radford, Wu, Amodei — arXiv 2001.08361, Jan 2020 (OpenAI)

The paper that turned LLM training into an engineering discipline. Across >5 orders of magnitude in compute, fits smooth power-law relationships between cross-entropy loss and (i) model size N, (ii) dataset size D, and (iii) training compute C. Key claim: at fixed compute, bigger models trained on less data are compute-optimal — a recommendation source-chinchilla-compute-optimal would later overturn.

Key results

  • Test loss obeys L(N) ∝ N^(-0.076), L(D) ∝ D^(-0.095), L(C) ∝ C^(-0.050) (rough exponents from the paper's fits over the studied range).
  • Architecture details (depth, width, heads) matter very little compared to total parameter count — within the studied family.
  • Sample efficiency improves with size: bigger models reach a target loss in fewer tokens.
  • Compute-optimal allocation (Kaplan recipe): when scaling C by 10×, scale N by ~5.5× and D by only ~1.8×. Implies very large, undertrained models.
  • Loss curves are smooth and predictable across scales — enables principled extrapolation before paying the training bill.

Method

  • Trained decoder-only transformers across a wide grid: 768 to 1.5B parameters; 22M to 23B tokens; up to ~10²¹ FLOPs.
  • Fit functional forms L(N), L(D), L(C), and combined L(N, D), to identify the irreducible loss floor and the diminishing-returns regimes.
  • Cross-validated on language modeling and downstream behaviour.

Why it matters

The economic and scientific case for scale-as-strategy. Without this paper, source-gpt3-few-shot-learners is an expensive gamble; with it, GPT-3 is a forecasted point on a curve. Every frontier-lab budget meeting since 2020 has used some descendant of these power laws.

The paper also created the debate it would lose two years later: its compute-optimal recommendation favoured bigger models over more data. source-chinchilla-compute-optimal re-ran the experiment with better data scaling and found N and D should grow roughly 1:1 — meaning Kaplan-era models (GPT-3, Gopher, Megatron-Turing NLG) were dramatically under-trained. The corrected scaling laws then reshaped Llama, Falcon, and beyond.

For this wiki, Kaplan is the reference point for concept-scaling-laws and the "just scale" thesis that source-language-models-as-scaffolds, source-residual-matrix-transformer, and source-neural-depth-scaling all push back on — and that source-neural-scaling-laws-from-language-statistics gives a parameter-free derivation for, grounding the empirical exponents in token-correlation statistics of natural language.
