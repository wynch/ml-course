Google DeepMind: SAEs lose to linear probes, and SAE research is deprioritised

source created 2026-10-20 updated 2026-10-20 interpretability sparse-autoencoders probing negative-results google-deepmind 2025

Authors: Lewis Smith and Sen Rajamanoharan (equal contribution), Arthur Conmy, Callum McDougall, Janos Kramar, Tom Lieberum, Rohin Shah, Neel Nanda; Google DeepMind's mechanistic interpretability team Published: 2025-03-26, on the DeepMind Safety Research Medium blog and cross-posted to LessWrong and the Alignment Forum ("Progress Update #2"); a set of research notes, not a paper Raw copy: raw/web-2025-gdm-negative-results-saes-downstream.md (captured 2026-10-20 from the LessWrong cross-post; figures as image links only)

Every claim below cites the raw file as R, with its section.

Summary

A sparse autoencoder (SAE) splits a model's internal activations into many directions, each meant to be one readable concept. A linear probe is a small classifier trained to read one property straight from the activations. The team asked whether SAEs earn their cost on a task people care about: detecting a user's harmful intent in prompts it was not trained on (R, "Our Task").

Setup: Gemma 2 9B IT, layer 20. Harmful prompts from HarmBench against harmless ones from Alpaca, with jailbreak wrappers and harmless rewrites added; some jailbreaks and rewrites are kept out of training to make the out-of-distribution (OOD) set. 4,046 training, 1,014 validation and 3,850 OOD examples (R, "Datasets", "Probing").

Key results

  • Dense linear probes are near perfect: AUROC 1.0 on train and validation and 0.999 OOD (R, "Results").
  • Single SAE latents fail to fit even the training set (R, "Our Task", item 2).
  • k-sparse SAE probes fit the training set from about k = 20 and generalise in distribution, but do worse OOD; the authors had predicted the reverse (R, "Our Task", item 3; "Results").
  • SAEs trained or fine-tuned on chat data close about half the gap to dense probes, no more. The ways of training them tried all did about as well (R, TL;DR; "Comparing different ways to train Chat SAEs").
  • Information is lost. A probe trained on the SAE's reconstruction does worse OOD than one on the raw activations: AUROC gap 0.048 (Gemma Scope SAE) and 0.039 (fine-tuned SAE) (R, "Related Work and Discussion", table).
  • One use that worked: SAE latents exposed spurious differences in the data, such as only the harmless set containing questions, which the team then removed. This helped the probes too (R, "Dataset debugging with SAEs").
  • Two method notes: averaging auto-interpretability over latents uniformly hides frequent, unreadable latents, so weight by firing frequency; a new JumpReLU penalty on firing frequency removes latents that fire on more than about 10% of tokens, at slightly worse reconstruction (R, "Autointerp and high frequency latents", "Removing High Frequency Latents from JumpReLU SAEs").

The decision

"We are deprioritising fundamental SAE research for the moment" (R, TL;DR). The team does not call SAEs useless; it expects them to stay in the toolkit, especially for exploratory debugging, but not to transform interpretability, and it "speculate[s] that the field is over-invested in them" (R, TL;DR; "Conclusions and Strategic Updates"). Its reasons go beyond this task: negative results by others on probing (Kantamneni et al.), unlearning (Farrell et al.) and steering (Wu et al., AxBench) (R, same section). Next directions named: model diffing, model organisms of deception, interpreting thinking models (same).

Caveats

  • One task, one model, one layer. The authors say they cannot extrapolate to all OOD settings (R, "Results").
  • They used SAEs to clean the data before training the probes, which "slightly stacks the deck against SAEs" (R, "Our Task").
  • They are no longer sure that even a perfect SAE should beat a linear probe OOD: "harmful intent" may not be a simple mix of a few concepts (R, "Is it surprising that SAEs didn't work?").
  • The JumpReLU formulas in the raw copy lost their layout; this page quotes none of them.
