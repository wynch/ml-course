Gemini Deep Think at IMO 2025

source created 2026-10-20 updated 2026-10-20 llm-reasoning mathematics theorem-proving test-time-compute google-deepmind benchmarks 2025

Authors: Thang Luong and Edward Lockhart (byline); Google DeepMind Published: 2025-07-21, DeepMind blog; no paper Raw copy: raw/web-2025-deepmind-gemini-deep-think-imo-gold.md (captured 2026-10-20; the page now also links IMO-Bench, added later)

Every claim below cites the raw file as R, with its section.

Summary

An advanced version of Gemini Deep Think solved five of the six IMO 2025 problems in full, scoring 35 of 42 points, which is gold-medal level. IMO coordinators graded the answers by the same rules as the students' (R, opening; "Breakthrough Performance at IMO 2025"). It worked "end-to-end in natural language", from the official problem text to written proofs, inside the 4.5-hour limit (R, "Breakthrough Performance").

The year before, AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2 reached silver (four problems, 28 points), but experts first translated the problems into Lean and similar languages, and the systems took two to three days (R, opening; "Breakthrough Performance").

Key claims

  • Official grading. DeepMind was among the first labs to have model answers "officially graded and certified" by IMO coordinators; the IMO President is quoted confirming 35 of 42 (R, opening; "Breakthrough Performance").
  • Parallel thinking. Deep Think explores and combines several candidate solutions before answering, instead of one chain of thought (R, "Making the most of Deep Think mode").
  • Extra training and help. This version was trained with new RL methods on multi-step reasoning and theorem-proving data, given a curated corpus of good maths solutions, and given "general hints and tips" on IMO problems in its instructions (R, same section).
  • Formal work continues. DeepMind still develops AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry, and expects systems that join natural language with verified formal reasoning (R, "The Future of AI and Mathematics").

Caveats

  • The IMO checked the answers, not the system: "their review does not extend to validating our system, processes, or underlying model" (R, closing note).
  • One competition, six problems, one run reported; no failure analysis, no compute figure.
  • The model is "an advanced version", not the product; DeepMind planned to give a version to trusted testers, then Google AI Ultra subscribers (R, "Making the most of Deep Think mode").
  • A human grader stood where a proof checker would stand: correctness was still judged outside the model.
