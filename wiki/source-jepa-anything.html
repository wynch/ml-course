Every claim below cites the raw file as R, with its section, table or figure.

It replaces source-orthogonal-jepa (arXiv 2608.20065), which its first author withdrew on 2026-09-21. The withdrawal note calls this paper "a substantially revised version" of that one (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20065, abs page, read 2026-10-20). This paper does not cite the withdrawn one.

Summary

A JEPA predicts the embedding of a hidden target from the embedding of a context; a target encoder, an exponential moving average (EMA) of the online encoder with no gradient, supplies the target (R, §2.1, Eq. 2). The authors argue that one target vector and one predictor let easy or loud structure take the capacity, while weaker structure gets conflicting gradients (R, §1).

Their fix, orthogonal predictive factorization (OPF): K learned matrices, each d × r with Kr = d, split the target embedding into K parts; each part gets its own predictor; the predictions are put back together into one full latent state through the pseudoinverse of the split, which is the matrices themselves when they are orthogonal (R, §2.2, Eq. 3–5). Four terms train it: per-part regression, a penalty pushing the matrices toward orthonormal and apart, a floor on the spread of each projected coordinate, and a floor on the spread of the online encoder (R, §2.2–2.3, Eq. 6–10). OPF is added to each domain's own loss; it does not replace it (R, §2.3, Eq. 10).

The same core runs in seven domains: vision, single cells, patient records, control, molecular dynamics, physical fields and weather (R, Abstract, Table 2).

Key results

All comparisons hold adapter, encoder, sampler, budget, split and readout fixed between standard JEPA and JEPA-Anything (R, §3.1).

  • The geometry works as built. On Interventional Pong, against unconstrained heads of the same size: overlap between factor subspaces 0.455 against about 5 × 10⁻¹⁶; condition number 438.5 against 1.00005 (R, §3.3.1, Table 5). This checks the construction, not the usefulness.
  • Intervention prediction. One-step error after a single intervention falls 34.8% (MSE 0.009541 → 0.006218); after an unseen combination of interventions 12.9%; over a six-step free rollout 8.6%. Five seeds (R, §3.3.1, Figure 4).
  • Ten dynamics tasks (CausalWorld, DeepMind Control, PDEBench, WeatherBench2): better on all ten, five seeds; the gains are shown only as a bar chart (R, Abstract, §3.3.2, Figure 5). The table of four tasks ranges from almost nothing (DMC pixels, step 6: 0.008594 → 0.008526) to large (PDEBench shallow water, step 6: 0.010510 → 0.006522) (R, Table 6).
  • PDE rollouts. APEBench Burgers error falls about 49.5% on held-out late states and 44.7% over six steps; Kuramoto–Sivashinsky about 13.2%; every seed improves (R, §3.3.2, Table 7). Over 50 steps of Burgers, the gain shrinks to about 3% (R, Table 8).
  • Planning is mixed. With a CEM planner on Hopper, Walker2d and HalfCheetah, JEPA-Anything wins on Walker2d and HalfCheetah, loses on Hopper (R, §3.3.3, Figure 6). The authors call planning "environment-dependent".
  • Every factor is used. In the locomotion model, replacing any one factor by its mean raises 20-step rollout error in all three environments; on HalfCheetah, it lowers return in four or five of five seeds (R, §3.3.4, Figure 7). A probe on one factor at a time reads one motion best, with R² 0.29–0.42 on Hopper (R, §3.3.4).
  • Molecules. Lowest one-step error and lowest 100-step drift on water, quartz, paracetamol and benzene, against the same model pretrained as a plain JEPA (water 2.536 → 2.459 Å; benzene 0.0701 → 0.0645 Å). Five seeds (R, §3.3.5, Table 9).
  • Readout tasks. Visual binding with DINOv3: .572 → .581 held-out accuracy; single cells, PBMC clustering AvgBIO 0.7830 → 0.8301 against Cell-JEPA (R, Tables 3–4). Clinical forecasting over 1,000+ events: a higher mean PRAUC, shown only in a figure (R, §3.2.3, Figure 3).

Caveats

  • The wet-lab claim is one paragraph and a figure: factor analysis named IL-18 plus CD73 blockade, and later lab work supported it, in three organoids, three tumour fragments and mice (R, §3.4.1, Figure 8). The text gives neither how the factor analysis picked it nor how many other candidates it named.
  • The orbit result recovers Kepler's f ∝ a^(−3/2) with slope −1.4991 from one run on simulated orbits (R, §3.4.2, Table 2, Figure 9). Simulated orbits obey that law by construction.
  • The predictor is still deterministic: each factor head maps context to one point (R, §2.2, Eq. 4).
  • The authors say orthogonal factors do not by themselves establish causal mechanisms (R, §6).
  • They used OpenAI Codex for editing, checks and "feedback on experimental design" (R, Appendix B.3).
