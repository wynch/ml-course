OpenAI o1 — Learning to Reason with LLMs

source created 2026-05-06 updated 2026-05-06 openai reasoning test-time-compute rlhf system-card ai-alignment

Author: OpenAI Date: September 2024

Summary

The introductory blog post and accompanying system card for OpenAI's o1, the first deployed frontier model trained with large-scale reinforcement learning to produce a long internal chain of thought before answering. o1 reset expectations for math, coding, and PhD-level science benchmarks and made "test-time compute" a first-class lever in frontier model design. Architectural and training details are deliberately scarce; the system card is the primary public artefact.

Key Claims

What o1 does

  • Before producing a user-visible answer, o1 generates an extended internal chain of thought that it uses to plan, verify, and self-correct.
  • The chain of thought is hidden from end users by policy — both for competitive reasons and (the system card argues) so the model can think freely without the CoT itself becoming an alignment surface.
  • More inference compute → better answers. The blog post's headline plot shows monotonic accuracy gains with more reasoning tokens on AIME and Codeforces.

How it was trained

  • "Large-scale reinforcement learning" with chain-of-thought as the policy. Specifics of the algorithm, reward, and dataset are not disclosed.
  • Public DeepSeek-R1 work (Jan 2025) is widely understood to have replicated the core recipe, lending strong indirect evidence that GRPO-style RL with verifiable rewards is sufficient.

Benchmark headlines

  • 89th percentile on Codeforces.
  • AIME 2024: 83% (vs ~13% for GPT-4o), without tool use.
  • GPQA Diamond: 78% — surpassing the human PhD baseline (~70%) for the first time.
  • The benchmarks where o1 most outperforms GPT-4o are precisely the verifiable-reward domains (math, code, structured science) — consistent with the RL story.

Alignment story (deliberative alignment)

  • The companion paper "Deliberative Alignment" trains the model to reason explicitly about safety policies within its hidden CoT before responding, rather than relying purely on output-level RLHF.
  • Claim: o1 is more robust to jailbreaks because it can think about whether to comply.
  • Tradeoff: hidden CoT means humans cannot directly audit the safety reasoning — the system card devotes substantial space to this tension.

Why it matters

  • The first deployed model where test-time compute scaling is a primary product axis. Rewrote the frontier-model menu (GPT-4o for cheap chat; o1 for hard problems).
  • Triggered the open replication wave that produced source-deepseek-r1, and established reasoning-via-RL as the default 2025 recipe.
  • "Hidden CoT" has become a contested alignment-research surface — connects to source-measuring-cot-monitorability and the broader question of whether CoT is the actual computation.
