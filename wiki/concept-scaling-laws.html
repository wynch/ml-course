Scaling Laws

concept created 2026-04-05 updated 2026-10-23 scaling training compute theory

Empirical and theoretical relationships between model size, training data, compute, and performance. The "just scale" paradigm has driven much of LLM progress, but is increasingly nuanced.

Theoretical Grounding

  • source-mit-6-7960-scaling-laws-lecture — added 2026-10-23: Isola's MIT lecture. One hypothesis for the exponent: a ReLU net fits data on a d-dimensional manifold with N linear pieces, so loss falls as N^(−4/d) and α ≈ 4/d, with d the intrinsic dimension of the network's embedding of the data, which depends on data and task (Sharma and Kaplan 2020; the N^(−4/d) rate assumes a smooth target); measured intrinsic dimension matches for small vision models; for GPT only α ≳ 4/d, so d ≳ 4/α (raw/web-2024-mit-6-7960-lec20-scaling-laws.md, slides 33–35). Also the critical batch size: past B_noise = trace(HΣ)/(GᵀHG), a bigger batch buys little progress per step (slides 43–44).
  • source-kaplan-scaling-laws — Kaplan 2020: smooth power-law L(N), L(D), L(C); the original empirical scaling-law paper. Compute-optimal favours bigger models (later corrected by Chinchilla).
  • source-chinchilla-compute-optimal — Hoffmann 2022: corrects Kaplan; N and D should scale ~1:1. 70B / 1.4T tokens beats 280B Gopher at the same FLOPs. The compute-optimal bible cited by every model report.
    • Contradiction (2026-10-19): production models train well past Chinchilla-optimal for inference cost (arXiv 2401.00448); approach 3 failed replication (arXiv 2404.10102). Both not in raw; see source-chinchilla-compute-optimal.
  • source-reconciling-kaplan-chinchilla — Pearce and Song 2024: the Kaplan–Chinchilla gap comes mostly from counting. Kaplan left out the embedding parameters and studied small models, where those are a large share; redone that way, Chinchilla's own formula gives Kaplan's exponent (0.74–0.78 against 0.73), and tiny models counted in total parameters give Chinchilla's (0.49) (raw/arxiv-2406-12907-reconciling-kaplan-and-chinchilla-scaling-laws.md, §3, §4, Table 1). Their advice for any scaling fit: count total parameters and compute, and keep a loss floor (same file, §7).
  • source-neural-scaling-laws-from-language-statistics — First parameter-free derivation of scaling exponents from natural language statistics (token correlations and conditional entropy decay). Validated on GPT-2 and LLaMA.
  • source-superposition-robust-scaling — Liu, Liu, Gore (MIT, 2025): a mechanism for the size exponent. In Anthropic's toy model, when nearly all features are stored as overlapping directions (strong superposition), loss falls as 1/width for many feature-frequency patterns; when few are stored, the loss follows the data's own power law. Open LLMs' output layers fit the first case; their width exponent is 0.91 ± 0.04 (raw/arxiv-2505-10465-superposition-yields-robust-neural-scaling.md, §3.1–3.3, Figure 6). The authors predict the exponent cannot be pushed much past 1 for natural language (same file, §5). Toy model unsolved; only the output layer is measured (same file, §3.3, §5).
  • source-learning-mechanics — a 2026 position paper by fourteen theorists: a physics-style theory of training is forming, but it cannot yet predict scaling-law exponents from properties of the data (https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21691v1, §2.3, as its source page reports; not in raw, whose copy holds the abstract only).

Challenges to Pure Scaling

  • source-language-models-as-scaffolds — argues existing models are "severely underutilized"; scaffolding and structural innovation can unlock capability without retraining.
  • source-zhang-harness-blog — same author, five months later: data scaling "will remain the biggest driver of progress", and the harness's inductive biases set its coefficients. Hand-coding strategies into harnesses will lose to the bitter lesson (raw/web-2026-alexzhang-harness-compositional.md, §"The Scaling Picture and Moving Forward"). Here the harness is trained through, not only used at inference.
    • No contradiction with source-language-models-as-scaffolds: its old wiki line "architecture matters more than raw scale" was our wording. The February post itself says scaling is not dead (checked live 2026-09-25). Both posts keep scale and add structure on top.
  • source-memory-mosaics-at-scale — on learning new tasks from the prompt, 8× the data does not close the gap to an architecture change: a transformer on 8T tokens scores 46.9 on RULER multi-document questions at 32k against 53.4 for Memory Mosaics v2 on 1T, and on in-context classification with anonymous labels more data makes the transformer worse (raw/arxiv-2507-03285-memory-mosaics-at-scale.md, §6.1, Table 5, Figure 7). The authors call this a weakness of the "more data more computation" belief (same file, §8).
    • Contradiction (partial, weak) with the source-zhang-harness-blog line above, that data scaling "will remain the biggest driver of progress". The same sentence of that post says the machinery and its inductive biases set the coefficients of that scaling, which is what this result shows; the two part only on whether data stays the biggest driver. One architecture pair, new-task measures only, and the 8T transformer differs in recipe (grouped-query attention, 8k training context; same file, Table 5 caption). The paper agrees that for knowledge from training, more data and a bigger model win (same file, §6.1, footnote 15).
  • source-residual-matrix-transformer — achieves equivalent loss with 58% fewer FLOPs by redesigning the residual stream.
  • source-neural-depth-scaling — loss falls as 1/depth; the authors say this probably comes from layers averaging like an ensemble, which would mean diminishing returns from adding layers (arXiv 2602.05970, abstract; hedge carried from A10).

Practical Scaling

  • source-frontier-model-training — practical guide from 7 frontier models: data mixture and architecture choices dominate algorithmic tweaks.
  • source-lacy-small-language-models — small models should allocate capacity wisely, not try to learn everything.
  • source-bundle-mit-6-7960-optimizer-geometry — added 2026-10-23: a side of scaling these laws leave out: keeping the best learning rate fixed as width and depth grow, by sizing each layer's updates in a width-aware norm (Bernstein's claim; one GPT sweep, raw/web-2024-mit-6-7960-lec23-metrized-deep-learning.md, slide 34).

The Emergence Debate

  • source-emergence-debate (Wei 2022 + Schaeffer 2023) — the canonical claim/rebuttal pair. Wei: emergent abilities aren't predictable from smaller-scale behaviour. Schaeffer: >92% of claimed emergent abilities on BIG-Bench appear under one of two metrics, Multiple Choice Grade or Exact String Match; under continuous metrics the jumps mostly vanish — emergence as a metric artefact. (Corrected 2026-10-19: the page said the 92% disappear.) See also analysis-emergence-vs-distribution-sharpening.

Generalization Theory

  • source-bundle-mit-6-7960-approximation-generalization — added 2026-10-23: MIT lectures on approximation (shallow nets are universal at exponential cost; depth separations) and generalization (random labels, double descent, inductive biases). Its practical slide: settling width against depth needs care over "minor details" of the training pipeline, as Chinchilla's revision of Kaplan showed (raw/web-2024-mit-6-7960-lec03-approximation-theory.md, slides 38–39). Hub: concept-generalization.
  • source-litman-generalization-theory — Litman & Guo 2026: NTK partitions output space into signal/noise channels; SGD routes coherent population structure to signal directions, idiosyncratic memorization to slow diffusive walk in noise dimensions. Unifies benign overfitting, double descent, implicit bias, and grokking under one mechanism. Practical artefact: SNR preconditioner on Adam → 5× grokking acceleration.
  • source-neural-low-degree-filtering — Dandi et al. 2026: deep feature learning as iterative spectral filtering, where each layer selects accessible low-degree label-correlated directions and makes new compositional features available to later layers.

Data-constrained scaling

  • source-diffusion-beats-ar-data-constrained — Prabhudesai, Wu et al. 2025 fit the repeated-data scaling law of Muennighoff et al. to 200 models trained on 25M–100M unique tokens. Reused data loses half its value after about 31 epochs for left-to-right models and about 500 for masked diffusion; past a compute threshold that grows as U^2.174 in the unique tokens U, diffusion reaches lower loss (raw/arxiv-2507-15857-diffusion-beats-autoregressive-in-data-constrained.md, §2.3, §3.2–3.3, Table 1b, Figure 6). The two families cross: which one is "better" depends on whether compute or data runs out first (same file, §4). The diffusion side wins at much more compute, 1.24e20 against 4.32e18 FLOPs for the best models in Table 4 (same file, Appendix 7).

Scaling RL

  • ScaleRL (Khatri et al., arXiv 2510.13786, Oct 2025): fits sigmoid compute-performance curves to RL training over more than 400,000 GPU-hours; stable recipes follow predictable curves, so small runs predict large ones (abstract; not in raw). The first RL scaling law on this page.

Test-Time vs Train-Time Compute

  • source-snell-test-time-compute-scaling — Snell 2024: scaling test-time compute can be more compute-efficient than scaling parameters for many tasks.

Open Questions

  • Can theoretical scaling laws predict performance across architectures, not just within one family?
  • At what point does scaffolding/inference-time compute dominate further pre-training scale?
    • One data point: an RLM gets roughly 10× the eval lift of a plain Transformer for the same train lift (raw/web-2026-alexzhang-harness-compositional.md, §Intro, Figure 1). One model size, one lab.
  • Is "emergence" real or an artefact? See the Wei/Schaeffer pair above.
