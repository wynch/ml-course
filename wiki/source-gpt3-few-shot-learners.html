Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3)

source created 2026-05-06 updated 2026-10-09 llm in-context-learning scaling foundational gpt 2020

Brown, Mann, Ryder, Subbiah, Kaplan, Dhariwal, Neelakantan, Shyam, Sastry, Askell et al. — arXiv 2005.14165, NeurIPS 2020 (OpenAI)

Trains an autoregressive transformer at unprecedented scale (175B parameters, ~300B training tokens, ~3.14×10²³ FLOPs) and shows that in-context learning emerges from scale: the model performs new tasks from a handful of demonstrations in the prompt, without any gradient updates. The paper that turned "language model" into "general-purpose API."

Method

  • Same decoder-only transformer as GPT-2; only difference is scale (175B vs 1.5B params, 96 layers, d_model=12288, 96 heads).
  • Trained on Common Crawl (filtered), WebText2, Books1/Books2, Wikipedia — ~300B tokens.
  • Evaluation modes — all done without weight updates:
    • Zero-shot: task description only.
    • One-shot: description + one example.
    • Few-shot: description + K examples (typically 10–100, fits in context).

Key results

  • Performance scales smoothly with parameter count across >24 NLP tasks, often crossing into competitive territory only at 175B.
  • TriviaQA few-shot: 71.2% — beats fine-tuned SOTA at the time.
  • LAMBADA few-shot: 86.4% — vs ~68% prior SOTA.
  • SuperGLUE few-shot ≈ fine-tuned BERT-Large.
  • Generates news articles humans can't reliably distinguish from human-written (52% accuracy, near chance).
  • Failures: arithmetic on >2-digit numbers, WiC, ANLI, novel reasoning chains. Foreshadows the concept-llm-reasoning debate.

Why it matters

Two world-shifting claims:

  1. In-context learning is real and emergent. The model learns from the prompt, with no gradient updates — implying capabilities are latent in the weights and "unlocked" by the right context. This is the seed of concept-test-time-compute, source-language-models-as-scaffolds, and the entire concept-harness-as-symbolic-substrate thesis: the substrate is fixed; what changes is what you write in the context window.
  2. Scaling pays off in capability, not just loss. Empirically validates the source-kaplan-scaling-laws prediction that more compute → smoother loss curves → qualitatively new behaviours at threshold sizes. Loss is the proxy; few-shot performance is the payoff. Sets up the concept-scaling-laws debate that source-chinchilla-compute-optimal would refine two years later.

GPT-3 also reframed everything downstream: source-instructgpt-rlhf is "GPT-3 made helpful," source-chain-of-thought-foundations discovers an emergent prompting technique that only works at GPT-3 scale, and the entire concept-ai-agents line presupposes a base model with this kind of generality. The reasoning-vs-pattern-matching question this wiki keeps returning to — see source-llm-reasoning-skeptics, source-what-llms-think-unprompted — was first sharpened by GPT-3's mix of stunning generalisations and embarrassing failures on slightly out-of-distribution problems.

Later evidence

source-memory-mosaics-at-scale measures in-context learning against the number of examples on three classification tasks. Its llama-style transformers (about 8B parameters, 1T tokens) get worse as more examples are added, while Memory Mosaics v2 gets better; the numbers are in plots only (raw/arxiv-2507-03285-memory-mosaics-at-scale.md, §5.3, Figures 3-4). Different tasks and models from GPT-3; this page makes no claim on how accuracy moves with the number of examples.
