Summary

Interpretability offers ways to control a model by editing its activations: add a "steering vector", or push up an SAE feature. AxBench asks how these compare with plain prompting and fine-tuning, on two tasks (R, §1, §3):

  • concept detection: does a direction fire on text that holds a concept? (scored by AUROC);
  • steering: add the direction while the model answers an instruction, and have an LLM judge rate concept, instruction-following and fluency from 0 to 2; the score is their harmonic mean, so failing any one sinks it (R, §3.3).

The 500 concepts come from the GemmaScope SAE labels on Neuronpedia; GPT-4o-mini writes 144 training and about 72 test examples per concept, including hard negatives that use another sense of a related word (R, §3.1, §4.1). Models: Gemma-2-2B and Gemma-2-9B, two layers each (R, §4.1).

Methods: difference of means (DiffMean), PCA, linear artificial tomography (LAT), a linear probe, a learned steering vector, GemmaScope SAEs (as labelled, or picked by AUROC on the training set), and the authors' own ReFT-r1, a single direction trained to both detect and steer; baselines are prompting, full fine-tuning, LoRA and LoReFT (R, §4).

Key results

  • Steering: prompting wins. Mean overall score, averaged over four model-layer settings: prompting 0.894, LoReFT 0.741, full fine-tuning 0.676 (2B only), LoRA 0.615, ReFT-r1 0.543, DiffMean 0.239, SAE 0.165, SAE picked by AUROC 0.157; LAT, PCA, probe and the learned vector score lower still (R, §5.2, Table 2). Prompting beats the SAE on 94.5% of concepts (R, Table 3).
  • Detection: simple directions win. Mean AUROC: DiffMean 0.942, probe 0.940, ReFT-r1 0.938 (no significant gap between the three), prompting 0.929, SAE picked by AUROC 0.917, bag of words 0.914, SAE as labelled 0.695 (R, §5.1, Table 1). A word-count classifier beats the labelled SAE features.
  • Bigger model, bigger prompting lead. ReFT-r1 is close to prompting on Gemma-2-2B but well behind on 9B, where prompting's score jumps from about 0.7 to about 1.07 (R, §5.2, Table 2).
  • Better detection does not mean better steering: the AUROC-picked SAE detects better than the labelled one but steers slightly worse (R, §5.2).
  • Every steering method loses instruction-following as the push grows (R, §5.2, Figure 4).

Caveats

  • Data and judge are both LLMs (GPT-4o-mini), so scores carry their biases; the authors note a bag-of-words classifier exploits quirks of LLM-written text (R, §3.1, §4).
  • SAE concepts use Neuronpedia's automatic labels, which skew to token-level ideas; the authors say SAE results partly reflect those labels (R, §6).
  • For Gemma-2-2B the SAEs were trained on the base model but used on the instruction-tuned one (R, §4.1, footnote 12). SAE steering adds the feature direction; clamping is in an ablation only (R, §4, Appendix F).
  • The text gives ReFT-r1 and DiffMean win rates over SAEs of 88.0% and 61.6%; Table 3 of the same version gives 81.8% and 58.7% (R, §5.2, Table 3).
  • The prompting baseline cannot detect concepts inside the model, which steering can; the case for steering is control with an inspectable handle, not raw score (R, Abstract, §6).
