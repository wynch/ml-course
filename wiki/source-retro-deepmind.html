RETRO — Retrieval-Enhanced Transformer

source created 2026-05-07 updated 2026-05-07 retrieval rag language-models deepmind 2021

Borgeaud, Mensch, Hoffmann, Cai, Rutherford, et al. — arXiv 2112.04426, Dec 2021 (DeepMind)

The most radical RAG paper of the early era: instead of bolting retrieval on as a separate step before generation, RETRO bakes retrieval into the transformer itself as a new architectural block. A 7.5B-parameter RETRO model matches GPT-3 (175B) and Jurassic-1 (178B) on language-modeling benchmarks while being 25× smaller, by reading from a frozen 2-trillion-token retrieval database during every forward pass.

Method

  • Pretraining corpus split into chunks (default 64 tokens). For each chunk, retrieve k=2 nearest neighbours from a 2T-token external database using a frozen BERT encoder (no gradient flow through retrieval).
  • New transformer block: Chunked Cross-Attention (CCA), interleaved with standard self-attention. Each position attends to the retrieved neighbours of the preceding chunk.
  • Database stored in a SCaNN nearest-neighbour index; retrieval is the dominant inference cost.
  • Training: standard next-token loss; the model learns to use retrieved evidence when helpful.
  • Retrieval-off ablation gracefully degrades to a regular transformer.

Key numbers

  • RETRO 7.5B with 2T-token database: bits-per-byte on Pile competitive with Gopher 280B and GPT-3 175B.
  • 25× parameter reduction at equal perplexity vs same-data baselines.
  • Database scaling: monotonic gains all the way to 2T tokens — retrieval keeps paying off where pretraining data saturates.
  • Question-answering: significant gains on Natural Questions over non-retrieval LMs at the same parameter count.

Why it matters

RETRO is the most explicit instantiation of the "parameters store skill, retrieval stores facts" thesis. Three durable contributions:

  1. Architectural retrieval, not pipeline retrieval. Unlike source-dpr-dense-passage-retrieval-style RAG that retrieves once and concatenates, RETRO retrieves chunk-by-chunk and integrates via cross-attention. This makes retrieval a continuous, fine-grained signal rather than a one-shot context dump.
  2. Decouples knowledge from weights. You can swap or update the 2T database without retraining. Anticipates the broader argument in source-engram-deepseek that memory should be plug-in modules and source-memoryllm-plug-n-play-ffn that FFN-as-memory is editable.
  3. Strong scaling-law evidence that retrieval is a complementary axis to parameters and data (source-kaplan-scaling-laws, source-chinchilla-compute-optimal). DeepMind showed the same curve as Chinchilla but with retrieval shifting the entire compute–performance frontier left.

RETRO is also a notable counterexample to "just scale the context window." Trillion-token-scale knowledge cannot live in any practical context window; chunked cross-attention to a retrieval index is one principled way out. Compare with source-recursive-language-models and source-lcm-lossless-context-management which take different approaches to the same long-context bottleneck.

In retrospect: RETRO's exact architecture didn't become dominant — the industry largely settled on cheaper "retrieve-and-prompt" RAG (source-dpr-dense-passage-retrieval, source-self-rag) for engineering reasons. But its core claim — retrieval is a free axis — remains correct and is the reason every frontier lab now ships RAG.
