Memorization

concept created 2026-10-23 updated 2026-10-23 memorization training-dynamics forgetting membership-inference privacy language-models hub

Working definition (this wiki's own): a model memorises an example when training on it raises the model's likelihood of that example beyond what it would assign to similar unseen text. Potts's looser heuristic is that "Memorization correlates with higher logprobs" (raw/web-2026-potts-palimpsestic-memorization.md, "The relationship between memorization and logprobs"). Most papers use a yes-or-no test instead: does a prefix make the model reproduce the training continuation? (raw/web-2026-potts-palimpsestic-memorization.md, "The relationship between memorization and logprobs"). Potts argues the yes-or-no test hides near misses and depends on how one samples (same section).

Over training: the palimpsest

Three terms first. A model's likelihood of a text is the probability it gives that text; higher means the text looks more familiar to it. A rank correlation asks whether two lists go up together in order, from −1 to 1, with 0 for no link. A p-value is the chance of seeing a link at least this strong if there were truly none; tiny p-values mean chance is a poor explanation.

Each example's trace peaks a few steps after it is seen, then fades toward the mean, and has not faded fully after 70,000 more steps (raw/arxiv-2510-19796-blackbox-model-provenance-via-palimpsestic-membership.md, Appendix A.3). Later examples therefore sit a little higher, and a trained model's likelihoods rank its training batches in the order it saw them (raw/web-2026-potts-palimpsestic-memorization.md, "Memorization profiles", "Seeing the palimpsest"). The effect is small, a rank correlation of 0.001 to 0.1 across many examples, but it is measurable and survives fine-tuning, model averaging and tens of thousands of further steps (raw/arxiv-2510-19796-blackbox-model-provenance-via-palimpsestic-membership.md, §4.2, Appendix A.3). Larger models keep more (same file, Appendix A.3). See source-palimpsest-model-provenance.

What it is used for

  • Provenance. The trace lets a model's trainer test whether another model or a body of text derives from theirs, with exact p-values for queried models and for the n-gram text test, approximate ones (from a z-score) for the reshuffle text test (source-palimpsest-model-provenance).
  • Forgetting. The same curves describe forgetting one example at a time (concept-continual-learning).
  • Removal. Unlearning aims to erase an example's influence; a trace detectable by statistics sets a high bar (concept-machine-unlearning).
  • Memorizing and generalizing at once (added 2026-10-23). A network can fit random labels perfectly and still generalize on real ones: on CIFAR-10, Inception reaches 100% training accuracy on random labels (9.78% test) and 85.75–89.31% test accuracy on real labels (raw/web-2024-mit-6-7960-lec06-generalization.md, slide 44, Zhang et al. 2017). Isola's "filing cabinet", a store of training pairs with no rule between them, is the model that memorizes without generalizing (same file, slides 6, 8). See source-bundle-mit-6-7960-approximation-generalization and concept-generalization.

Open questions

  • Is the delay a property of the optimiser? The delay between seeing an example and its peak likelihood has been put down to Adam's momentum (raw/web-2026-potts-palimpsestic-memorization.md, "Memorization profiles", citing Huang et al. 2024).
  • Distillation passes behaviour but, in pilot runs, hardly any order trace (same file, "Looking ahead"). What exactly does a distilled student inherit?
  • Do all architectures memorise alike? Potts flags a paper suggesting not (same file, "Looking ahead").
