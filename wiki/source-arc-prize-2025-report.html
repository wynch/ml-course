ARC Prize 2025: Technical Report

Authors: François Chollet, Mike Knoop, Gregory Kamradt, Bryan Landers (the ARC Prize team) Published: arXiv 2601.10904, v1 2026-01-15 (the only version) Raw copy: raw/arxiv-2601-10904-arc-prize-2025-technical-report.md (captured 2026-10-19, full text of v1)

Every claim below cites the raw file as R, with its section or table. The organisers wrote it, so it reports their own competition.

Summary

The 2025 contest ran on ARC-AGI-2 from 2025-03-26 to 2025-11-03. ARC-AGI-2 has 400 public training tasks (taken from ARC-AGI-1), 120 semi-private tasks that commercial APIs have seen, and 120 private tasks used for final scoring (R, §1.1, §2.1). Every ARC-AGI-2 task was solved by at least two untrained people (R, §1.1).

1,455 teams sent 15,154 entries to Kaggle. The top score was 24.03% on the private set, at $0.20 per task; 90 papers came in, against 47 in 2024 (R, §2.1, Table 1).

The report's theme is the refinement loop: per task, a program or a set of weights is improved step by step against a feedback signal (R, §3). It names four kinds: test-time training of a pretrained model, training from scratch on one task ("zero-pretraining"), evolutionary program synthesis, and chain-of-thought refined against a verifier (R, §3.1).

Runners-up include two evolutionary program-synthesis papers (Berman; Pang), vector symbolic algebras (Joffe, Eliasmith) and neural cellular automata (R, Table 2). A neuro-symbolic architecture (Das et al.) got an honourable mention (R, §2.4).

Test-time training won the Kaggle track in both 2024 and 2025 (R, §3.1.2).

Other claims

  • Harnesses. ARC Prize verified Poetiq's open-source harness on Gemini 3 Pro: ARC-AGI-2 rose from 31% at $0.81 per task to 54% at $31 per task. Poetiq reports similar accuracy on Claude Opus 4.5 at about $60 per task (R, §3.4). The report does not name the task set; the Kaggle numbers above are on the private set.
  • Longer chains. On one ARC-AGI-1 task Gemini 3 Pro used 96 reasoning tokens and Gemini 3 Deep Think 138,000 (R, §3.3).
  • Knowledge-bound reasoning. The authors judge that tasks are now automatable when the pretraining data covers the domain and a verifier gives feedback; reasoning in current models stays tied to what they know (R, §4).
  • Knowledge overfitting. The authors assert that ARC-AGI-1 and -2 are now being overfit, "accidentally or intentionally": public and private sets are close enough that a model trained on much public ARC data gains. Their evidence is one trace: Gemini 3 Deep Think used ARC's colour names though the harness never mentioned ARC. They cannot size the effect (R, §4.1, §4.2).
  • ARC-AGI-3 was announced for early 2026: hundreds of interactive environments testing exploration, planning, memory, goal acquisition and alignment, scored on action efficiency against humans (R, §4.3). See source-arc-agi-3.

Caveats

  • The organisers grade their own contest. Results come from tables, not reruns.
  • CompressARC is given as 20–34% on ARC-AGI-1 in §2.3 and as 20% in §3.2.2; the report does not say which set or setting gives which.
  • TRM's 45% and 8% are on the public evaluation sets (the TRM paper, Table 5), not the private set the Kaggle numbers use.
  • The contamination claim rests on one quoted trace.
