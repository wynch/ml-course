Sparse Autoencoders

concept created 2026-10-20 updated 2026-10-20 mechanistic-interpretability sparse-autoencoders dictionary-learning transcoders crosscoders features hub

A sparse autoencoder (SAE) rewrites one of a model's activation vectors as a sparse sum of learned directions, in the hope that each direction is one of the model's own features (raw/arxiv-2602-14111-sanity-checks-for-sparse-autoencoders-do.md, §2.1). It is the main tool of dictionary learning in concept-mechanistic-interpretability: a bid to read a layer as a list of concepts rather than as neurons that each mean several things (concept-polysemanticity, concept-superposition).

This page gathers the family and the 2025–26 evidence against it. The source pages hold the detail.

The family

VariantWhat it reads and writesWhere in this wiki
SAEreads one activation, rebuilds the same activation through a wide, sparse hidden layersource-sae-monosemanticity-2023 (Bricken et al.; Cunningham et al.)

Transcoderreads an MLP's input, predicts the MLP's output, so it can stand in for the MLPsource-sae-scaling-trilogy (Gemma Scope 2), source-complete-replacement-lm-layers
Cross-layer transcoder (CLT)reads from one layer, writes to all later ones; the base of Anthropic's attribution graphssource-circuit-tracing-methods-anthropic
Crosscoderone dictionary trained across two models (say base and chat), so shared and model-only features can be told apartsource-crosscoder-model-diffing
Lorsa (low-rank sparse attention)stands in for an attention layer; each feature has its own "where to look" and a rank-1 "what to copy"source-complete-replacement-lm-layers (raw/web-2026-openmoss-complete-replacement-models.md, "Low-Rank Sparse Attention (Lorsa)")

With a transcoder for every MLP and a Lorsa for every attention layer, OpenMOSS replace all 56 sublayers of Qwen3-1.7B. Error terms remain, and show in the graph as nodes labelled "Uninterpretable" (raw/web-2026-openmoss-complete-replacement-models.md, "Introduction", "Node Types").

How it changed

  • 2023, the bet. Two groups at once trained SAEs on transformer activations and found readable features: Anthropic on a one-layer model's MLP, Cunningham et al. on the residual stream of Pythia models (source-sae-monosemanticity-2023; transformer-circuits.pub/2023/monosemantic-features; arXiv 2309.08600; not in raw).
  • 2024–25, scale and tools. Anthropic found millions of features in Claude 3 Sonnet, and clamping one (the Golden Gate Bridge) changed the model's behaviour. Google DeepMind released SAEs and transcoders for every layer of every Gemma 3 size (source-sae-scaling-trilogy; URLs on that page, not in raw).
  • 2025, circuits. Cross-layer transcoders became the nodes of attribution graphs (source-circuit-tracing-methods-anthropic, source-lindsey-2025-biology-of-llm). Per that methods paper, the CLT of the 18-layer model matches its next-token completion on 50% of prompts, so the graphs carry error nodes (as quoted on source-circuit-tracing-methods-anthropic; raw/goodfire-already-clean-2026-10-15.md, "Exploring Language Models").
  • 2025–26, the negative results. Listed below.

The negative results

Each line cites the raw file named; the source pages give caveats.

  • Probes win out of distribution. Google DeepMind's team detected harmful intent in Gemma 2 9B: a dense linear probe scores AUROC 0.999 on held-out jailbreaks; k-sparse SAE probes do worse there, though the team had predicted the reverse. A probe trained on the SAE's reconstruction loses 0.039 to 0.048 AUROC against one on the raw activation. The team "deprioritis[ed] fundamental SAE research" (raw/web-2025-gdm-negative-results-saes-downstream.md, TL;DR, "Results", "Related Work and Discussion"; source-gdm-sae-negative-results).
  • Random directions nearly match. In a toy with 3,200 planted features, BatchTopK and JumpReLU SAEs explain 67–71% of the variance but recover 7–9% of the features. On Gemma-2-2B and Llama-3-8B, SAEs whose decoder stays near or at its random start come within a few hundredths of trained ones on LLM-judged interpretability (0.87 against 0.90), sparse probing (0.69 against 0.72) and causal editing (raw/arxiv-2602-14111-sanity-checks-for-sparse-autoencoders-do.md, §3.2, §4.2, Abstract; source-sae-sanity-checks). A plain TopK SAE recovers far more of the toy's features; the authors call that an artefact (same file, Appendix B).
  • Prompting steers better. On 500 concepts in Gemma-2-2B and 9B, steering scores average 0.894 for prompting, 0.239 for a difference of means and 0.165 for GemmaScope SAE features; for spotting a concept, the labelled SAE features reach AUROC 0.695 against 0.942 for a difference of means (raw/arxiv-2501-17148-axbench-steering-llms-even-simple-baselines.md, §5, Tables 1–2; source-axbench).
  • In vision, SAE latents copy neurons. On a ConvNeXt last layer, 88% of SAE activity is near-copies of single neurons; removing the directions that match no neuron leaves accuracy unchanged (raw/goodfire-already-clean-2026-10-15.md, "What Dictionaries Learn"; source-goodfire-already-clean).

What still works, by the same sources: SAE latents exposed spurious differences in DeepMind's dataset, which the team then removed (raw/web-2025-gdm-negative-results-saes-downstream.md, "Dataset debugging with SAEs"). The sanity-check authors present their baselines as checks, "not a verdict on SAEs as a paradigm" (raw/arxiv-2602-14111-sanity-checks-for-sparse-autoencoders-do.md, §6).

Contradiction (open), 2026-10-20: source-sae-monosemanticity-2023 and source-sae-scaling-trilogy present SAEs as the working unit of interpretability, "theoretically forced" in the first page's words. The four results above say SAEs lose to probes and prompting on the tasks tried, nearly tie with random dictionaries, and in vision mostly copy neurons. Neither side has tested the other's setting: the negative results cover probing, steering, editing and one vision model, not circuit tracing with transcoders, which source-sae-sanity-checks leaves out (raw/arxiv-2602-14111-sanity-checks-for-sparse-autoencoders-do.md, §5).

Open questions

  • Do transcoders and crosscoders pass the random-baseline test? Not yet tried (raw/arxiv-2602-14111-sanity-checks-for-sparse-autoencoders-do.md, §5).
  • Should a perfect SAE beat a linear probe at all? DeepMind's team is no longer sure, since "harmful intent" may not be a mix of a few concepts (raw/web-2025-gdm-negative-results-saes-downstream.md, "Is it surprising that SAEs didn't work?").
  • If the neurons of modern models are mostly readable, as the Goodfire essay argues, what is left for a dictionary to do? (source-goodfire-already-clean).
