Flow Map Language Models (FMLM)

source created 2026-04-13 updated 2026-04-13 language-models diffusion flow-matching one-step-generation 2026

Lee, Yoo, Agarwal, Shah, Huang, Raghunathan, Hong, Boffi, Kim — arXiv 2602.16813, Feb 2026

Proposes a paradigm shift away from discrete diffusion for text generation. FMLM operates on continuous flows over one-hot token embeddings, enabling efficient single-step generation. The key innovation is learning a flow map — a direct mapping function that eliminates the iterative refinement typical of diffusion models — which the authors argue is "unavailable to discrete methods."

Method

  • Models learn both the flow and its flow map using cross-entropy objectives that respect the simplex geometry of discrete token distributions.
  • Evaluates three flow-map distillation approaches.
  • Continuous formulation preserves geometric properties crucial for discrete token outputs.

Results

  • Matches SOTA discrete diffusion baselines on One Billion Words and OpenWebText.
  • Distilled FMLM reaches the 8-step quality of recent few-step discrete diffusion language models in just one step.

Relevance

Challenges the assumption that discrete processes are necessary for modeling discrete modalities. Joins a thread of architectures beyond pure autoregressive decoding — see concept-transformer-architecture and its alternatives (source-olmo-hybrid-rnn-transformer, source-m2rnn-matrix-valued-states, source-residual-matrix-transformer). Especially interesting as a counterpoint to concept-test-time-compute: if one-step continuous generation matches multi-step diffusion, the "more steps = more compute = better" story gets weaker on this axis.
