The Emergence Debate

source created 2026-05-07 updated 2026-05-07 scaling-laws emergence evaluation metrics foundational debate

Two papers, ~10 months apart, that frame one of the most consequential ongoing debates in modern ML: are LLM capabilities qualitatively discontinuous at scale, or is "emergence" a measurement artefact? Read together — the pairing is the entry point.

Wei et al. — "Emergent Abilities of LLMs" (TMLR 2022)

arXiv: 2206.07682. Wei, Tay, Bommasani, Raffel, Zoph, Borgeaud, Yogatama, Bosma, Zhou, Metzler, Chi, Hashimoto, Vinyals, Liang, Dean, Fedus.

Defined "emergent ability" for the LLM era and gave the field a vocabulary for sudden capability jumps with scale.

"An ability is emergent if it is not present in smaller models but is present in larger models."

The headline implication: such abilities cannot be predicted by extrapolating from smaller-scale runs, breaking the smooth-scaling worldview of source-kaplan-scaling-laws and motivating training ever-larger models on the chance that new capabilities pop into existence.

Method: survey-style. Catalogues >137 BIG-Bench tasks plus prompting setups (few-shot, augmented, multi-step) showing non-smooth scaling — near-random performance until a threshold, then a sharp rise. Examples of canonical "emergent" capabilities (under the discontinuous-metric framing later critiqued):

  • Multi-digit arithmetic (3-digit add/subtract)
  • Word unscrambling, Persian QA, modular arithmetic
  • Few-shot chain-of-thought (source-chain-of-thought-foundations — same lead author): only meaningfully helpful at ~100B+ params
  • Instruction following, calibration, transliteration

Strategic impact: for two years after publication, "emergent abilities" was the most-cited justification for the scale-is-all-you-need narrative.

Schaeffer, Miranda, Koyejo — "Emergent Abilities a Mirage?" (NeurIPS 2023, Outstanding Paper)

arXiv: 2304.15004.

A pointed counterpoint: the apparent discontinuous emergence is largely a measurement artefact. Switch from a discontinuous, non-linear metric (exact-string-match, multiple-choice grade) to a continuous one (token-level edit distance, Brier score), and the same models show smooth, predictable scaling curves. Emergence lives in the metric, not the model.

Three lines of evidence:

  1. Direct test on InstructGPT/GPT-3. Pick tasks Wei et al. claimed were emergent, swap the discontinuous scoring metric for a continuous one, watch the sharp transition disappear.
  2. Meta-analysis of BIG-Bench. Survey all claimed-emergent abilities and check what metric they use. Result: emergence appears overwhelmingly under two specific metrics — Multiple Choice Grade and Exact String Match — and rarely under continuous ones. >92% of claimed emergent abilities use one of those two.
  3. Synthetic demonstration. Show that any smoothly-improving system (a vision classifier, a small autoencoder) can be made to look "emergent" by retroactively choosing a sufficiently nonlinear or thresholded metric. Emergence isn't unique to LLMs.

The argument doesn't deny that capabilities improve with scale — it denies that the improvement is qualitatively discontinuous.

Three predictions made and confirmed: (i) emergence disappears with continuous metrics, (ii) emergence disappears with finer-grained statistics, (iii) you can synthesise apparent emergence in vision tasks where smooth scaling is uncontroversial.

How to read the pair

  • Pro-emergence (Wei): scale produces qualitatively new capabilities; sub-scale extrapolation is unreliable; you have to actually train the bigger model.
  • Anti-emergence (Schaeffer): scale produces smooth improvements; capabilities are predictable from sub-scale runs; "emergence" is a metric artefact; the right move is more careful continuous evaluation.

The wiki's stance: smooth scaling and structural innovation (source-language-models-as-scaffolds, source-residual-matrix-transformer, concept-harness-as-symbolic-substrate) are more productive frames than faith in opaque phase transitions. Schaeffer is one reason this wiki leans that way.

Knock-on effects

The "metric hides smoothness" critique re-applies to many reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval all use exact-match-flavoured scoring), which:

  • Tempers headline gains from source-tree-of-thoughts, source-reflexion-verbal-rl, source-self-refine — large jumps in pass-rate may partly reflect the shape of pass/fail metrics.
  • Weakens parts of source-chain-of-thought-foundations's "CoT is emergent at 100B+" story.
  • Pushes back on concept-llm-reasoning arguments that lean on emergence to claim qualitatively new behaviour.
