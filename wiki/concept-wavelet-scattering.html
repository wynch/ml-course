Wavelet Scattering

concept created 2026-05-14 updated 2026-05-14 wavelets scattering-transform representation-learning signal-processing edge-ai robustness

Wavelet scattering is a signal representation that looks like a deep convolutional network but replaces learned early filters with fixed wavelet filter banks. A scattering transform repeatedly applies wavelet convolutions, a modulus nonlinearity, and averaging or pooling to build representations that are more invariant and stable than raw pixels, audio samples, or sensor readings.

The key idea for this wiki:

Some low-level perceptual structure may be better treated as mathematical geometry than as something the model must relearn from data.

Core Properties

  • Scale separation: wavelets split a signal into local variations at multiple scales.
  • Frequency localization: coefficients preserve where and at what frequency a structure appears.
  • Deformation stability: small warps or shifts should not destroy the representation.
  • Variance reduction: scattering can reduce irrelevant variability before a learned head sees the signal.
  • Fixed early filters: the substrate has no need to learn generic edges, local oscillations, or scale bands from scratch.

Why It Is Not Just Feature Engineering

The scattering transform is not a bag of handcrafted descriptors. It is a structured cascade with an explicit relationship to convolutional networks:

  1. wavelet filters play the role of first-layer and deeper convolution filters
  2. modulus replaces a learned nonlinearity
  3. averaging builds local invariance
  4. higher-order scattering captures interactions across scales

The difference from ordinary neural networks is where learning is placed. In scattering, the early perceptual geometry is fixed. Learning can then focus on task-specific composition, residual correction, prediction, and control.

Tradeoff

Scattering's strength is also its risk. Stability and invariance can erase information. Tasks that need exact geometry, identity, phase, causality, or controllable reconstruction may need:

  • less averaging
  • phase-aware statistics
  • adaptive wavelets
  • residual learners
  • sparse event routing
  • task-conditioned selection of scale and orientation

This is why the wiki should treat scattering as a substrate, not as a complete model.

2026 Direction

The newest relevant 2026 papers move scattering in three directions:

  • Moment-constrained generation: source-mallat-2026-moment-generative-models uses scattering moments as constraints or feature maps for maximum-entropy and training-free generative modeling.
  • Budgeted computation: source-scrapl-random-path-scattering samples scattering paths to reduce the cost of scattering-based objectives.
  • Uncertainty under shift: source-bayesian-scattering-uncertainty couples scattering features with a probabilistic head as a distribution-shift baseline.

For the local research program, this makes calibration, sparse path selection, and residual prediction more important than clean accuracy alone.

Research Use In This Wiki

The practical question is not "wavelets instead of neural networks." It is:

Can fixed multiscale operators reduce the amount of learned neural machinery needed for robust edge intelligence?

The first evidence gate should be small, strict, and comparative: same data, same budget, a tiny learned network versus scattering plus a small learned head, evaluated on accuracy per parameter, sample efficiency, latency, memory, and robustness to noise and deformation.
