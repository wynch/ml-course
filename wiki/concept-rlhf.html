RLHF

concept created 2026-05-06 updated 2026-05-06 rlhf alignment preference-learning reward-modeling

Three-stage pipeline that defined modern LLM post-training: (1) supervised fine-tuning on demonstrations, (2) train a reward model from human preference comparisons, (3) RL-fine-tune the LM against the reward model. Christiano et al. (2017) is the original; Ouyang et al. (2022, "InstructGPT") is the canonical LLM application.

Canonical sources

  • source-instructgpt-rlhf — Ouyang 2022: SFT → reward model → PPO. 1.3B InstructGPT outperforms 175B GPT-3 on human-rated quality. The paper that defined the post-ChatGPT alignment paradigm.
  • source-constitutional-ai — Bai 2022 (Anthropic): SL-CAI + RLAIF using a written constitution; reduces human red-teaming load by having the model self-critique against principles.
  • source-dpo-direct-preference-optimization — Rafailov 2023: closed-form derivation eliminates the explicit reward model and the RL phase. Now widely the default.

Wiki framing

The wiki has more coverage of RLHF alternatives than canonical RLHF — see concept-rlhf-alternatives for the alternatives. This concept page is the canonical baseline against which they're measured.

Tensions worth tracking

  1. Is RLHF actually needed? — source-distribution-sharpening-power-sampling argues the gains are inference-time amplification of latent capabilities; RL doesn't teach new behaviour, just sharpens distributions. source-intuitor-reasoning-without-rewards makes a compatible claim using model confidence as the only signal.
  2. Constitutional AI vs InstructGPT vs DPO — three answers to "how do we encode preferences":
    • InstructGPT: human comparisons → reward model → PPO
    • CAI: written constitution → AI self-critique → RLAIF
    • DPO: skip the explicit reward model entirely; classification loss directly on preferences
  3. Reward-modelling failure modes — reward hacking, length bias, sycophancy. DPO sidesteps some but inherits the underlying preference-data quality problem.
