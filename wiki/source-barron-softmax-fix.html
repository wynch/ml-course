A Fix for Softmax and Cross-Entropy

source created 2026-10-23 updated 2026-10-23 softmax cross-entropy floating-point grokking distillation adam weight-decay numerical-stability 2026

Authors: Jonathan T. Barron, Dan Gnanapragasam, Michael Tschannen; Google DeepMind Published: arXiv 2610.31642, v1 2026-10-22. HTML version and demo: https://jonbarron.info/softmaxfix/ Raw copy: raw/arxiv-2610-31642-a-fix-for-softmax-and-cross.md (full text from the authors' HTML version, captured 2026-10-23; figures not captured) Thread: raw/tweet-barron-softmax-fix.md (Barron on X, 2026-10-23, six posts; how the link arrived)

Every claim below cites the paper's raw file as R, with its section, or the thread as T, with its post number.

Summary

Softmax turns scores (logits) into probabilities; cross-entropy is the loss on them. Every framework first subtracts the largest logit so the exponentials cannot overflow (R, §1, Eq. 1). The paper says two rounding bugs survive that step, and gives a rewrite that removes both (R, Abstract).

Bug 1, Softmax Collapse. When the right class's logit leads the rest by a margin m, the sum in the softmax becomes 1 + e^(−m). Past a threshold the authors call the precision cliff, adding e^(−m) to 1.0 changes nothing: about 5.5 in bf16, 16.6 in fp32, 36.7 in fp64. Then the loss and the right class's gradient round to exactly zero, but the wrong classes' gradients do not, since they never pass through the "1 +". The gradients no longer sum to zero (R, §1, §2, Eq. 2–4, Figure 1a). The same holds for a standalone softmax (R, §2).

Fix 1. Never add 1 to e^(−m). Set the top logit's slot to an exact 0, compute the margin from the other logits alone, and use softplus. The right class's gradient then stays near −e^(−m) down to underflow, about m = 88 in fp32, and the gradients sum to zero (R, §2, Eq. 5–7). The paper prints a short JAX version. On logits [9, −9, −9], standard jax.nn and optax return a loss of 0.0; the fix returns 3.046e-8 (R, §2).

Bug 2, Softmax Cancellation. In distillation the target is the teacher's softmax. Standard code computes student and teacher probabilities apart and subtracts them; when the two sets of logits are close, the small difference is lost. Forward and backward passes also use slightly different formulas, so the gradient is not zero even when student equals teacher (R, §1, §3, Figure 1b).

Fix 2. Pass the teacher's logits to the loss and compute the difference in log space with expm1 and log1p. On a 1e-7 nudge in fp32, the standard gradient shrinks one entry by nearly 3× and flips the sign of another; the fix matches the exact fp64 gradient (R, §3, Eq. 9).

Key results

The grokking runs use small transformers and MLPs on modular arithmetic, full-batch AdamW, 3–5 seeds (R, §4, §6).

  • Slingshots are bug 1 meeting Adam. After memorising, margins grow. Once a batch sits past the cliff, gradients nearly vanish and Adam's running estimate of gradient size decays. When a few examples fall back below the cliff, their gradient returns and Adam divides it by a tiny number: a large step, a loss spike, and the cycle repeats. With the fix, the spikes go (R, §4, Figure 2).
  • Whether spikes help depends on the setup. With LayerNorm and no weight decay, each spike inflates the weights and acts as accidental learning-rate decay. With weight decay, spikes only hurt: 94 ± 5% against 100 ± 0% test accuracy on a + b mod 31. With neither, one spike is ruinous: on a² + b² mod 97, standard cross-entropy drops from 61% to 23% test accuracy around step 1,250, while the fix reaches 85% (R, §4, Figure 2).
  • Adam's ε and weight decay cap the margin. At rest, Adam's normalised step must balance weight decay. Once gradients fall below ε, the step shrinks with e^(−m), so the margin settles near m* ≈ log(1/ε) (R, §5, Eq. 10–11). The default ε = 1e-8 gives 18.4, just past the fp32 cliff of 16.6, which is why it causes spikes; ε ≥ 1e-7 gives at most 16.1 and no spikes (R, §5). Measured final margins with the fix: 17.9, 27.4 and 36.8 for ε = 1e-8, 1e-12, 1e-16 (R, §6, Table 1). Without weight decay, margins climb past log(1/ε) (R, §5, Figure 4b).
  • Fixed fp32 matches standard fp64. On a + b mod 31 at ε = 1e-8, the fix reaches 100.0 ± 0.1% in bf16 and 100.0 ± 0.0% in fp32 and fp64; standard cross-entropy gets 93.3 ± 7.4% (bf16) and 94.3 ± 5.4% (fp32). At ε = 1e-16 even fp64 standard cross-entropy fails at its cliff, 97.2 ± 3.2% (R, §6, Table 1, Figure 5). The same holds on four grokking tasks at ε = 1e-12 (R, §6, Figure 6).
  • Against earlier remedies. StableMax (Prieto et al.) lets margins grow to thousands and trails the fix at every ε: 97.3 ± 4.7% against 100.0 ± 0.0% in fp32 at ε = 1e-8. A zero-sum gradient projection (Liu et al., 2026) falls below 50% for ε ≤ 1e-12 (R, §6, Table 1).
  • Distillation from an identical teacher. A student starts as an exact copy of the teacher (a + b mod 53, temperature 4, Adam, ε = 1e-8). The true gradient is zero, so it should not move. Standard code leaves a leftover gradient of about 1e-17 in fp64, 1e-8 in fp32 and 1e-3 in bf16; wherever that beats ε, Adam turns it into full-size steps. In bf16 the student drifts and loses test accuracy; in fp32 the KL spikes, then floors near 1e-6. The fix keeps KL = 0 and 100% accuracy in all three (R, §7, Figure 7).

Caveats

  • The authors say the extreme margins and near-zero student–teacher gaps they test are "uncommon in standard large-scale training"; they offer the fix as a cheap drop-in, "without meaningful slowdown" (R, §8). No timing is reported, and no large-model result.
  • The thread's "all existing implementations", "textbooks" included (T, post 1), is wider than the paper, which names JAX, PyTorch and TensorFlow and tests JAX and optax (R, §1, §2).
  • All grokking and distillation results are on toy modular-arithmetic tasks.
  • Per Barron, Gemini Flash 3.7 first found and fixed the bug, Flash 3.8 ran most experiments, and Gemini 4 Argon drafted the paper and figures, which he then rewrote by hand (T, post 6). The paper's own text does not say this; it is the author's report, unchecked.

A possible local case

2026-10-23: Vincent's local experiment 03 trained a one-layer transformer on (a + b) mod 97 with full-batch AdamW (learning rate 1e-3, betas 0.9 and 0.98, weight decay 1.0, no LayerNorm) (compass/experiments/03-grokking/RESULTS.md, "Config"); ε and the number format are left at PyTorch's defaults, 1e-8 and float32 (compass/experiments/03-grokking/run.py). After grokking, train and test loss both "oscillate hard", "swinging over an order of magnitude from one eval to the next", which the run did not predict (same file, "Verdict vs pre-registration"). That is the setting where Barron et al. report slingshot spikes (R, §4–5). The link is untested: nobody logged the margin against the fp32 cliff of about 16.6, so the swings may have another cause.

  • source-grokking-softmax-collapse — Prieto et al. named Softmax Collapse; this paper fixes it at the source and compares against StableMax. Not a clash, 2026-10-23: both papers train with AdamW (raw/arxiv-2501-04697-grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical.md, §2.2). Prieto et al. guessed this paper's mechanism in their Appendix H: zero gradients from collapse let Adam's second moments decay, giving a slingshot. This paper confirms it and shows spikes in setups where Prieto et al. saw learning halt; without weight decay a spike can drop test accuracy from 61% to 23% (R, §4, Figure 2).
