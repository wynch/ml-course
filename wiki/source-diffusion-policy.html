Diffusion Policy

source created 2026-05-07 updated 2026-05-07 robotics imitation-learning diffusion behaviour-cloning 2023

Chi, Feng, Du, Xu, Cousineau, Burchfiel, Song — arXiv 2303.04137, RSS 2023 (Columbia / Toyota Research Institute / MIT)

Reformulates robot behaviour cloning as conditional diffusion over action sequences. Instead of predicting a single next action (regression) or sampling from a categorical action distribution, Diffusion Policy iteratively denoises a short action horizon (8–16 timesteps), conditioned on the current visual observation. The denoising prior absorbs the multimodality of human demonstrations — long the central failure mode of behaviour cloning.

Method

  • Input: short history of observations (RGB images + proprioception) encoded by a ResNet or ViT.
  • Output: a chunk of T future actions, denoised together. Two variants tested: CNN-based U-Net over the action sequence, and Transformer over action tokens. Both work; transformer scales better.
  • Receding-horizon execution: predict T actions, execute the first k (~4), re-plan. Like model-predictive control but with a diffusion prior instead of an explicit dynamics model.
  • Training: standard DDPM noise-prediction loss on demonstration action sequences.
  • 50–100 demonstrations per task suffice in many cases.

Key numbers

  • Benchmarked on 12 simulation tasks + 4 real-robot tasks (push-T, pour, cup-flip, etc.).
  • +46.9% average improvement over LSTM-GMM, IBC, and BCRNN baselines on the 12-task benchmark suite — a step-change for behaviour cloning, not an incremental gain.
  • Robust to demonstration multimodality (multiple valid ways to solve a task) — the empirical failure mode that crippled previous BC methods.
  • Inference: 10–100 denoising steps; ~10 Hz control achievable with DDIM.

Why it matters

Diffusion Policy is the small-model, task-specific imitation counterpart to large VLAs (source-rt-2, source-openvla, source-pi-zero). The two camps illustrate the field's split:

  • Big foundation VLAs: a single multi-billion-parameter model pretrained on internet + robot data, finetuned per embodiment. Generalist; cost-of-entry high.
  • Small per-task imitation (Diffusion Policy, source-aloha-act): collect tens to hundreds of demonstrations, train a focused policy, deploy. Specialist; works now.

What Diffusion Policy contributes intellectually:

  1. Generative models as priors over action sequences. The "predict denoised actions" framing imports everything diffusion has learned about modeling multimodal distributions (source-latent-diffusion-stable-diffusion, source-dit-diffusion-transformer) into robotics. Action sequences, like images, are high-dimensional and multimodal — exactly diffusion's home.
  2. Action chunking matters. Predicting a horizon (not one step) provides temporal smoothing, sidesteps idle-state issues, and gives the policy room to commit to a strategy. source-aloha-act reaches the same conclusion via transformer regression rather than diffusion.
  3. Behaviour cloning is competitive again. Before 2023, the field had largely concluded that pure BC was a dead end without RL or world models. Diffusion Policy reopened that question by showing the bottleneck was representational (Gaussian / unimodal heads), not fundamental.

Connects to concept-vla-robot-foundation-models as the "small, focused, demonstration-only" baseline that VLAs must beat at deployment to justify their cost. Also connects to concept-world-models negatively: Diffusion Policy works without an explicit dynamics model, suggesting the priors learned from demonstration are often sufficient for short-horizon manipulation. Pi-zero (source-pi-zero) explicitly inherits Diffusion Policy's action-chunking head, grafted onto a VLM backbone — a literal merger of the two paradigms.
