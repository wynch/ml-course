Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models (Chinchilla)

source created 2026-05-06 updated 2026-10-20 scaling-laws training compute foundational chinchilla 2022

Hoffmann, Borgeaud, Mensch, Buchatskaya, Cai, Rutherford, de Las Casas, Hendricks, Welbl, Clark et al. — arXiv 2203.15556, Mar 2022 (DeepMind) Full text: raw/arxiv-2203-15556-training-compute-optimal-large-language-models.md (v1, the only version, captured 2026-10-20), cited below by section.

Re-runs source-kaplan-scaling-laws with a wider sweep over token counts (not just model sizes) and reaches a strikingly different conclusion: at fixed compute, N and D should scale roughly 1:1. Most existing frontier LLMs were therefore severely under-trained on data. Demonstrates this with Chinchilla (70B, 1.4T tokens), which beats Gopher (280B, 300B tokens) on nearly every benchmark using the same compute budget.

Method

  • Fits scaling laws via three approaches (corrected 2026-10-19: the page had confused the loss exponents α, β with the allocation exponents a, b, and named the approaches wrongly):
    1. Fixed model sizes, varied training tokens — take the lowest loss at each FLOP count (§3.1).
    2. IsoFLOP profiles — for each fixed compute, sweep model sizes (§3.2).
    3. Parametric fit — L(N, D) = E + A/N^α + B/D^β, fitted α = 0.34, β = 0.28 (§3.3; Appendix D.2, Eq. 10).
  • All three give N_opt ∝ C^a and D_opt ∝ C^b with a ≈ b ≈ 0.5 (a = 0.50, 0.49, 0.46; Table 2), i.e. tokens and parameters should grow at roughly equal rates as compute increases.
  • Contradiction (2026-10-19): Besiroglu et al. (arXiv 2404.10102) refit approach 3 from data reconstructed from the paper's own figure. The reported estimates disagree with approaches 1 and 2 and have implausibly narrow confidence intervals; their own refit agrees with approaches 1 and 2 (abstract; not in raw).
  • Trains Chinchilla (70B params, 1.4T tokens) under this recipe; trains Gopher-clones for comparison.

Key numbers

  • Compute budget Chinchilla = compute budget Gopher (~5.76 × 10²³ FLOPs). (§3.4)
  • Chinchilla is 4× smaller but trained on 4.6× more tokens than Gopher. (The paper says "4 times more tokens"; 4.6 is 1.4T ÷ 300B, the wiki's arithmetic; §1, §4.1.)
  • Wins on MMLU (67.5% vs 60.0%), BIG-bench, RACE, HellaSwag, and most reading-comprehension/QA benchmarks. (§4.2; the abstract gives 67.5%, the MMLU table 67.6%)
  • Reduced size means lower inference cost — a structural win, not just a training-time one.
  • Implication: GPT-3 (175B / 300B tokens) was off-optimal by an order of magnitude in token count.

Why it matters

The scaling-law correction that quietly reshaped the open-weight era. Llama 1/2/3, Falcon, Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek — every model designed after April 2022 used some Chinchilla-style data-rich recipe.

Contradiction (2026-10-19), which the page's own Llama 3 line below already hints at: later open models (Llama, Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek) train on more tokens per parameter than Kaplan advised, and usually far more than Chinchilla-optimal, because inference cost dominates (Sardana et al., arXiv 2401.00448; not in raw). The result that bigger ≠ better at fixed compute moved the field from "stack more parameters" to "find more high-quality tokens," which led directly to the data-curation literature this wiki tracks: source-finephrase-synthetic-data-playbook, source-lacy-small-language-models, source-frontier-model-training.

Two threads worth holding together:

  • Scaling exponents are architecture-and-data-dependent. Kaplan and Chinchilla disagreed because their data-scaling sweeps were limited. Superseded (2026-10-20): Pearce and Song (arXiv 2406.12907) trace most of the gap to Kaplan counting non-embedding parameters at small scale (source-reconciling-kaplan-chinchilla; raw/arxiv-2406-12907-reconciling-kaplan-and-chinchilla-scaling-laws.md, §3, Table 1); Porian et al. (arXiv 2406.19146; not in raw) to last-layer compute, warmup and optimiser tuning (as summarised in the same file, §6). Contradiction (2026-10-20): this paper's suggestion that Kaplan's single cosine schedule per model biased the exponent upward does not hold in Pearce and Song's small runs: Chinchilla's scheme gives 0.49 against 0.46 for Kaplan's (same file, §4, Table 1). Later work — source-neural-scaling-laws-from-language-statistics — derives exponents from intrinsic statistics of the training distribution rather than fitting curves.
  • Chinchilla is itself probably superseded. The Llama 3 era trains at N tokens >> Chinchilla-optimal because inference cost dominates, and overtraining is cheaper than serving a bigger model. This is the modern axis concept-model-training tracks.

Later: when data runs out

Chinchilla assumes every training token is new. source-diffusion-beats-ar-data-constrained starts from the one-pass compute-optimal point and repeats the data: for left-to-right models a repeated token is worth about as much as a fresh one for about 4 epochs, for masked diffusion models for about 100 (raw/arxiv-2507-15857-diffusion-beats-autoregressive-in-data-constrained.md, §3.1, §3.2, Figure 5).
