Summary

Meta's launch post for Llama 4: two open-weight models, Scout and Maverick, and a preview of a teacher, Behemoth, still in training. They are Meta's first mixture-of-experts models and its first natively multimodal ones (R, opening).

Key claims

  • Mixture of experts. Maverick alternates dense and MoE layers; each token goes to the shared expert and to one of 128 routed experts (R, "Pre-training").
  • Early fusion. Text and vision tokens enter one backbone, pretrained together on unlabelled text, image and video; the vision encoder is based on MetaCLIP, trained beside a frozen Llama (R, "Pre-training"). Up to 48 images in pretraining, tested up to eight in post-training (R, "Post-training our new models").
  • Data and compute. Over 30 trillion tokens, more than double Llama 3's; 200 languages; FP8 training of Behemoth on 32K GPUs at 390 TFLOPs per GPU (R, "Pre-training").
  • MetaP, a method to set per-layer learning rates and initialisation scales that, Meta says, transfer across batch size, width, depth and token count (R, "Pre-training").
  • Long context. Scout claims 10 million tokens of input, though it was pretrained and post-trained at 256K. The "iRoPE" design interleaves attention layers with no position embedding among RoPE layers, and scales attention temperature at inference (R, "Post-training our new models").
  • Post-training. Light SFT, then online RL, then light DPO. Meta found that SFT and DPO "can over-constrain the model" and hurt RL; it dropped over 50% of SFT data judged easy (95% for Behemoth) and kept only medium-to-hard prompts during RL (R, "Post-training our new models"; "Pushing Llama to new sizes").
  • Distillation. Maverick was "codistilled" from Behemoth during pretraining, with a loss that weights soft and hard targets over training (R, "Pushing Llama to new sizes").
  • RL infrastructure. A fully asynchronous online RL framework gave "~10x" training efficiency over earlier generations (R, same section).
  • Benchmarks, as Meta states them: Scout beats Gemma 3, Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite and Mistral 3.1; Maverick beats GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 Flash and is comparable to DeepSeek v3 on reasoning and coding; an "experimental chat version" of Maverick scored 1417 Elo on LMArena; Behemoth beats GPT-4.5, Claude Sonnet 3.7 and Gemini 2.0 Pro on STEM benchmarks such as MATH-500 and GPQA Diamond (R, "Takeaways"; opening).
  • Political bias. Refusals on debated political and social topics fall from 7% (Llama 3.3) to under 2%; strong political lean at half Llama 3.3's rate, "comparable to Grok" (R, "Addressing bias in LLMs").

Caveats

  • A launch post, not a report. The benchmark tables are images not kept in raw; no table of scores can be checked here.
  • The LMArena score is for an "experimental chat version", not the released weights (R, "Takeaways").
  • The post is inconsistent about DeepSeek: "comparable results to the new DeepSeek v3" in the takeaways, "competitive with the much larger DeepSeek v3.1" in the body (R, "Takeaways"; "Post-training our new models").
  • 10M tokens of context rests on retrieval and code-likelihood tests the post describes but does not report in text.
  • Behemoth was not released; its claims cannot be checked.
