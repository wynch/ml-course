The Three Axes

guide created 2026-05-07 updated 2026-05-07 guide meta axes navigation thesis

This wiki isn't organised around topics. It's organised around three arguments about what modern AI is becoming. Each axis is a thesis someone is making about which framings will turn out to matter. If you're new, start here.

Axis 1 — Neural-Symbolic

The thesis: neural and symbolic are two parts of a single architecture, not opposing paradigms. The interesting work is happening at the seam — verifiers wrapped around neural proposers, libraries of programs that grow during training, decompilation of weights to source code, symbolic handles inside dense layers.

Anchor: source-neural-computer-metauto (Zhuge / MetaAuto) — the essay this whole axis is built around.

Synthesis: analysis-neural-symbolic-middle-point — the five axes of articulation.

Concept hubs: concept-neural-symbolic-bridge, concept-harness-as-symbolic-substrate, concept-mechanistic-interpretability (the readout side of the bridge).

Why it's here: if you believe LLMs are best understood as cooperating with symbolic substrates rather than replacing them, this is the axis you care about. The wiki's neural-symbolic coverage is the largest of the three.

Axis 2 — Predictive / Self-Supervised

The thesis: prediction-in-latent-space is the substrate of grounded intelligence. Vision SSL didn't just produce better features — it produced the right kind of features for prediction-then-planning. JEPA + world models is what happens when that programme is taken seriously beyond representation learning.

Position paper: source-lecun-path-autonomous-intelligence (LeCun 2022).

Synthesis: analysis-world-models-vs-vla — four answers to embodied intelligence, with the predictive position as one of them.

Concept hubs: concept-self-supervised-learning, concept-jepa, concept-world-models, concept-active-inference (the hand-tuned-generative-model counterpart).

Why it's here: if you believe the limits of LLM scaling will be addressed by what is being predicted, not just by more text, this is your axis. V-JEPA 2-AC's zero-shot Droid robot control is the strongest 2025 data point that this is more than representation learning.

Axis 3 — Foundational LLM canon

The thesis: most of the wiki's modern arguments cite a handful of foundational papers; you should know them. This isn't an argument so much as a base layer that the other two axes presuppose.

Founding papers: source-attention-is-all-you-need · source-gpt3-few-shot-learners · source-chinchilla-compute-optimal · source-chain-of-thought-foundations · source-instructgpt-rlhf · source-dpo-direct-preference-optimization · source-lora-low-rank-adaptation.

Synthesis pieces: analysis-emergence-vs-distribution-sharpening — pulls the canon's scaling/RL claims into the modern debate. analysis-self-distillation-as-universal-lever — argues a single mechanism connects DPO, retrofitting, R1, and DINO.

Concept hubs: concept-scaling-laws, concept-rlhf, concept-transformer-architecture, concept-mixture-of-experts, concept-test-time-compute.

Why it's here: because without it, the rest of the wiki is unreadable. Read these before anything else.

How the axes interact

The wiki has been getting more interesting where the axes cross:

  • Axis 1 ↔ Axis 2. source-engram-deepseek (an explicit symbolic table inside a dense LLM) is a neural-symbolic move that simultaneously is a prediction architecture innovation. source-vjepa-family's predictor module is, in some sense, a symbolic operator wrapped in neural form.
  • Axis 2 ↔ Axis 3. source-deepseek-r1 is canonical-LLM frontier work that nonetheless gives the predictive/SSL camp ammunition (see analysis-emergence-vs-distribution-sharpening).
  • Axis 1 ↔ Axis 3. concept-mechanistic-interpretability is the readout-side of Axis 1 but is mostly applied to Axis-3 models (Claude, GPT, Gemma). The interesting question is whether the same techniques transfer to Axis-2 models.

The three axes have not yet met deeply — most pages live cleanly inside one axis. The wiki's most interesting open questions are at the crossings.
