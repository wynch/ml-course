PixelRAG

source created 2026-07-04 updated 2026-07-04 retrieval rag multimodal vision-language-models embeddings

Authors: Yichuan Wang, Zhifei Li, Zirui Wang, Paul Teiletche, Lesheng Jin, Matei Zaharia, Joseph E. Gonzalez, Sewon Min (UC Berkeley — SkyLab, BAIR, Berkeley NLP) / Date: 2026-06-01 / arXiv: 2606.28344

Summary

PixelRAG is a retrieval-augmented-generation system that abandons HTML-to-text parsing entirely: it represents web pages in their native visual form and performs both retrieval and reading in pixel space. The motivating claim is that HTML-to-text conversion is a "self-inflicted bottleneck" — it discards layout, structure, tables, charts, and infographics that carry real signal. Instead PixelRAG renders each page to screenshot tiles, retrieves the relevant screenshots, and lets a vision-language model read them directly, with no intermediate text extraction.

The retriever is a VLM continued-pretrained on web-page screenshots: Qwen3-VL-Embedding, fine-tuned with LoRA + contrastive training, which the authors report becomes "a surprisingly strong visual retriever." The full pipeline is render (pixelshot) → chunk → embed → build index → serve (FastAPI). The shipped datastore holds ~30M screenshot images across Wikipedia (~8.28M pages in the hosted index), demonstrating the approach scales to production-size corpora.

Headline result: PixelRAG outperforms text-based RAG by up to +18.1% — notably including on text-only QA benchmarks, not just visually-rich ones — while achieving up to 3× token-cost reduction at lower resolutions. Benchmarks span NQ, SimpleQA, MMSearch, LiveVQA, and MoNaCo. The broader thesis: recent VLMs read web pages more accurately and token-efficiently than text-only pipelines, so pixels beat text as the retrieval substrate for the web.

Key Ideas

  • Pixel-native RAG. Retrieval and reading happen on screenshots; HTML parsing is eliminated from the loop.
  • VLM-as-retriever. Continued pretraining of Qwen3-VL-Embedding (LoRA, contrastive) on screenshots yields a strong visual embedding/retrieval model.
  • Beats text on text-only QA (+18.1%). Preserving layout/tables/structure helps even when the answer is textual.
  • Efficiency. Up to 3× token-cost reduction at lower resolutions.
  • Scale. ~30M-image datastore across Wikipedia — pixel-native search at web scale.
