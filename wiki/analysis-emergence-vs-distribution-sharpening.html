Emergence vs Distribution Sharpening

analysis created 2026-05-07 updated 2026-10-20 analysis scaling-laws emergence distribution-sharpening rlhf test-time-compute

A through-line that has emerged across four nominally distinct wiki threads (scaling debates, test-time compute, RLHF alternatives, reasoning frontier) all asking the same question: when a bigger or RL-trained model performs better, is it because new capability has been installed, or because latent capability has been amplified?

The claim being contested

The classical scaling story (e.g. source-kaplan-scaling-laws, source-chinchilla-compute-optimal) is that bigger pre-training installs capability. The RLHF story (source-instructgpt-rlhf) is that RL post-training installs alignment. The reasoning-scaling story (source-chain-of-thought-foundations + source-emergence-debate Wei half) is that qualitatively new reasoning appears at threshold scales. All three are versions of an "installation" hypothesis.

The challenge — which has hardened into a coherent counter-position over 2022–2026 — is that what looks like capability installation is often just distribution sharpening: the underlying model already knew the answer; the apparent gain comes from concentrating probability mass on the right region, either through metric choice, sampling, or selective amplification.

Four data points for the amplification hypothesis

1. Self-Consistency (Wang 2022)

Within source-chain-of-thought-foundations, the Self-Consistency half is the cleanest early demonstration. Sample N CoTs from the same model; majority-vote the answer; GSM8K jumps from 56.9% (CoT greedy) to 74.4%. No new training, no extra prompts, no scaling — just sampling diversity + selection. The +17.5 pp gain was already latent in the model's distribution, accessible only at temperature > 0.

The conceptual move: answer is the latent variable, chain is a noisy decoder, majority vote selects the modal answer. Reasoning lives in the distribution, not in any single trace.

2. The Emergence Debate

source-emergence-debate sharpens this further at the scaling-law level. Schaeffer's NeurIPS paper finds that >92% of claimed emergent abilities on BIG-Bench are scored with one of two discontinuous metrics, Multiple Choice Grade or Exact String Match (source-emergence-debate). Re-scored with continuous metrics (token edit distance, Brier score), the abilities it tested scale smoothly. Under Multiple Choice Grade or Exact String Match, capability looks discontinuous; under continuous scoring, it looks like smooth scaling all the way down. The metric was acting as a thresholding distribution-sharpener.

The same critique transfers to many reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval all use exact-match-flavoured scoring). Modern reasoning headlines should be read through this filter.

3. Distribution-Sharpening Power Sampling

source-distribution-sharpening-power-sampling is the cleanest formalisation. Train-free power sampling is reported to match or beat one-shot GRPO on maths, code and QA, at over 10× lower latency than MCMC power sampling (arXiv 2601.21590, abstract). Its framing: RL post-training is largely distribution sharpening of latent capability. The full text qualifies the abstract: GRPO trained on MATH still leads on MATH500 in all three models tested, by about 3 points; power sampling matches or beats it out of domain, on HumanEval for the two Qwen models and on GPQA for two of three (raw/arxiv-2601-21590-scalable-power-sampling-unlocking-efficient-training.md, Table 1). It also keeps more of the base model's diversity than GRPO: duplicate answers over 8 samples 62.5% against 83.3% (same file, §4.3). So sampling recovers most, not all, of GRPO's in-domain gain.

Contradiction (partial), 2026-10-19: arXiv 2608.14420 ("More Correct Mass, Worse Answers") reports that global power sampling can lower self-consistency accuracy by up to 18.5 points (abstract; not in raw).

If true, this collapses concept-rlhf / concept-rlhf-alternatives / concept-test-time-compute into a single axis: how do you sharpen the latent distribution? Training (RL), constitution-based critique (CAI), preference optimisation (DPO), sampling (Self-Consistency, ToT), search (RAP, LATS), or verifier-guided inference (o1).

4. DeepSeek-R1's R1-Zero variant

source-deepseek-r1 adds a hard empirical data point on the other side: pure RL on the base model (no SFT cold-start) elicits o1-style reasoning. R1-Zero is the existence proof that base models contain the reasoning latent capability; RL surfaces it but doesn't install it.

This is consistent with distribution sharpening but more radical: not even SFT is needed to get the latent behaviour out.

Why these four belong together

Each comes from a different sub-literature (source-chain-of-thought-foundations from prompting, source-emergence-debate from scaling laws, source-distribution-sharpening-power-sampling from sampling theory, source-deepseek-r1 from open-weight LLM engineering) but they make the same epistemological move: the distinction between "model has this capability" and "model can be made to express this capability" is finer than the field treated it for the first decade of LLM scaling.

Implications across the wiki

  • For concept-scaling-laws: the question shifts from "what does scale add?" to "what does scale make accessible?" Continuous-metric eval becomes the methodological baseline.
  • For concept-test-time-compute: inference-time methods aren't a poor substitute for training; they're an equivalent class of distribution-sharpening operators. The cost frontier between train-time and test-time compute is genuinely open.
  • For concept-rlhf / concept-rlhf-alternatives: the SFT-first orthodoxy is on weaker ground than it appeared. DPO (source-dpo-direct-preference-optimization) is a tighter version of preference-amplification; R1-Zero is a tighter version still.
  • For concept-llm-reasoning: the reasoning-vs-pattern-matching debate is reframed. Even if models are just pattern-matching, the patterns they can be made to express via good distribution sharpening cover most behaviours the field calls "reasoning." The interesting question becomes: what's NOT in the latent distribution that bigger pretraining or genuinely-new RL would add?

What this analysis does NOT settle

  • The ceiling of amplification. Can sharpening reach arbitrarily-far-from-training-distribution capabilities, or only those latent within distribution? See source-llm-reasoning-skeptics (LLMs-can't-jump argument: distribution-bound by construction).
  • A training-side data point (2026). source-frontier-learning-edge-of-capability: GRPO runs that keep generating new problems near the model's edge reach 72.6% on Hard and 20.7% on Extra-hard Dice levels, where every standard baseline (fixed pools and fresh random levels) stays under 5.5% (raw/arxiv-2609-35426-frontier-learning-training-llm-reasoners-at.md, §5.1). The wiki's reading: what RL can install may depend on the supply of problems it trains on. The paper does not measure the base model's pass@k, so it does not test the latent-capability claim directly.
  • Base-model pass@k (2025). source-rl-reasoning-boundary-yue (Yue et al., arXiv 2504.13837) supplies the test this page lacked: RLVR models win at pass@1, base models win at large k, across maths, code and visual reasoning, several model families and six RL algorithms (raw/arxiv-2504-13837-does-reinforcement-learning-really-incentivize-reasoning.md, §3, §4.3). On AIME24 at k = 1024, 13.3% of problems are solved by the base model only and none by the RL model only (same file, Table 2). This supports sharpening for RL runs of the length tested; the authors leave longer RL open (same file, §4.4, §7). Distillation from a stronger model, by contrast, lifts pass@k at every k (same file, §4.2). Contradiction (open), 2026-10-19: source-prorl (Liu et al., arXiv 2505.24864) trains a 1.5B model with RL for over 2,000 steps across five task kinds and reports tasks the starting model never solves in 256 samples that the trained model does, such as the unseen puzzle boxnet (raw/arxiv-2505-24864-prorl-prolonged-reinforcement-learning-expands-reasoning.md, §4.3, Figure 5). Its start, though, is an already distilled model, and on maths it sees the same narrowing Yue et al. report (same file, §2.3.1, §4.2). Read together: short RL on maths sharpens; long RL on tasks the model starts weak at can widen reach.
  • Spurious rewards (2025). source-spurious-rewards (Shao et al., arXiv 2506.10947): random rewards give Qwen2.5-Math-7B +21.4 points on MATH-500 against +29.1 for true rewards; rewarding a wrong answer gives +24.1 (raw/arxiv-2506-10947-spurious-rewards-rethinking-training-signals-in.md, §2.2, Figure 1). The gain comes from GRPO's clipping, which pushes up what the model already prefers; in Qwen-Math that is reasoning in Python it cannot run (same file, §4, §5.2). The same rewards do little or harm on Llama and OLMo (same file, §3). For this page: the strongest sharpening evidence yet, but for one model family. Any small-RL gain on Qwen-Math needs a random-reward control before it counts as learning.
  • What source-arc-agi-3 tells us. <1% for frontier models at launch (2026-03-25; raw/web-2026-arcprize-arc-agi-3-launch.md, opening: 0.26% on launch day, later restated as 0.51%) on a benchmark designed for novel interactive reasoning is the strongest current counter-evidence to the strong amplification position. If sharpening could reach there, the curve would be smoother.
  • Train-time vs test-time compute optimum. source-snell-test-time-compute-scaling argues test-time can be more compute-efficient for some problems. Mapping which problems is still open.

Reading order

For someone coming to this debate fresh:

  1. source-chain-of-thought-foundations (CoT + Self-Consistency, the original seed)
  2. source-emergence-debate (Wei vs Schaeffer — the scaling-law fight)
  3. source-distribution-sharpening-power-sampling (the formalised general claim)
  4. source-deepseek-r1 (the cleanest empirical test of the strong amplification position)
  5. source-rl-reasoning-boundary-yue (pass@k at large k: the measured version of that test)
  6. source-arc-agi-3 (the strongest counter-evidence)
