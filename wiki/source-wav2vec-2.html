Wav2Vec 2.0 — Contrastive SSL on Raw Audio

source created 2026-05-06 updated 2026-05-06 speech audio self-supervised-learning contrastive-learning wav2vec meta-fair

Baevski, Zhou, Mohamed, Auli (Facebook AI Research) — arXiv 2006.11477, NeurIPS 2020

The reference paper for contrastive self-supervised learning on speech. Wav2Vec 2.0 establishes that you can pretrain a powerful speech encoder from unlabelled audio alone — masked latent prediction with a contrastive loss over a learned discrete codebook — and then fine-tune with very little labelled data to match or beat fully supervised systems. Sets the foundation for a generation of speech foundation models (HuBERT, WavLM, data2vec, MMS).

Architecture

Three components stacked on raw 16 kHz waveform:

  1. Convolutional feature encoder — strided 1D convs map raw audio to ~50 Hz latent vectors z_t.
  2. Quantization module — Gumbel-softmax with product quantization over two codebooks of 320 entries each, yielding ~100k discrete codes. The codebook is learned end-to-end and provides the targets for the contrastive loss.
  3. Transformer context network — operates on masked latents; outputs context vectors c_t.

SSL objective

A random subset of latents is masked. For each masked timestep, the Transformer's output c_t must identify the true quantized target q_t from a pool of 100 distractors sampled from other masked positions in the same utterance — a contrastive InfoNCE loss. A diversity term encourages even codebook usage to prevent collapse to a few codes.

Data and results

  • Pretraining on 53k hours of unlabelled LibriVox audio (LV-60k).
  • Fine-tuning on tiny labelled subsets:
    • 10 minutes of labelled audio + LM → 4.8/8.2 WER on LibriSpeech clean/other.
    • 1 hour → outperforms prior SOTA trained on 100 hours (a 100× labelled-data reduction at parity).
    • Full LibriSpeech → 1.8/3.3 WER, SOTA at the time.

Significance

Wav2Vec 2.0 is the speech analogue of the SSL revolution that swept vision a year earlier with SimCLR and MoCo. The discrete codebook target is the key trick — it gives the contrastive loss a stable, well-defined positive class without the representation-collapse failure modes of raw-vector contrast. Successors generalise the targets (MAE-style reconstruction in HuBERT, latent-prediction in data2vec).

The paper is also the foundational reference behind which Whisper argues against in 2022: Whisper's 680k-hour weak-supervision recipe out-scales Wav2Vec-style SSL on most downstream benchmarks. The two sit as the canonical "SSL vs. paired-data scaling" axis for speech.
