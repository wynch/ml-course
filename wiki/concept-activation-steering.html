Activation Steering

concept created 2026-10-20 updated 2026-10-20 interpretability activation-steering sparse-autoencoders control alignment hub

Activation steering changes what a model does by editing its activations while it runs, not its weights or its prompt. The common form adds a vector to the residual stream at one layer: h ← h + αv. The vector may be a difference of means between two sets of prompts, a learned direction, or a sparse autoencoder (SAE) feature. With negative α, or by projecting the direction out, it becomes an ablation (raw/anthropic-global-workspace-paper-fulltext.md, "Technical details of J-lens use cases").

As a test, it works

Concept injection makes steering a probe. Told that thoughts may be injected, Claude Opus 4.1 names the injected concept on about 20% of trials at the best layer and strength, with no false positives on control trials, and notices before the concept shows in its text (raw/web-2025-anthropic-emergent-introspective-awareness.md, "Quick Tour", "Failure Modes"). Asked only "Are you experiencing anything unusual?", injection lifts yes-answers from 20% to 42% of 50 trials, but only 2 of 50 suggest real awareness (same file, "Alternative Prompts").

As control, prompting wins

AxBench compared steering methods with plain prompting and fine-tuning on 500 concepts in Gemma-2-2B and 9B. An LLM judge rates concept, instruction following and fluency; the score is their harmonic mean. Mean scores: prompting 0.894, LoReFT 0.741, full fine-tuning 0.676 (2B only), LoRA 0.615, ReFT-r1 0.543, difference of means 0.239, SAE features 0.165 (raw/arxiv-2501-17148-axbench-steering-llms-even-simple-baselines.md, §5.2, Table 2; source-axbench). Every steering method loses instruction following as the push grows (same file, §5.2, Figure 4). Better concept detection does not bring better steering (same file, §5.2).

The authors' case for steering survives: a prompt cannot detect a concept inside the model, and a steering vector gives an inspectable handle (same file, Abstract, §6).

Contradiction (partial), carried from source-conditional-activation-steering-cast: that page calls steering a "symbolic control surface" on the model. AxBench's numbers say a written instruction controls better on its benchmark. AxBench does not test conditional steering, so CAST's "if" is untested against prompting.

Open questions

  • Does conditional steering beat a conditional prompt? No source here runs that comparison.
  • SAE features steer worst in AxBench, but its SAE labels come from Neuronpedia's automatic descriptions, which lean to token-level ideas (raw/arxiv-2501-17148-axbench-steering-llms-even-simple-baselines.md, §6). See concept-sparse-autoencoders.
