Polysemanticity

concept created 2026-10-17 updated 2026-10-17 mechanistic-interpretability polysemanticity neurons features stub

Stub. A neuron is polysemantic when it seems to respond to several unrelated things, or when no one can say what it does. Its opposite is a monosemantic neuron, with one readable job. Polysemanticity is what people observe; concept-superposition is one proposed cause. This wiki keeps the two on separate pages because the newest source argues they come apart.

Causes on offer

The Goodfire essay lists five reasons a neuron may look polysemantic, and says "Polysemanticity is often more a claim about the observer than the neuron" (R, "Superposition of the Gaps"):

  1. Bad tools.
  2. Bad explanations built from those tools.
  3. Bad learning: weak architecture, data or training.
  4. Recursive generalization: a neuron grows along related branches, as "iy" → "diy" → "do it yourself" (R, "Recursive Generalization").
  5. Superposition: unrelated things packed together because they seldom collide.

It puts superposition last, as "the most radical and the least testable" (R, "Superposition of the Gaps").

How it changed

  • From Feature Visualization (2017) to Zoom In (2020), the cats-and-cars neuron was cited as evidence that neurons may not be the right unit of study (R, "Cars and Animals").
  • source-anthropic-mi-foundations tied polysemanticity to superposition; source-sae-monosemanticity-2023 answered with dictionaries that turn polysemantic neurons into monosemantic features.
  • source-goodfire-already-clean (2026) reports ~90% of Qwen3-32B neuron poles with one main job and ~2–4% not yet understood (R, "The Short Version"), and treats "a neuron that means two or three things as one neuron with a few grouped meanings" (R, "Cars and Animals"). It doubts the word itself: "nobody asks whether it's mono-organic" of a kidney (R, App. "A Note on Features and Definitions").

Contradiction: source-sae-monosemanticity-2023 calls the move "from polysemantic neurons to monosemantic features" "overdue and theoretically forced". source-goodfire-already-clean reads the same polysemantic neurons as mostly misread or weakly trained, and finds the neurons of modern models mostly readable as they are (R, "The Short Version"; "Slouching Towards Monosemanticity").
