SSL Contrastive Foundations — SimCLR, MoCo, BYOL

source created 2026-05-07 updated 2026-05-07 self-supervised-learning contrastive-learning vision representation-learning foundational

The 2019–2020 trilogy that established self-supervised vision as a foundation-model paradigm. SimCLR and MoCo are the two contrastive recipes that closed the gap to supervised pretraining; BYOL is the surprise non-contrastive extension that opened a parallel design space. All three are universally cited together as the founding triple — read as a unit.

SimCLR — Chen, Kornblith, Norouzi, Hinton (Google Brain, Feb 2020)

arXiv: 2002.05709.

Stripped away pretext-task scaffolding to show that a tight pipeline of (1) heavy augmentation, (2) a non-linear projection head, and (3) the NT-Xent contrastive loss with very large batches gets self-supervised vision within striking distance of supervised pretraining.

Method:

  • Two stochastic augmentations (random crop + colour jitter are load-bearing).
  • Encoder f (ResNet) → 2048-d representation h.
  • Projection head g (MLP) → 128-d z used for contrastive loss; representations h (not z) used downstream.
  • NT-Xent: for a positive pair (i,j), maximise cosine similarity of z_i and z_j against 2N-2 in-batch negatives, scaled by temperature τ.

Key numbers: ResNet-50 + batches of 4096 + 1000 epochs → ImageNet linear-eval 76.5%, matching supervised ResNet-50.

Distinctive contributions: the projection head matters a lot — discarding it before downstream use gives a large boost. Augmentation composition is the dominant lever; colour distortion + crop interaction is critical. Established large batch size as a contrastive lever — and motivated MoCo's queue/momentum solution.

MoCo — He, Fan, Wu, Xie, Girshick (FAIR, Nov 2019)

arXiv: 1911.05722.

Reframed contrastive SSL as dictionary lookup with a queue and a momentum-updated key encoder — decoupling the number of negatives from batch size, and giving the first SSL features that beat supervised ImageNet pretraining on multiple downstream detection/segmentation tasks.

Method:

  • Maintain a FIFO queue of past mini-batch keys as negatives — effective dictionary size (e.g. 65,536) independent of GPU batch.
  • Queries from an online encoder (gradient-updated); keys from a slow-moving momentum encoder updated as θ_k ← m·θ_k + (1−m)·θ_q with m=0.999.
  • Large but slowly-drifting key bank keeps representations consistent enough for InfoNCE to be a clean signal.
  • v2 (Mar 2020) adds an MLP projection head and stronger augmentation (lessons from SimCLR) — closes most of the gap to SimCLR with a fraction of the batch size.

Distinctive contributions:

  • First SSL representation to transfer better than supervised ImageNet pretraining on PASCAL VOC / COCO detection — the moment the field believed SSL was production-ready.
  • The momentum-encoder pattern became a foundational primitive — reused in BYOL and downstream in DINO, iBOT.

BYOL — Grill et al. (DeepMind, June 2020)

arXiv: 2006.07733. 14 authors, lead by Grill.

The first SSL method to match contrastive performance without any negatives, using only an asymmetric two-network setup with a predictor head and an EMA target encoder. Violated the conventional wisdom that contrastive negatives were essential to prevent collapse — and reset the SSL design space overnight.

Method:

  • Online network: encoder + projector + predictor (f_θ → g_θ → q_θ).
  • Target network: encoder + projector, no predictor (f_ξ → g_ξ); ξ ← τ·ξ + (1−τ)·θ. No gradient flows here.
  • Online predictor regresses the (stop-gradient) target projection of the other view. Loss: 2 − 2·⟨q_θ(z_θ), sg(z_ξ)⟩ / (‖q_θ(z_θ)‖·‖z_ξ‖), symmetrised by swapping views.
  • The combination of (predictor asymmetry + EMA stop-gradient target + initialisation variance + batch-norm dynamics) is what experimentally avoids trivial collapse to a constant.

Key numbers: ResNet-50 → 74.3% ImageNet linear-eval (~79.6% with a wider variant), competitive with SimCLR without any in-batch negatives.

Distinctive contributions:

  • Decoupled SSL from negative sampling, opening the non-contrastive branch.
  • Made the predictor + stop-gradient asymmetry a permanent fixture of the SSL design vocabulary; direct ancestor of DINO, iBOT, and the predictor used in I-JEPA. Subsequent analyses (and SimSiam) showed the stop-gradient + predictor is the load-bearing ingredient — EMA helps but isn't strictly required.

Why this trio matters

  • SimCLR + MoCo showed contrastive learning + ViT/ResNet could match supervised pretraining at scale, with two engineering paths (large batch vs queue) that became templates.
  • BYOL broke the negatives-required orthodoxy and created the asymmetric self-distillation family that downstream produced source-dino-lineage, source-masked-image-modeling (iBOT side), and the concept-jepa predictor design.
  • Together they're the canonical 2019–2020 SSL stack — every later vision foundation model can be traced back to one of these three recipes (or to the source-ssl-decorrelation alternative path).
