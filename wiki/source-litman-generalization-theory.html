A Theory of Generalization in Deep Learning

source created 2026-05-12 updated 2026-05-12 generalization ntk grokking signal-noise-decomposition deep-learning-theory optimization 2026

Litman & Guo — arXiv 2605.01172, May 2026

A unified theoretical account that uses the neural tangent kernel (NTK) to partition output space into signal and noise channels, then traces how SGD's stochasticity routes information differently through each. The framework claims to explain — under one mechanism — benign overfitting, double descent, implicit bias, and grokking. The headline practical artefact is an SNR preconditioner on Adam that accelerates grokking ~5×.

Core framework

  • The NTK has a spectrum: large eigenvalues span signal directions (correlated structure across the population); small eigenvalues span noise dimensions (idiosyncratic memorization).
  • In signal directions, error dissipates quickly under gradient flow.
  • In noise dimensions, the kernel's small eigenvalues retain residual error that is invisible to test data — explaining how training loss can keep falling while test loss plateaus or improves later (grokking).

The SGD claim

Minibatch SGD's stochastic gradient noise interacts with this partition:

  • Coherent population signal accumulates via fast linear drift along signal directions.
  • Idiosyncratic memorization is suppressed into a slow, diffusive random walk in noise dimensions.

This is the mechanism behind the unified account. The same dynamic produces benign overfitting (memorization happens, but stays in noise channels), double descent (signal/noise rebalances as capacity grows), implicit bias (drift selects low-norm signal solutions), and grokking (delayed projection onto signal channels).

The feature-learning extension

A standard objection to NTK theories: real networks leave the lazy/kernel regime — their kernel changes by O(1) in operator norm during training. The paper claims the signal/noise generalization story survives the full feature-learning regime, not just the linearization.

Practical artefact: SNR preconditioner

Two derived results:

  • Exact population-risk objective from a single training run, no validation data. Estimates noise level in the signal channel directly from training dynamics. This is the strongest claim if it holds.
  • SNR preconditioner on Adam: weights updates by the signal/noise ratio per direction. Reportedly 5× faster grokking on standard setups.

Why it matters

  • Unifies four phenomena under one mechanism ([benign overfitting / double descent / implicit bias / grokking]). Each previously had its own theoretical lens; this paper claims they're all signal/noise routing under SGD.
  • The validation-free risk estimate, if it generalises beyond toy setups, would change how generalization is measured during training.
  • Provides a constructive explanation of grokking — not just "phase transition happens" but a mechanism (delayed noise-to-signal projection) plus an intervention (SNR preconditioning) that accelerates it.
