Softmax Numerics

concept created 2026-10-23 updated 2026-10-23 softmax cross-entropy floating-point numerical-stability adam grokking distillation

Softmax turns a list of scores (logits) into probabilities that sum to 1; cross-entropy is the loss on them. On paper the gradient of cross-entropy with respect to the logits is simply q − p, predicted minus target, and its entries sum to zero (raw/arxiv-2610-31642-a-fix-for-softmax-and-cross.md, §1). In floating point, rounding can break that. This page collects what the wiki's sources say about where and why.

The standard trick and what it leaves

Computers store these numbers in floating point. bf16, fp32 and fp64 are 16-, 32- and 64-bit formats; more bits give finer steps between neighbouring numbers. In fp32, e^z is larger than the biggest number the format can hold once z passes about 88, and it becomes infinity. So every framework subtracts the largest logit first, which leaves the top logit at 0 and all others negative (raw/arxiv-2610-31642-a-fix-for-softmax-and-cross.md, §1, Eq. 1). Two rounding faults remain.

Both depend on the margin m: the top logit minus the log-sum-exp of the other logits, roughly how far the top choice leads (same file, Eq. 2). After the shift, the softmax's sum is 1 + e^(−m). Floating point has fine steps near 0, down to about 1e-38 in fp32, but coarse ones near 1: anything under about 1e-7 added to 1.0 is lost (same file, §1). So past a margin the authors call the precision cliff, about 5.5 in bf16, 16.6 in fp32 and 36.7 in fp64, the sum rounds to exactly 1 (same file, §1–2, Figure 1a).

Why an optimiser cares

Adam rescales each gradient to about size 1 unless it is below ε, so even tiny gradients give full steps (raw/arxiv-2610-31642-a-fix-for-softmax-and-cross.md, §4–5, Eq. 10). That turns both faults into real damage:

  • Collapse → "slingshot" spikes in grokking: gradients vanish past the cliff, Adam's running estimate of their size decays, then returning gradients produce oversized steps (same file, §4).
  • Cancellation → drift in distillation: a student that starts equal to its teacher walks away in bf16 (same file, §7).

Weight decay and ε together cap how far the margin grows. Once the gradient falls below ε, Adam's step shrinks with it, as e^(−m); weight decay keeps pulling the weights back; the margin stops where the two balance, near log(1/ε): 18.4 for the default ε = 1e-8, just past the fp32 cliff (same file, §5, Eq. 10–11). Prieto et al. saw the other side: an ε too large can halt learning at very low losses (raw/arxiv-2501-04697-grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical.md, Appendix I).

Open questions

  • Does any of this matter at scale? Barron et al. call the extreme margins "uncommon in standard large-scale training" (§8); Prieto et al.'s remedies gave no clear gain on ResNet18 or GPT-2 small (Appendix G).
  • A grokking run with full-batch AdamW, default ε = 1e-8, weight decay and standard fp32 cross-entropy is the setting where Barron et al. report slingshot spikes (§4–5). Any loss swings after grokking in such a run can be checked by logging the margin against 16.6.
  • 2026-10-23: one such run exists. Vincent's local experiment 03 trained a one-layer transformer on (a + b) mod 97 with full-batch AdamW (learning rate 1e-3, betas 0.9 and 0.98, weight decay 1.0, no LayerNorm) (compass/experiments/03-grokking/RESULTS.md, "Config"); ε and the number format are left at PyTorch's defaults, 1e-8 and float32 (compass/experiments/03-grokking/run.py). After grokking, train and test loss both "oscillate hard", "swinging over an order of magnitude from one eval to the next", which the run did not predict (same file, "Verdict vs pre-registration"). That is the setting where Barron et al. report slingshot spikes (raw/arxiv-2610-31642-a-fix-for-softmax-and-cross.md, §4–5). The link is untested: nobody logged the margin against the fp32 cliff of about 16.6, so the swings may have another cause.
