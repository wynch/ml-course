CodeLeWM

Author: abdel (@AbdelStark) — Head of Applied AI & Verifiable Intelligence, StarkWare (repo forked from lucas-maes/le-wm) / Date: 2026-06-10 / Type: open-source study + write-up

Summary

CodeLeWM ports the JEPA world-model idea out of vision and into source code. It treats a code edit as a state-action-state transition — CodeState_before + EditAction → latent(CodeState_after) — and trains a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture to predict the after-state latent from the before-state and action, without generating a single token or executing untrusted code in the scoring path. The intended payoff is a "safe, cheap, non-hallucinating ranker": candidate edits are scored by transition energy, the distance between the predicted and observed after-state latents. This is part of the author's broader bet that the JEPA paradigm (LeCun's "predict in latent space, don't reconstruct pixels/tokens") travels to any domain with a before-state, action, and after-state — cells, genomes, code.

The central finding is about substrate. The first attempt used commit messages as the action; it barely worked, the representation partially collapsed, and it lost to a trivial baseline that ignored the action entirely — commit text just isn't discriminative enough to describe what an edit does. Switching the state substrate to deterministic execution behavior fixed everything: collapse disappeared, diagnostics became clean, and the model perfectly separated real transitions from mutated/broken ones (mutation-surprise AUC 1.0). The generalizable lesson: in symbolic domains, JEPA "lives or dies on whether your latent substrate carries real, low-noise signal about the transition."

The study is notable for its claim-gating methodology — acceptance criteria hardcoded before any experiment ran, to prevent post-hoc storytelling. Honest negative/mixed result: healthy internal diagnostics are not downstream usefulness. Only one narrow, real win (a mutation-distractor reranking slice); the broad "general downstream improvement" claim stays closed because the other benchmark was saturated (model and controls all at ceiling, no headroom). Supported claims are limited to "reproducible code-edit world-model harness" and "negative action-use evidence."

Key Ideas

  • JEPA for code. Predict the after-edit latent from (before-state, edit-action); no token generation, no untrusted execution in scoring — a non-hallucinating edit ranker.
  • Substrate is everything. Commit-message actions → collapse and loss to a trivial baseline; deterministic-execution-behavior state → clean diagnostics, mutation-surprise AUC 1.0.
  • Claim-gating. Acceptance criteria fixed a priori; negative results (action-use) reported honestly; broad downstream claim explicitly not made.
  • Diagnostics ≠ usefulness. Beautiful internal training health can still yield no reranking lift.
  • Cross-domain map. One data point on where world-model thinking transfers (and breaks) beyond vision — cells and genomes flagged as next.
