DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report

source created 2026-05-06 updated 2026-09-26 model-training moe sparsity fp8 deepseek technical-report frontier-model

Authors: DeepSeek-AI (Liang Wenfeng et al.) Date: December 2024 (arXiv 2412.19437)

Summary

The technical report for DeepSeek-V3, a 671B-total / 37B-active sparse Mixture-of-Experts language model. Trained on 14.8T tokens using 2.788M H800 GPU-hours — roughly an order of magnitude cheaper than comparable Western frontier models, which made it the canonical reference for "efficient frontier training" through 2025.

Key Claims

Architecture

  • 671B total / 37B active parameters per token; DeepSeekMoE block with many fine-grained experts and a small set of shared experts.
  • Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) carried over from V2: low-rank KV compression that shrinks the KV cache without quality loss.
  • Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) auxiliary objective: predict the next two tokens, then drop the auxiliary head at inference. Improves data efficiency and enables speculative decoding.
  • Auxiliary-loss-free load balancing: per-expert bias terms updated by routing statistics replace the usual auxiliary balance loss. Avoids the gradient interference of auxiliary losses while keeping experts roughly balanced.

Training

  • 14.8T tokens, multilingual + heavy code/math weighting in late stages.
  • FP8 mixed-precision training end-to-end — one of the first credible demonstrations at this scale. Required custom block-wise scaling and accumulation tricks to keep activations representable.
  • 2.788M H800 GPU-hours total, with no irrecoverable loss spikes or rollbacks reported. Roughly $5–6M training cost at standard rental rates — anomalously cheap.
  • Long-context extension to 128k via two-stage YaRN-style stretching.

Post-training

  • SFT + RL stage; the RL recipe is the precursor to what becomes source-deepseek-r1.

Why it matters

  • The proof point that sparse MoE + FP8 + algorithmic discipline can reach near-frontier quality at a fraction of typical training cost. Reframed the cost narrative around frontier models for the entire industry.
  • The auxiliary-loss-free balancing trick has been adopted broadly in subsequent MoE work; it sidesteps the perennial complaint that load-balance auxiliary losses corrupt the main objective.
  • Pairs naturally with source-engram-deepseek as DeepSeek's second sparsity primitive — MoE in V3, conditional N-gram memory in Engram, both explored in parallel.
