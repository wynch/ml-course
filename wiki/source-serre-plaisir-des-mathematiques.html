Summary

Serre does not talk about the pleasure of finding things. He talks about the pleasure of watching mathematics advance, from about 1950 on, in his own view (R, 0:07). He groups it under three heads: dictionaries between fields, counterexamples, and connections between branches. He ends with a fairy tale he told when Deligne received an honorary doctorate.

Slips and doubts

  • He recalls "7" smooth structures on the 7-sphere, for their classification (R, 20:01). The classification (Kervaire-Milnor, 1963) gives 28 oriented classes; seven is what Milnor's first paper (1956) could tell apart. The words are his, not a caption error ("sept" does not sound like "vingt-huit"), and he hedges twice ("sauf erreur", "si je me souviens bien").
  • He does not say "definite" when stating Donaldson's theorem: only "sums of plus or minus one" (R, 20:01). Donaldson's diagonal result is for definite forms; read loosely (any mix of +1 and -1) the statement would rule out the K3 surface. Read as "all +1 or all -1" it is exact. Either way his conclusion holds, since his example, E8, is definite.
