Retrieval-Augmented Generation

concept created 2026-10-20 updated 2026-10-20 retrieval rag embeddings memory late-interaction hub

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG): before or while it writes, a language model fetches passages from a store outside its weights and reads them. The store can change without retraining, and the passages can be cited. source-bundle-retrieval-and-rag holds the lineage; this page holds the ideas and the one limit the wiki has found.

Four ways to do it

WayHowExample
Retrieve, then promptembed the query, find the nearest passages, paste them into the promptDPR's two encoders (arXiv 2004.04906; not in raw; source-dpr-dense-passage-retrieval)
Late interactionkeep one vector per token; score a passage by summing, over query tokens, the best match among its tokensColBERT (raw/arxiv-2004-12832-colbert-efficient-and-effective-passage-search.md, §3.3; source-colbert)
Retrieval inside the modela layer attends to neighbours fetched for the previous chunkRETRO, with a 2T-token store (raw/arxiv-2112-04426-improving-language-models-by-retrieving-from.md, §2.2–§2.4; source-retro-deepmind)
The model decidesspecial tokens say whether to retrieve, whether a passage is relevant and whether the answer is supportedSelf-RAG (raw/arxiv-2310-11511-self-rag-learning-to-retrieve-generate.md, §3.1, Table 1; source-self-rag)

Two newer variants: BGE-M3 serves dense, sparse and multi-vector retrieval from one model (raw/arxiv-2402-03216-m3-embedding-multi-linguality-multi-functionality.md, Abstract, §3.2; source-bge-m3-embeddings); PixelRAG retrieves web pages as screenshots and reads them with a vision-language model, reporting up to +18.1% over text RAG (arXiv 2606.28344, as the authors' post gives it in raw/tweet-pixelrag.md; source-pixelrag).

The single-vector limit

Contradiction (open), carried from source-bundle-retrieval-and-rag: Weller et al. prove that the number of distinct top-k sets one embedding per document can return is bounded by the embedding's dimension, and their LIMIT dataset defeats strong single-vector models while multi-vector models do better (arXiv 2508.21038; not in raw). This bears on DPR and on BGE-M3's dense mode, and favours late interaction. The proof is not in raw; capture it before building on it.

Late interaction has its own cost. AdaptiveEmbed measures it: as MetaEmbed grows from 2/4 to 16/64 tokens, the share of query tokens that match distinct document tokens falls from 100.0% to 80.8%, so extra vectors pile onto the same targets (raw/arxiv-2608-25412-adaptiveembed-sample-adaptive-multi-vector-representation-fulltext.md, §4.4, Figure 4c; source-adaptiveembed-samvr). Its SetSim score pairs tokens one to one to rule this out.

Leakage

Retrieval can score well by finding the test text. RETRO's authors measure this: it exploits overlap between store and test set more than its baselines do, yet still beats them on chunks that share fewer than 8 contiguous tokens with the training data (raw/arxiv-2112-04426-improving-language-models-by-retrieving-from.md, §2.6, §4.4). Norlund et al. find most of RETRO's gain comes from such overlap (arXiv 2302.12128; not in raw). See concept-evaluation-methodology.

Open questions

  • Single vector or many, at what store size? The proof and LIMIT say many; the cost of many grows with the store.
  • When should a model retrieve at all? Self-RAG learns it; most products always retrieve.
  • Retrieval or weights for new facts: the race experiment-weights-vs-context set up, and that concept-continual-learning and concept-knowledge-editing approach from the weights side.
