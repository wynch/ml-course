Inference Economics

concept created 2026-07-04 updated 2026-10-03 ai-economics token-pricing inference developer-tools evaluation

The economics of running LLMs: what a token costs, what a token buys, and how the gap between the two moves. New axis in the wiki as of 2026-07; previously the closest coverage was efficiency work (quantization, small models) without the demand-side lens.

Key Ideas

  • Tokenflation (demand side). source-token-purchasing-power-bigspin shows that for a fixed model at flat nominal prices, output per token can collapse — a 10K-token Opus 4.6 session shipped ~0.30 PRs / ~630 LOC in Feb 2026 vs ~0.16 PRs / ~91 LOC by mid-April, driven by a job-mix shift toward thinking (1% → 18%). Real cost rises while price lists stay flat.
  • Test-time compute is the cost driver. The same mechanism celebrated in concept-test-time-compute as a capability lever is, on the invoice, the main inflator of tokens-per-task.
  • The cheap-model tier (supply side). A deflationary counter-force: $10/mo access to open models (OpenCode Go: GLM, Kimi, Qwen, DeepSeek, MiniMax), local code models (Gemma 4 12B Coder), and 1M-context MoEs from new entrants (Meituan LongCat-2.0) — see source-2026-q2-ai-ecosystem-notes. Related efficiency levers: source-quantization-ground-up-ngrok, source-lacy-small-language-models.
  • Independent price/quality tracking. Artificial Analysis benchmarks quality-vs-price-vs-latency across 548+ models and providers (source-2026-q2-ai-ecosystem-notes), making the supply curve legible.
  • Harness search buys score with tokens. Unregularized harness evolution raised policy tokens per trial from 1.56 M to 3.80 M on an agentic workspace suite for +0.6 OOD points; the full regularized search, whose selection rule caps cost growth by measured gain, held it at 2.42 M for +3.9 (raw/arxiv-2609-24972-rrsi-regularized-recursive-self-improvement.md, §4.3, Table 2). See source-rrsi-regularized-harness-evolution.
  • Why batching makes tokens cheap. A decode step reads every weight to make one token per request. Below a saturation batch size the step costs about the same for 1 or 10 requests, so batching spreads the weight reads over more tokens; the price is higher inter-token latency. Servers tune for "goodput", the throughput that still meets latency targets (raw/web-2025-aleksagordic-inside-vllm.md, §"Scheduler"; §"Benchmarks and auto-tuning - latency vs throughput"). See source-inside-vllm-gordic, concept-llm-serving.
  • Numbers out, output free. Most LLMs charge more per output token than per input token. Jev, a "decision model" that answers only in numbers (probabilities, choice distributions, scores), charges for input alone, at $0.042 per million tokens, below GPT-5 Nano's $0.05; many questions on one input run in parallel (raw/web-2026-simonwillison-jev-decision-models.md, opening). Cheap enough, per Willison, that thousands of eval prompts cost a few cents (same file, §"Black boxes are back in fashion"). See source-willison-jev-decision-models. A measured run: 25,000 IMDb reviews in 22 min 24 s for $0.6492, at 96.47% accuracy (raw/web-2026-raschka-classifier-history-and-jev.md, §3.3; see source-raschka-text-classification-to-jev).
  • Loop budgeting. source-loop-engineering-addy-osmani frames autonomous-loop design as explicitly token-rich vs token-poor engineering — the harness layer is where economics and capability meet.

Why it matters for the wiki's axes

Agent harnesses (concept-harness-as-symbolic-substrate) are usually justified by capability; the economics lens gives an independent justification — deterministic scaffolding is cheap, thinking tokens are expensive, so pushing work out of the model and into the substrate is also a cost strategy. The measurement approach (PRs shipped, survival-adjusted output) is close in spirit to idea-benchmark-honest-epistemic-loop.

Open Questions

  1. Is tokenflation a model-behavior drift, a task-mix drift, or a Goodhart effect of optimizing for benchmark-visible reasoning? Bigspin's own three-way decomposition is unresolved.
  2. Does the cheap-open-model tier put a real ceiling on frontier pricing, or do capability gaps (e.g. source-frontiercode-cognition-eval's 13.4%-max Diamond split) keep the tiers segmented?
