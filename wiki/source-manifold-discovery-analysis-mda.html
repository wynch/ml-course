Manifold Discovery and Analysis (MDA)

source created 2026-05-06 updated 2026-05-06 representation-learning visualization regression manifold-learning interpretability nature-comms 2023

Md Tauhidul Islam, Zixia Zhou, Hongyi Ren, Masoud Badiei Khuzani, Daniel Kapp, James Zou, Lu Tian, Joseph C. Liao, Lei Xing — Nature Communications, 14:8506, 2023

Introduces MDA, a feature-visualization method aimed at the case existing techniques largely ignore: regression DNNs, where labels live on a continuous high-dimensional manifold rather than in a finite class set.

Problem

Most DNN visualization (t-SNE, UMAP variants on penultimate features) implicitly assumes discrete classes, and either fails or distorts when applied to regression. Yet a large fraction of practical deep learning is regression — segmentation, super-resolution, gene expression, survival prediction, etc. — and the feature manifold there has an inherently complex, continuum-valued shape.

Method

  1. Compute pairwise distances between DNN-estimated labels to reconstruct the outline of the label manifold.
  2. Use that outline to group labels by manifold-surface distance.
  3. Embed the high-dimensional features under the constraint imposed by these sorted label groups, using a Bayesian projection.
  4. Use a small DL component to map the constrained features into a low-dimensional space for visualization and analysis.

The pipeline therefore preserves local geometry of the label manifold, not just the feature manifold — which is what makes regression visualizations meaningful.

Applications

Demonstrated on five regression-flavored tasks:

  • Medical image segmentation
  • Image super-resolution
  • Gene expression prediction
  • Survival prediction
  • COVID-19 X-ray classification (as a contrast case)

Used to assess appropriateness of features, generalization behavior, and adversarial robustness — i.e. it's pitched as a diagnostic, not just a pretty picture.

Relevance

Sits in the concept-representation-learning cluster, with a focus on how to look at learned representations rather than how to train them. Shares the high-dimensional-geometry concern of source-visual-cortex-dimensionality and source-contrastive-learning-gaussian-representations, but the use case (regression, biomedical) is pretty different from typical contrastive/transformer settings. A useful tool-page rather than a theoretical contribution. Code is open-source at xinglab-ai/mda.
