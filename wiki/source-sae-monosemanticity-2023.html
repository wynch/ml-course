SAE for Monosemanticity — Two Concurrent 2023 Papers

source created 2026-05-07 updated 2026-05-07 interpretability sae dictionary-learning monosemanticity features foundational

Two independent groups, same finding: sparse autoencoders trained on transformer activations recover human-interpretable features at scale. Together they established SAEs as the working unit of post-2023 mech interp. Read both as a pair — Bricken et al. is the dictionary-learning recipe and the universality validation; Cunningham et al. is the residual-stream demonstration on real models.

Towards Monosemanticity — Bricken et al. (Anthropic, October 2023)

Source: transformer-circuits.pub/2023/monosemantic-features. Bricken, Templeton, Batson, ..., Olah.

Trains a sparse autoencoder on a 1-layer transformer's MLP activations and recovers >4000 monosemantic features from 512 neurons — Arabic text, DNA sequences, base64, legal language, HTTP headers, and so on. Each feature is a linear direction; activations reconstruct as sparse sums over them.

Validates features three ways:

  • Specificity: the feature fires only for its concept.
  • Steering: clamping the feature forces the concept into outputs.
  • Universality: the same features emerge across differently-seeded models.

The empirical payoff of superposition theory from source-anthropic-mi-foundations: a principled algorithm for reading out a neural layer as a discrete, human-labelable dictionary of concepts. Turns activation vectors into multi-hot symbol sets — the closest thing yet to "extracting the ontology" from a model.

Sparse Autoencoders Find Highly Interpretable Features — Cunningham et al. (ICLR 2024)

arXiv: 2309.08600. Cunningham, Ewart, Riggs, Huben, Sharkey.

Concurrent with Bricken et al. Applies SAEs to residual-stream activations of Pythia-70M and 410M (rather than Anthropic's 1-layer MLP toy). Shows SAE features score higher on automated LLM-judged interpretability than raw neurons, PCA, or ICA directions, and enable finer causal interventions — e.g. isolating the specific feature responsible for an IOI-style behaviour more precisely than activation patching on neurons.

Independent academic confirmation that sparse dictionary learning is the right lens on polysemantic activations. Crucially demonstrates it on the residual stream, not just MLPs — which matters because the residual stream is the shared computational bus every head reads/writes.

Why this pair matters

Together these papers established SAEs as a general-purpose tool for symbolically indexing any layer of any model — and made them the working unit for the entire post-2023 circuits programme. Subsequent work scales the recipe (source-sae-scaling-trilogy, source-sae-scaling-trilogy, source-sae-scaling-trilogy), discovers complete-replacement variants (source-complete-replacement-lm-layers), and uses SAE features as the substrate for circuit tracing (source-circuit-tracing-methods-anthropic, source-lindsey-2025-biology-of-llm).

The "two concurrent papers" pattern is itself notable: independent teams converged on dictionary learning at the same time, suggesting the move from polysemantic neurons to monosemantic features was overdue and theoretically forced (cf. source-anthropic-mi-foundations).
