Global Workspace (Access & Broadcast in LLMs)

concept created 2026-07-06 updated 2026-07-06 interpretability introspection global-workspace consciousness access-consciousness ai-alignment

Global Workspace Theory (GWT) — from cognitive neuroscience (Baars; Dehaene & Naccache) — holds that information becomes consciously accessible when it enters a small shared channel and is broadcast to many downstream systems. The interpretability question this concept tracks: does a trained language model develop an analogous broadcast hub, and can we read and steer it?

The J-space finding

source-anthropic-global-workspace answers yes for Claude. A small set of activation patterns — the J-space — behaves like GWT's workspace:

  • Broadcast connectivity. ~100× more components read/write these patterns than ordinary ones.
  • Access properties. Reportable, deliberately modulable, causally mediating, flexibly reused across tasks, and selective — it drives higher-order reasoning but not automatic processing (grammar, fluency).
  • Emergent, then shaped. Present in the pretrained model; post-training gives it "point-of-view" signatures (self-monitoring, warnings).

Reading it: the Jacobian Lens (J-Lens)

Defines the internal pattern for each vocabulary word as the one that most raises the probability of emitting that word at future timesteps — a Jacobian of future-token probability w.r.t. activations. Because it is defined by downstream effect, it is a form of predicting in latent space (thread-predict-in-latent-space).

Access vs. phenomenal consciousness

The result is framed strictly as evidence for access consciousness (functional reportability and reasoning), not phenomenal consciousness (subjective experience). The distinction matters for how much weight the finding can bear in AI-welfare and alignment debates.

Open questions

  • Is the J-space a genuinely unified channel or several overlapping hubs the method aggregates?
  • How much of "reportability" is the model reading its own workspace vs. confabulating a plausible description?
  • Does depth-substitutes-for-time (single forward pass vs. recurrent broadcast) weaken or just re-implement the GWT analogy?
  • Does the hub survive architecture changes (SSMs, MoE), or is it transformer/attention-cache specific?
