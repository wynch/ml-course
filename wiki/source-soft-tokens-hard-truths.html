Soft Tokens, Hard Truths

Summary

Earlier continuous chains of thought were either used only at inference on a normal model ("soft thinking") or distilled from written chains, which is costly and, in Coconut, limited the chain to 6 steps (R, §1). This paper trains them with reinforcement learning, rewarding only the final answer (R, §3, §4).

What the tokens are: during the chain of thought, instead of sampling one word, the model takes the probability-weighted mix of all word embeddings (a "soft token") and adds Gaussian noise to it before feeding it back. The noise is what reinforcement learning explores with: its log-probability is a simple squared distance, so the usual Reinforce-style update applies (R, §3, Eq. 6-10). "Fuzzy" tokens are the same at a temperature near 0, so each is almost a normal word plus noise (R, §3). Extra cost: one probability vector stored per step (R, §3). This allows chains of hundreds of tokens: up to 128 on GSM8K and 512 on MATH in training (R, §4).

What supervises them: a reward of 100 for a correct answer, 10 for a wrong answer in the right format, 0 otherwise, checked by Math Verify; RLOO with 32 samples per prompt, 3 seeds per setup (R, §4).

Key results

Llama 3.2 3B Instruct, Llama 3.1 8B Instruct and Qwen 2.5 3B Instruct, trained on GSM8K, MATH or DeepScaleR; tested on GSM8K, MATH-500 and OlympiadBench (R, §4).

  • Pass@1 is a draw: hard (normal), soft and fuzzy training reach similar greedy pass@1 (R, §4 "Results", Table 1).
  • Pass@32 favours soft and fuzzy training, clearest on Llama. Example, Llama 3B trained on GSM8K and tested on MATH-500: pass@32 72.4 (hard) vs 83.2 (fuzzy) vs 84.8 (soft); the untrained model has 82.0 (R, Table 1).
  • Hard inference wins: for every training method, decoding the chain as normal words at test time is generally best. So a model trained with soft tokens can be served in the standard way (R, §1, §4 "Results").
  • No gain from soft inference alone: "we do not confirm previously reported benefits of soft inference on hard (normal) training (Zhang et al., 2025)" (R, §4).
  • One collapse avoided: Llama 8B trained on GSM8K with hard tokens falls on MATH-500 to 20.2% greedy (base 44.4%); soft and fuzzy training keep 44.6-44.7% (R, §4, Table 1).
  • Softer touch out of domain: accuracy on HellaSwag, ARC and MMLU is about the same for all three, but hard training raises the loss on the correct answer, most on ARC (Llama 3B: 2.86 → 3.19; soft 2.92, fuzzy 2.88) (R, §4 "Out-of-domain robustness", Table 2).
  • Entropy: soft and fuzzy training keep the base model's entropy profile along the chain; hard training lowers it, which the authors read as overconfidence (R, §4 "Entropy behavior").
  • Cost: each run used one node of 8 H100 or H200 GPUs for 48-96 hours (R, Appendix H).

Caveats

  • Maths only for training; three models up to 8B (R, §4).
  • Much of the pass@32 advantage is hard training losing diversity: soft and fuzzy land near the untrained model (83.2 and 84.8 vs 82.0 in the example above), hard falls below it (72.4). The authors note the loss of diversity is known for answer-reward Reinforce (R, §4 "Results", Table 1).
  • Qwen without finetuning scores far below its own report under this prompt (8.9% on GSM8K greedy), a format problem the authors explain in Appendix I (R, Table 1 footnote, Appendix I).
  • The trained soft chains are not read or probed; the analysis is about scores and entropy.

  • source-coconut-continuous-latent-reasoning — this paper's foil: Coconut distills written chains and backpropagates through the whole continuous chain; the authors say its benefits "appear confined to a benchmark designed for this purpose" (R, §2). Coconut feeds back the hidden state; soft tokens feed back a mix of word embeddings (R, §3 footnote 1).
  • source-abstract-token-curriculum — the other route to continuous thoughts with only the answer supervised: ATC uses a difficulty curriculum and backpropagation, this paper uses reinforcement learning with noise.

  • source-distribution-sharpening-power-sampling — the same pass@k story from the other side: answer-reward RL narrows the distribution; soft-token RL narrows it less (R, §4).
  • source-continuous-cot-two-regimes — Contradiction (partial): that paper finds compressing continuous-CoT methods fall to about 35% of written-CoT accuracy on MATH and speaks of "the limitations of current continuous CoT methods" (raw/arxiv-2607-16972-continuous-cot-tale-of-two-regimes.md, Abstract, Table 5). Here, Llama 3B trained on MATH with soft tokens and read with soft greedy inference scores 43.2% on MATH-500, against 49.1% for normal-token training (R, Appendix E, Table 4); read with normal tokens it scores 44.5%, so most of the gap comes from training, not from thinking in soft tokens. Soft chains are as long as written ones, so no compression is tested here; model size (3B vs 1-1.5B) and test set (MATH-500 vs MATH) also differ.
