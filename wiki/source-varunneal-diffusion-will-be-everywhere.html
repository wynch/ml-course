Diffusion will be everywhere

source created 2026-10-03 updated 2026-10-03 diffusion-language-models inference test-time-compute speculative-decoding reinforcement-learning looped-transformers essay 2026

Author: Varun Srivastava (@varunneal), personal essay Published: 2026-09-27; announced on X on 2026-10-01 Raw copies: raw/web-2026-varunneal-diffusion-everywhere.md (the essay, full text; figures as captions only) and raw/tweet-varunneal-diffusion-everywhere.md (the post, which says only "I'm convinced text diffusion is the future, and we'll start to see it everywhere very soon")

Claims cite the essay as R, with its section names. This is an argument from one author, not a study: most numbers come from papers he cites, chiefly Google DeepMind's DiffusionGemma technical report, which the wiki has not ingested.

Summary

Text diffusion writes a whole block of tokens at once. The block starts as noise; each step predicts every position and commits the ones it is most sure of, until all are committed (R, "What's diffusion?"). P tokens then take K forward passes, K much smaller than P, at the cost of predicting all P positions each pass (R, "What's diffusion?", table).

His thesis: diffusion beats left-to-right (autoregressive) generation on inference speed, test-time scaling and RL, and turning a finished autoregressive model into a diffusion model is now cheap, so any lab can do it (R, Overview, "Why now?").

Key claims

  • Speed where memory is the limit. Decoding often waits on memory reads (weights once per batch, the KV cache once per sequence), so extra arithmetic is nearly free; long contexts, small batches and mixture-of-experts models are all memory-bound (R, "The case from inference"). As he reports it, DiffusionGemma is faster than its autoregressive twin (with multi-token prediction) up to a batch size of about 32 at 4,096 input and 1,024 output tokens (R, same section).
  • Diffusion is already everywhere as a drafter. DFlash-style speculative decoding uses a small diffusion model to guess a block in one pass; he says almost every serving stack ships it (R, "The case from inference", footnote 8).
  • Test-time scaling without new states. Lowering an entropy threshold commits fewer tokens per step and buys more steps (R, "Test-time scaling"). Training already samples every noise level, so extra steps at inference land in states the model has seen; extra loops of a looped transformer do not (R, same section). As reported from the DiffusionGemma report, its GPQA-Diamond and LiveCodeBench average climbs from about 9 to 59 with more denoising steps (R, same section, Figure 3).
  • Loops do not extrapolate. Looped transformers give "a compute knob over the range you trained on, not beyond it"; Parcae's test-time gains saturate near its mean training loop count (R, "Test-time scaling"). His reasons: new loops make KV entries no attention head has seen, and a loop either contracts to a fixed point, where more loops add nothing, or expands and diverges (R, same section).
  • No growing cache. Unlike chain of thought, finished denoising steps leave nothing in the KV cache to pay for later; the two can be combined (R, "Test-time scaling").
  • RL is now tractable. The probability of a sampled trajectory factors over the tokens committed at each step, so policy gradients are exact; sampling a random subset of steps keeps them unbiased and cheap (R, "RL for diffusion", Eq. 1-2). Repair and self-conditioning break this and need a cruder or slightly biased estimate (R, same section).
  • Conversion is cheap. DiffusionGemma starts from the post-trained Gemma 4 checkpoint, uses under 10% of the original training tokens, about 1,400 RL steps (his estimate from a plot), and trades "a moderate quality cost" for a 5-7× speedup (R, "Why now?", footnotes 1, 43).
  • His own recipe (appendix): feed the last hidden state back as self-conditioning, and train with two noise levels so training matches what the sampler sees (R, "Appendix: self-conditioning"). No results given.

Caveats

  • Almost every number is second-hand, mostly from one technical report the wiki does not hold; some, such as the RL step count, are his reading of a plot (R, footnote 43).
  • He gives the quality cost of conversion only as "moderate" (R, "Why now?").
  • The claim about loops rests on citations and an argument, not on his own runs.
  • Predictions ("diffusion models will become anti-modal", byte-level diffusion) are opinion (R, "Why now?").
