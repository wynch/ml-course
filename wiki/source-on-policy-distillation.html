Summary

Two ways to post-train a small "student" model: on-policy, where it learns from its own samples graded by a reward (RL), and off-policy, where it imitates a teacher's text (supervised fine-tuning, SFT). RL's signal is sparse, one reward per episode; imitation's is dense but taught on states the student never reaches itself (R, opening, "On-policy distillation — best of both worlds").

On-policy distillation mixes the two. The student writes its own answers; the teacher gives its log-probability for every token the student wrote; the per-token reward is the negative reverse KL between student and teacher, so the student is pushed toward what the teacher would do in each state the student actually visits (R, "Loss function: reverse KL", "Pseudocode"). It needs only the teacher's compute_logprobs, not its weights (R, "On-policy distillation recovers post-training behavior").

  • Forgetting repair. Fine-tuning Qwen3-8B on internal company documents mixed 70/30 with chat data raised internal QA from 18% to 36% and cut instruction following (IF-eval) from 85% to 79%; on-policy distillation from the original Qwen3-8B, on chat prompts only, brought IF-eval back to 83% and internal QA to 41%. Documents alone: QA 43%, IF-eval 45% (R, "On-policy distillation recovers post-training behavior", table).
  • No data mix keeps IF-eval. Even 100% chat data during fine-tuning lowers it; LoRA learns less and still forgets (R, "Training on new knowledge degrades learned behavior").
  • SFT on the model's own samples still hurts. Qwen3-32B fine-tuned on its own temperature-1 samples, a dataset with zero KL to itself, loses IF-eval at any practical learning rate (R, "On-policy learning as a tool for continual learning").
  • The authors' reading: RL spends its compute searching for strategies; once one is found, distillation is a cheap way to teach it (R, "RL searches in the space of semantic strategies").
  • Proposal for continual learning: alternate fine-tuning on new data with distillation from an earlier version of the model (R, "On-policy distillation recovers post-training behavior", "On-policy learning as a tool for continual learning").

Caveats

  • A company blog, not peer-reviewed; the company sells the fine-tuning service (Tinker) that runs these recipes.
  • The internal-assistant results use a private document set and private QA eval.
  • "70%" for 2M SFT examples is an extrapolation; FLOP counts favour methods that parallelise well, by the authors' own note (R, "On-policy distillation").
  • For the maths run the teacher was Qwen3-8B, not Qwen3-32B, though FLOPs are counted for the 32B (R, same section).
  • The repair gets IF-eval back to 83%, not to the original 85%.
