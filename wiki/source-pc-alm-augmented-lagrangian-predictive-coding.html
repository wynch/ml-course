PC-ALM: predictive coding with a Lagrange multiplier per layer

Summary

Backpropagation needs a forward pass, then a backward pass, then a weight update, in strict order; the authors argue the brain cannot do this, at least not exactly (R, §1; B, opening). Predictive coding (PC) is a local alternative: each layer adjusts its activation to shrink the prediction error with its neighbours, and weights then follow the errors left over. Every step uses only adjacent layers (R, §3).

PC's weakness: credit from the output spreads like heat, so in deep, narrow networks it fades before reaching early layers, and PC falls behind backprop (R, §1, §5.5; B, "PC trains deep networks, but exhibits signal decay").

PC-ALM adds one more variable per layer, a Lagrange multiplier λ the size of the layer, which adds up that layer's prediction errors over the inference steps and feeds back into it (R, §4, Algorithm 1). The blog calls each layer a proportional-integral feedback controller: the error is the P term, λ the I term (B, "Control theory and credit assignment"). Setting the dual rate α to 0 gives back plain PC (R, §4).

The root idea is old: LeCun (1988) showed that the multipliers of a network's Lagrangian equal backprop's gradients. PC's energy is the extra quadratic term of the augmented Lagrangian, so PC-ALM joins the two (R, §1, §3; X).

Key results

  • Linear networks: PC-ALM converges to exact backprop gradients, using only layer-local updates (R, §5.1, Appendix C, Proposition 3).
  • Deep, narrow networks: residual MLPs with width and depth each in {8, 16, 32, 64, 128}, MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, one epoch. PC degrades with depth; PC-ALM matches backprop across the grid with T = 2L inference steps, and closes part of the gap at T = L (R, §5.3, Appendix F).
  • Credit moves as a wave: in T steps PC spreads credit on the order of √(ρη_hT) layers; PC-ALM about T·√(αη_h) layers, and evenly across depth ("ballistic" against "diffusive") (R, §5.5, Appendix D). Alignment with backprop jumps near step L/√(αη_h), about 2L at α = 1 (R, §5.6).
  • Cost: twice PC's activation memory. To reach 0.9 cosine similarity with backprop's gradient at depth 32, PC-ALM at fixed width costs about an order of magnitude less compute than widening PC (R, §5.7, Figure 8).
  • Blog only, not in the paper: residual MLPs up to 1000 layers on MNIST, five epochs, width 32, within about 2 points of backprop (B, "PC-ALM: a local learning method for training 1000-layer neural networks"); gains over PC on every image task tried, including ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 and Tiny ImageNet (B, "Image classification benchmarks"). These appear as figures; the text gives no other numbers.

Caveats

  • The paper's tests: MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, residual MLPs, one epoch, depth up to 128. The authors list larger data, longer training and other architectures as future work (R, Appendix G).
  • The post's "trains 1000-layer neural nets" (X) rests on the blog's figure, not on the paper.
  • The proof covers linear networks without biases; nonlinear convergence is open (R, Appendix G). Exact backprop gradients need equilibrium, and the steps needed grow with depth (R, Appendix G).
  • The compute comparison assumes PC runs a fixed inner budget, which the authors say favours PC (R, Appendix E).
  • PC's settled activations differ from the forward pass ("prospective configuration"), which may help sample efficiency; PC-ALM gives that up at convergence (B, "Prospective-configuration tradeoff").
