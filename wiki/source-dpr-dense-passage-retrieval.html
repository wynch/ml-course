Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR)

source created 2026-05-07 updated 2026-05-07 retrieval dense-retrieval rag embeddings foundational 2020

Karpukhin, Oguz, Min, Lewis, Wu, Edunov, Chen, Yih — arXiv 2004.04906, EMNLP 2020 (Facebook AI / U. Washington / Princeton)

The paper that made dense retrieval beat BM25 on open-domain QA, decisively shifting the field from sparse (TF-IDF / BM25) toward learned embedding search. Two independently trained BERT encoders — one for questions, one for passages — produce fixed-size vectors; retrieval is dot-product nearest-neighbour over a precomputed passage index.

Method

  • Dual-encoder ("bi-encoder") architecture: E_Q(question) and E_P(passage), each a BERT-base, output 768-d CLS embeddings.
  • Similarity = simple dot product. Index built with FAISS for sub-linear ANN search.
  • Training: contrastive loss on (question, positive passage, negatives) triples. The decisive trick: in-batch negatives — each question's positive serves as a negative for every other question in the batch. A batch of 128 gives 127 free negatives per example.
  • Hard negatives mined from BM25's top-ranked but irrelevant passages add another ~2 points of accuracy.
  • Training data: ~60k question–passage pairs from Natural Questions, TriviaQA, WebQuestions, CuratedTREC, SQuAD.

Key numbers

  • Top-20 retrieval accuracy on Natural Questions: DPR 78.4 vs BM25 59.1 — a ~20-point absolute gap.
  • End-to-end open-domain QA exact-match on NQ: 41.5 (vs prior 33.3 for BM25+reader).
  • Index of 21M Wikipedia passages searchable in milliseconds with FAISS HNSW.

Why it matters

DPR is the foundation paper of modern retrieval-augmented generation. Every RAG pipeline that uses a learned embedding model — production systems at OpenAI, Anthropic, Cohere, every vector database — descends from this dual-encoder template. The paper established three durable ideas:

  1. Dense beats sparse for semantic matching, given enough training data — though hybrid sparse+dense remains common in practice.
  2. In-batch negatives is the right contrastive recipe for retrieval, mirroring the contrastive vision lineage (source-ssl-contrastive-foundations) and reused everywhere from sentence-transformers to OpenAI's text-embedding-3.
  3. Bi-encoder cheapness is load-bearing: the passage index is precomputed once, queries are a single forward pass + ANN lookup. This is what lets retrieval scale to billions of documents and millisecond latency.

The follow-up tension DPR sets up: bi-encoders compress too aggressively — a single 768-d vector can't capture all token-level evidence. source-colbert addresses exactly this with late interaction, trading index size for fidelity. source-retro-deepmind then asks whether retrieval should be a separate pipeline at all or baked into LM pretraining. source-self-rag adds the reflection/decision layer that DPR's pipeline lacks.

DPR is the retrieval-side counterpart to BERT for QA: not the most clever idea in retrospect, but the one that proved the regime works.
