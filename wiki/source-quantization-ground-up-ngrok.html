Quantization from the Ground Up

source created 2026-04-13 updated 2026-09-26 quantization inference llm deployment tutorial

Sam Rose — ngrok blog, 2025/2026

A ground-up tutorial on LLM quantization, focused on post-training quantization (PTQ) fundamentals rather than specialized schemes like GPTQ or AWQ. Builds intuition from floating-point representation through symmetric and asymmetric integer quantization, walks through converting bfloat16 weights down to 4-bit and 2-bit representations, and benchmarks the quality cliff.

Two primary approaches

  • Symmetric quantization — scale values around zero into a smaller integer range.
  • Asymmetric quantization — use an offset "zero point" to more efficiently pack the available bits.

Benchmarked tradeoffs (Qwen3.5 9B)

PrecisionQualitySpeed
8-bitminimal loss~2.5× faster
4-bit~5-10% accuracy loss~2× faster
2-bitsevere degradationunusable

Key insight: degradation is non-linear. 16-bit → 8-bit is almost free; 16-bit → 4-bit retains roughly 90% quality; 2-bit falls off a cliff. Measured via perplexity, KL divergence, GPQA Diamond, and direct conversational testing.

Relevance

Practical counterweight to the "scaling-only" story: real deployments already live in 4-bit territory, meaning the effective inference substrate is substantially sparser than the training substrate. Relevant to concept-model-training and concept-developer-tools. Also interesting as a cheap approximation of source-neural-computer-metauto's call for sparser, more addressable substrates — quantization is the dumbest way to reduce representational redundancy, but it works, and it's what everyone actually ships.

Why fewer bytes can mean faster (2026-09-26)

Decode is memory-bandwidth-bound: each step reads all weights to make one token (raw/web-2025-aleksagordic-inside-vllm.md, §"Scheduler"). The course's int8 lab gives the same reason for real-engine speedups and shows none at 620K parameters, where its engine is compute-bound and dequantizes at load (course/modules/07-inference-internals/README.md, "The quantization lab"). The speed column above fits that account; this page's source did not state the reason in the summary kept here. See concept-llm-serving.
