Neural Thickets: Diverse Task Experts Are Dense Around Pretrained Weights (RandOpt)

source created 2026-04-05 updated 2026-10-20 reinforcement-learning optimization noise ensembling

Authors: Yulu Gan, Phillip Isola · Date: 2026-03-12 arXiv: 2603.12228 Code: sunrainyg/RandOpt Via: @yule_gan on X Full text: raw/arxiv-2603-12228-neural-thickets-diverse-task-experts-are.md (v1, the only version, captured 2026-10-20), cited below by section.

Summary

Sample N random Gaussian perturbations of the pretrained weights, keep the top K on a task, and ensemble them by majority vote. The method is fully parallel and uses no gradients. The authors call it "competitive with" PPO, GRPO and evolution strategies for large modern models. Their explanation: in large, well-pretrained models, task experts lie densely around the pretrained weights; in small models they are rare, and gradient descent is needed (arXiv 2603.12228, abstract). Task domains (maths, coding, writing, chemistry) per the repo. Superseded (2026-10-20): the paper names them itself: maths (Countdown, GSM8K, MATH-500, OlyBench), code (MBPP), writing (ROCStories), chemistry (USPTO), on Qwen, Llama and OLMo-3 models from 0.5B to 8B (§5.1).

Key Ideas

  • Single-step Gaussian noise injection + ensembling
  • No gradients, no iterative optimization
  • Competitive with PPO/GRPO for large models (abstract)
    • Added 2026-10-20: the comparison is uneven by design. RandOpt votes over K = 50 perturbed models at test time; PPO and GRPO answer with one sample, which the authors say "disadvantages the baseline" (§5.1, Figure 6). With K = 1 RandOpt is much weaker (§5.1). Training flops are matched (§5.1).
  • Raises fundamental questions about what RL training actually provides
  • Provocative result: is RL just noise + selection?
    • The authors' own limits: useless from scratch and weak on small pretrained models; gains saturate at large model size and large N; K forward passes at inference unless distilled; majority vote does not fit structured outputs (§11).
