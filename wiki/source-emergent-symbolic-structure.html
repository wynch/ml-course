The Emergent Symbolic Structure of Artificial Neural Networks

source created 2026-09-10 updated 2026-09-10 neural-symbolic tensor-product-representations mechanistic-interpretability representation-learning causal-intervention 2026

Authors: R. Thomas McCoy, Paul Soulos, Tal Linzen, Paul Smolensky · arXiv: 2608.29530v1 · Date: 2026-08-30

Summary

The claim: a network's vectors may already be symbol structures, just written in continuous form. The tool is the Tensor Product Representation (TPR) — Smolensky's scheme that binds a filler (a value, e.g. "dogs") to a role (a slot, e.g. "object") by tensor product and sums the bindings. The method, DISCOVER, replaces a network's whole representation-generating process with one closed-form equation, W(Σᵢ fᵢ ⊗ rᵢ) + b, and checks whether behaviour survives. It does, on small sequence models trained to copy/reverse/interleave letters and on seven LLMs across arithmetic, logic, code execution, and syntax. They then edit the fitted TPR — swapping which role an adjective occupies — and the LLM's output changes as predicted, which is the causal half of the argument.

Key claims

  • A closed-form symbolic equation can stand in for the representation pipeline. On letter-sequence tasks with a bidirectional role scheme, approximation accuracy was near-perfect: the lowest across 10 reruns of GRU reversal was 99.98%.
  • It holds at LLM scale. Seven models tested (Gemma-3-27b, GPT-2-XL, GPT-OSS-20b, Pythia-12b, Qwen3-14b, OLMo-2-13B, Llama-3.1-8b). On GPT-OSS across arithmetic, syllogisms, code execution, passivization, tense reinflection and question formation, the symbolic substitute tracked the model's own accuracy, with a largest gap of 2.36% (arithmetic).
  • Fidelity degrades with structural complexity. Subject-verb-object position encoding hit 100%; list conditions typically above 85%; complex sentences only above 50%.
  • Interventions are targeted, not just correlational. Editing the role assignment in the TPR (e.g. moving "clever" from subject-adjective to object-adjective) makes the LLM behave as if the adjective moved — evidence the behaviour depends on the recovered structure.
  • The authors are explicit about what they do not explain. They state they are not attempting to explain how the network produces vectors with this structure. Role schemes are specified by a human, so the method is supervised discovery, not automatic. Task-specific role schemes beat task-agnostic ones, suggesting the structure is domain-dependent rather than one universal grammar. The syllogism task had the lowest LLM accuracy (76%), so the approximation there is fitting weaker behaviour.
