Stop-Gradient

concept created 2026-10-23 updated 2026-10-23 stop-gradient optimization training-dynamics self-supervised-learning reinforcement-learning flow-maps hub

A stop-gradient (detach in PyTorch, stop_gradient in JAX) passes a value forward unchanged but blocks its gradient on the way back. In a loss where the model appears twice, wrapping one copy in a stop-gradient means training moves the model toward that copy as if it were a fixed target (raw/arxiv-2609-16222-how-i-learned-to-stop-worrying.md, §1, §2).

Where it is used

Shen et al. list self-distillation, reinforcement learning (TD learning and actor-critic), consistency models and flow maps, representation learning and variational inference (same file, §1). In this wiki:

The catch

With a stop-gradient, the update is a semi-gradient: in general not the gradient of the loss, and possibly not the gradient of any loss, so optimisation theory gives no guarantee that training settles where intended (raw/arxiv-2609-16222-how-i-learned-to-stop-worrying.md, §1, §5.1). BYOL's authors made a similar point about their own method in 2020: they hypothesised that no loss exists whose gradient descent gives BYOL's dynamics. They tie this to the moving-average target, whose updates do not follow the loss's gradient, rather than to the stop-gradient itself (raw/arxiv-2006-07733-bootstrap-your-own-latent-a-new.md, §3.2). The parallel is this wiki's; Shen et al. do not analyse BYOL (raw/arxiv-2609-16222-how-i-learned-to-stop-worrying.md, §7).

A sufficient answer for one family

Shen et al. give a template: the model appears once outside the stop-gradient and regresses onto a target, that is, is trained to match it under squared error. The target must be affine in the labels: a part that ignores the labels plus a part proportional to them. For flow maps the labels are the velocities of individual training samples. Then training can only stop where the model equals its target, a fixed point, and a full update step is exactly fixed-point iteration (raw/arxiv-2609-16222-how-i-learned-to-stop-worrying.md, §3, Theorem 1). For four flow-map losses they prove the fixed point is unique (same file, §4) and that idealised training (infinite capacity, continuous time) reaches it; for three of the four, the convergence proof, not the uniqueness proof, assumes perfect pretraining first (same file, §5.2, §8). In this wiki's reading, the template does not cover losses with a separate predictor and a moving-average target, such as BYOL's.

Open questions

  • Does the idealised convergence carry over to finite networks trained by SGD? The authors flag this as a limit (raw/arxiv-2609-16222-how-i-learned-to-stop-worrying.md, §8).
  • Can a similar template say when stop-gradients prevent collapse in self-supervised learning, where the bad fixed point is a constant output? Shen et al. cite Ponce et al. for two-player settings such as BYOL, where stop-gradients prevent collapse (raw/arxiv-2609-16222-how-i-learned-to-stop-worrying.md, §7).
