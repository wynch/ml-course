Predictive Coding

concept created 2026-10-20 updated 2026-10-20 predictive-coding local-learning backpropagation credit-assignment neuroai hub

Predictive coding (PC) began as a model of cortex: each level predicts the activity of the level below and passes up only the error (Rao and Ballard, 1999; Friston and Kiebel, 2009; cited in raw/arxiv-2605-31022-augmented-lagrangian-predictive-coding.md, §2). In machine learning it is a learning rule. Each layer nudges its activation to shrink the prediction error with its neighbours; the weights then follow the errors that remain. Every step uses only adjacent layers, so no backward pass runs through the whole network (same file, §3).

The wiki uses the term in three senses. Keep them apart.

The third sense borrows the name. V-JEPA's authors trace their "predictive feature principle" to Rao and Ballard (raw/arxiv-2404-08471-revisiting-feature-prediction-for-learning-visual.md, §1), and InfoNCE comes from "contrastive predictive coding" (raw/arxiv-2609-37789-predictive-ssl-identifies-stochastic-signals-under-nuisance.md, §3). Both train with backprop.

As a learning rule

PC is gradient flow on one scalar energy, the sum of squared prediction errors between layers (concept-energy-based-models; raw/arxiv-2605-31022-augmented-lagrangian-predictive-coding.md, Abstract, §3).

  • Where it stands. PC can match backprop on small and medium image classification but lags well behind on deep networks; the paper credits Pinchetti et al. (2025) for this summary of the field (raw/arxiv-2605-31022-augmented-lagrangian-predictive-coding.md, §2).
  • Why it lags. Credit from the output spreads like heat: in T inference steps it reaches on the order of √(ρη_hT) layers, so it fades before the early layers of a deep, narrow network (same file, §1, §5.5; the √ rate is Appendix D, Eq. 23).
  • One fix: PC-ALM. Add a Lagrange multiplier per layer that sums that layer's errors over the inference steps. Credit then travels as a wave, about T·√(αη_h) layers in T steps. On residual MLPs up to depth and width 128, on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST for one epoch, PC-ALM matches backprop at T = 2L steps, where plain PC falls behind with depth (same file, §4, §5.3, §5.5; the T·√(αη_h) rate is Appendix D, Eq. 23). In linear networks it converges to backprop's exact gradients (same file, §5.1, Appendix C).
  • The price. Twice PC's activation memory, and the steps needed grow with depth (same file, §5.7, Appendix G).
  • What the blog adds. 1,000-layer residual MLPs on MNIST within about 2 points of backprop, and gains over PC on ResNet-18 for CIFAR-10 and Tiny ImageNet. These appear only as figures in the blog, not in the paper (raw/web-2026-sakana-pc-alm-blog.md, "PC-ALM: a local learning method for training 1000-layer neural networks", "Image classification benchmarks").

Not the same as active inference

Active inference uses a generative model to choose actions by minimising expected free energy. PC as a learning rule chooses no actions; PC-ALM's authors frame it as a penalty method from constrained optimisation, not as a probabilistic model (raw/arxiv-2605-31022-augmented-lagrangian-predictive-coding.md, Appendix H). The two share a history and the word "prediction error", not a method.

Open questions

  • Does PC-ALM hold beyond one epoch of MNIST-scale data and residual MLPs? Its authors list larger data, longer training and other architectures as future work (raw/arxiv-2605-31022-augmented-lagrangian-predictive-coding.md, Appendix G).
  • No proof covers nonlinear networks (same file, Appendix G).
  • PC's settled activations differ from the forward pass ("prospective configuration"), which may help learn from few samples; PC-ALM gives that up at convergence (raw/web-2026-sakana-pc-alm-blog.md, "Prospective-configuration tradeoff"). Which matters more is untested here.
