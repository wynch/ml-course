LLM Reasoning Skeptics

source created 2026-05-07 updated 2026-05-07 llm-reasoning mechanistic-analysis grokking reasoning-evaluation survey failure-modes limitations ai-limitations scientific-discovery philosophy-of-ai creativity

Three sources, early 2026, that push back on the "LLMs reason" framing from three different angles: a mechanistic critique showing a flagship reasoning architecture guesses rather than reasons, a comprehensive survey taxonomising reasoning failures, and a philosophical position paper arguing that compression-based systems cannot make genuine creative leaps. Together they form the wiki's principled skepticism stance on concept-llm-reasoning.

Are Your Reasoning Models Reasoning or Guessing? — Ren, Liu 2026

arXiv 2601.10679, 2026-01-15 (revised 2026-03-22). Via @zimingliu11 on X.

Mechanistic investigation of hierarchical reasoning models (HRM) revealing they often guess rather than truly reason. Proposes augmentation strategies that improve Sudoku-Extreme accuracy from 54.5% → 96.9%.

Key claims and numbers

  • HRMs fail on puzzles with only one unknown cell — violating basic reasoning assumptions.
  • Exhibit grokking-style dynamics: solutions suddenly improve at critical steps rather than gradually.
  • Multiple fixed points cause models to get trapped in incorrect solutions.
  • Three scaling strategies proposed:
    1. Data augmentation — improves guess quality.
    2. Input perturbation — increases guess variety via inference randomness.
    3. Model bootstrapping — leverages training randomness.
  • Augmented HRM: 54.5% → 96.9% on Sudoku-Extreme.

Distinctive contribution: a mechanistic (not behavioural) demonstration that even a model explicitly designed for hierarchical reasoning relies heavily on guessing. The one-unknown-cell failure mode is a sharp diagnostic — any genuine reasoner should solve it trivially.

Large Language Model Reasoning Failures — Song, Han, Goodman 2026

arXiv 2602.06176, 2026-02-05.

Comprehensive survey of reasoning failures in LLMs through a novel categorization framework. Distinguishes embodied vs non-embodied reasoning, with non-embodied subdivided into informal and formal variants.

Three failure categories

  1. Fundamental architectural limitations — inherent to current designs.
  2. Application-specific constraints — domain-dependent failures.
  3. Robustness inconsistencies — fragile reasoning under perturbation.

Analyzes root causes and mitigation strategies for each, producing a structured taxonomy for understanding where and why LLMs fail.

Distinctive contribution: the taxonomy reference — a unified vocabulary for talking about reasoning failures across heterogeneous benchmarks. Lets a practitioner classify a new failure mode by family rather than treating each as bespoke.

LLMs Can't Jump — Zahavy 2026

Position paper, PhilSci Archive. Via @tzahavy on X.

Position paper using Einstein's "happiest thought" (the equivalence principle) as a case study to argue that LLMs cannot truly invent — they compress existing knowledge rather than making genuine creative leaps. Explores the boundary between compression/interpolation and genuine scientific invention.

Argument

  • Uses Einstein's happiest thought (equivalence principle) as the canonical example of a creative leap that cannot be produced by interpolating in a training distribution.
  • Argues LLMs are fundamentally compressors, not inventors.
  • Questions whether genuine scientific discovery is possible through compression alone.

Distinctive contribution: the philosophical position the wiki cites when the question is not "do LLMs benchmark well" but "is what they're doing the same kind of thing as scientific invention." Reframes the capability debate as a question about the difference between within-distribution and out-of-distribution generation.

Why this grouping matters

The three papers attack the "LLMs reason" framing from three mutually reinforcing angles:

AngleSourceWhat it shows
MechanisticRen & LiuA reasoning-specialised architecture (HRM) guesses rather than reasons, even on tasks where reasoning is trivially required.
Empirical taxonomySong, Han, GoodmanReasoning failures fall into structural categories — architectural, application, robustness. Not noise.
PhilosophicalZahavyEven granting empirical reasoning, compression-based systems cannot make the kind of creative leaps that define genuine scientific discovery.

Read as a unit, they argue the field's reasoning claims are simultaneously over-stated (mechanistic and empirical evidence of failures) and mis-framed (compression ≠ invention). This pairs naturally with the metric-side critique in source-emergence-debate (Schaeffer): apparent reasoning gains are partly metric artefacts, and what remains is partly guessing.
