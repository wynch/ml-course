Self-Correction

concept created 2026-10-20 updated 2026-10-20 self-correction self-refine reflection test-time-compute reasoning hub

Self-correction: a model checks its own answer and revises it. The question that matters is where the check gets its information. A model that critiques itself with nothing new to go on can only reuse what it already knew; a model that reads a test result, a retrieved passage or a reward has outside feedback.

The check without feedback

Contradiction (open), carried from source-self-refine: Huang et al., "Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet" (ICLR 2024, arXiv 2310.01798), find that with no outside feedback, self-correction leaves reasoning scores flat or lowers them, and that some reported gains came from oracle labels deciding when to stop (abstract; not in raw). Six of Self-Refine's seven tasks are writing or code tasks (dialogue, code optimisation and readability, sentiment, acronyms, constrained text); one is maths reasoning (source-self-refine). Reflexion's evaluator brings outside feedback (source-reflexion-verbal-rl). Read together: self-correction helps when something outside the model says what went wrong.

R1-Zero sits apart. Its "rethink" behaviour shows up in the text at test time with no checker, but it was grown by a checker during training (raw/arxiv-2501-12948-deepseek-r1-incentivizing-reasoning-capability-in.md, §2.3). Whether such learned reflection fixes errors, or only adds length, is the sharpening question of concept-rlvr.

Open questions

  • Neither Self-Refine, Reflexion nor Huang et al. has a full text in raw; capture them before weighing the contradiction further.
  • How much of R1-style reflection corrects real errors? No source here counts revised-and-right against revised-and-wrong.
