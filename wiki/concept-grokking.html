Grokking

concept created 2026-10-20 updated 2026-10-23 training-dynamics generalization grokking weight-decay mechanistic-interpretability hub

Grokking: a network fits its training set, then much later generalises. Power et al. (2022) saw it on small transformers trained on tables like a + b mod p; weight decay made it come sooner (arXiv 2201.02177, as course module 14 reports it; not in raw). Two puzzles follow: why the delay, and why it often fails to happen at all without weight decay (raw/arxiv-2501-04697-grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical.md, §1).

The wiki holds four accounts of the same effect. They do not all exclude each other, but they disagree on what weight decay does and on what sets the timing.

Four accounts

AccountWhat happens during the delayWhat weight decay doesSource
Circuit, then cleanupthe network memorises, then builds a Fourier circuit for modular addition, then cleans away the lookup tabledrives the cleanupsource-nanda-2023-grokking-modular-addition (arXiv 2301.05217; not in raw)
Task symmetrymemorisation, then the network acquires the task's symmetries, then organises them in geometry; a symmetry measure predicts onsetnot stated in the abstractsource-grokking-intrinsic-symmetries (arXiv 2603.01968, abstract only; not in raw)
Signal and noisethe neural tangent kernel splits outputs into signal and noise directions; under SGD, signal drifts in fast and memorisation diffuses slowly; an SNR preconditioner on Adam speeds grokking about 5×not the focussource-litman-generalization-theory (arXiv 2605.01172; not in raw)
Softmax collapseonce training is fitted, the cheapest way to lower the loss is to scale every logit up; the logits grow until float32 rounds the loss and its gradient to zero, and learning stopsstops the logit growthsource-grokking-softmax-collapse (raw/arxiv-2501-04697-grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical.md, §3.1, §4.2, §5.2)

Only the last has a full text in raw. Its evidence (same file):

  • Precision decides when learning stops. With no weight decay, test accuracy halts when collapse sets in; float64 halts later than float32, float16 earlier (§3.2–3.3, Figure 2).
  • Stop the collapse and grokking returns with no weight decay. StableMax, a softmax whose exponential is replaced by a ramp, groks at 40% training data, while the weight norm grows all the while (§3.3, Definition 4, Figure 4).
  • Remove the scaling and the delay goes. Keeping only the part of each gradient orthogonal to the weights reaches 100% test accuracy on subtraction mod 113 within 400 iterations, with no overfitting phase first (§5.1, Figure 6).
  • A smaller input removes the delay too. With each number coded as a random 14-bit vector instead of one-hot, train and test accuracy rise together (§4.1, Figure 4).

Contradiction (partial), 2026-10-20, carried from source-nanda-2023-grokking-modular-addition: there, weight decay drives the late cleanup phase. With StableMax and no weight decay, MLPs grok and still show sparse Fourier weights, a check the authors call "very superficial"; on their reading, weight decay's job is to stop logit growth (raw/arxiv-2501-04697-grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical.md, Appendix C.2, §5.2). The symmetry and signal-noise accounts are known here only by their abstracts, so this page cannot set them against the others in detail.

Superseded, 2026-10-23: the table's softmax-collapse row says float32 rounds "the loss and its gradient" to zero. Only the correct class's gradient rounds to zero; the other classes' gradients stay, so they no longer sum to zero; Prieto et al.'s Figure 2 caption speaks loosely of "the gradient from a large fraction of the samples" going to zero (raw/arxiv-2501-04697-grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical.md, §3.1; raw/arxiv-2610-31642-a-fix-for-softmax-and-cross.md, §2, Eq. 4).

Slingshot spikes (added 2026-10-23)

Barron, Gnanapragasam and Tschannen (2026) read the loss spikes often seen after memorisation, the "slingshot" of Thilak et al. (2022), as a rounding bug meeting Adam. Past the precision cliff (a margin of about 16.6 in fp32) the right class's gradient is zero; Adam's running estimate of gradient size decays; when an example falls back below the cliff, its returning gradient gets a huge step (raw/arxiv-2610-31642-a-fix-for-softmax-and-cross.md, §4). A cross-entropy rewritten to avoid the rounding removes the spikes. With weight decay it groks on every seed: 100 ± 0% against 94 ± 5% test accuracy on a + b mod 31. Without weight decay one spike drops standard cross-entropy from 61% to 23% on a² + b² mod 97, where the fix reaches 85% (same file, §4, Figure 2). With weight decay, the margin settles near log(1/ε) of Adam's ε, 18.4 at the default 1e-8 (same file, §5, Eq. 11). This sides with the softmax-collapse account and sharpens it (source-barron-softmax-fix, concept-softmax-numerics).

Not a clash, 2026-10-23: both papers train with AdamW (raw/arxiv-2501-04697-grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical.md, §2.2). Prieto et al. guessed this mechanism in their Appendix H: zero gradients from collapse let Adam's second moments decay, giving a large update, a slingshot (same file, Appendix H). Barron et al. confirm it and show spikes in setups where Prieto et al. saw learning halt; they frame the difference as regimes, set by LayerNorm and weight decay (raw/arxiv-2610-31642-a-fix-for-softmax-and-cross.md, §4, Figure 2).

Beyond toy tasks

On ResNet18 and GPT-2 small, neither StableMax nor the orthogonal gradient helps much: CIFAR100 59.98% (softmax) against 62.69% (orthogonal gradient); WikiText-103 top-5 60.48% against 51.85% for StableMax (raw/arxiv-2501-04697-grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical.md, Appendix G, Table 1). Grokking is so far a small-network effect in this wiki's sources.

The Goodfire essay cites Nanda et al. as the case where memorisation wins first, in its account of why networks generalise (raw/goodfire-already-clean-2026-10-15.md, App. "Why Networks Generalize"; source-goodfire-already-clean).

Local result

2026-10-23: Vincent's local experiment 03 trained a one-layer transformer on (a + b) mod 97 with full-batch AdamW (learning rate 1e-3, betas 0.9 and 0.98, weight decay 1.0, no LayerNorm) (compass/experiments/03-grokking/RESULTS.md, "Config"); ε and the number format are left at PyTorch's defaults, 1e-8 and float32 (compass/experiments/03-grokking/run.py). After grokking, train and test loss both "oscillate hard", "swinging over an order of magnitude from one eval to the next", which the run did not predict (same file, "Verdict vs pre-registration"). That is the setting where Barron et al. report slingshot spikes (raw/arxiv-2610-31642-a-fix-for-softmax-and-cross.md, §4–5). The link is untested: nobody logged the margin against the fp32 cliff of about 16.6, so the swings may have another cause.

Course module 14 §4 trains a one-layer transformer on (a + b) mod 97 with 30% of the pairs. With weight decay 1.0 it fits the training set at step 130 and passes 95% test accuracy at step 5,900; with weight decay 0 it fits at step 120 and never groks in 20,000 steps, ending at 4.5% (course/modules/14-how-training-finds-a-solution/README.md, §4). The module cites the softmax-collapse paper for the failed run: its final logits span −2,198 to −366, against −115.2 to 11.5 with weight decay (source-grokking-softmax-collapse). A rerun with StableMax and no weight decay would test that reading directly.

Open questions

  • Do the four accounts describe one mechanism from four sides, or compete? Only one has full text here; the others need their papers captured.
  • Does anything like grokking happen in large models, where the training set is never fitted? The softmax-collapse remedies give no clear gain there (raw/arxiv-2501-04697-grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical.md, Appendix G).
  • Are the loss swings often seen after grokking with AdamW all slingshots at the precision cliff? Logging the logit margin against 16.6 (fp32) would tell (concept-softmax-numerics).
  • Is the Fourier circuit a product of weight decay or of the task? (raw/arxiv-2501-04697-grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical.md, Appendix C.2).
