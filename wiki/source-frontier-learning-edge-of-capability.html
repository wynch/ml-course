Frontier Learning: Training LLM Reasoners at the Edge of Capability

Summary

GRPO, the usual RL method for reasoning models, samples several answers to a problem and rewards each against the group's average. If all answers are right, or all wrong, the problem teaches nothing: the gradient is zero. A fixed pool of problems therefore goes stale as the model improves (R, §1, §2).

Frontier learning swaps the fixed pool for a problem generator used during training. Each generator has settings (for Countdown: how many numbers, how large, the target range); one setting is a level, and any level yields as many problems as wanted (R, §3.1). The method keeps a buffer of up to 100 levels and, each step (R, §3.3, §4, Table 6):

  1. Scores each level by regret: for one problem, regret = 1 if solved at least once, minus the success rate. It is zero for problems never solved and for problems always solved, and positive only where the gradient is not zero. A level's regret is the mean over its recent problems; it needs no extra rollouts (R, §3.3.1, Eq. 4-6).
  2. Samples levels by priority: regret plus a small bonus for levels not seen for a while (R, Eq. 7).
  3. Explores: each slot is swapped for a random new level with probability 0.3 (R, §3.3.2, Table 6).
  4. Mutates: a sampled level gets one setting nudged, most often when it is informative (0.4), less when mastered (0.25), rarely when impossible (0.02) (R, §3.3.3, Table 6).

The settings need not rank difficulty in a known order; the method finds the edge from the model's own results (R, §1, Appendix A).

Key results

Five tasks from Reasoning Gym, scored on held-out levels never used in training, greedy decoding, four seeds (R, §5).

  • 200 steps: best on all three tasks. Countdown 50.9 vs 44.7 for the best baseline; Sokoban 45.2 vs 41.1; Decimal Arithmetic 34.7 vs 31.0 (R, Table 1). The gains sit on Easy and Medium levels; on Hard, methods tie (R, §5.1, Appendix F).
  • 500 steps on Dice: baselines plateau after about 100-200 steps; frontier learning keeps rising to 71.8, against 33.3 for the best baseline (SEC), 33.2 for ACE-GRPO, and 25.4 for DAPO matched on 256k rollouts (R, §5.1, Table 2). On Hard and Extra-hard levels it reaches 72.6% and 20.7%; every standard baseline stays under 5.5% (R, §5.1).
  • What carries it: mutation. Without mutation (PLR+Explore) 33.5; with mutation but no exploration (PLR+Mut) 62.5; mutation without regret priority 47.1 (R, Table 2).
  • Other models: Llama-3.2-3B on Countdown 39.2 vs 33.8; Olmo3-7B on Largest Island 73.7 vs 52.0 (R, Table 3).
  • Not just more compute: at 200 steps runtimes match the baselines (R, Appendix H, Tables 8-10). On Dice it runs longer (27.8 h vs 18.7 h for PLR) because its answers grow longer, but at PLR's 18.7 h it already scores 68.0 (R, Appendix H, Table 12).
  • Not an untuned baseline: a learning-rate sweep lifts no baseline above about 35% on Dice (R, Appendix G.2).

Caveats

  • Every test uses levels from the same generators the model trained on; the paper reports no test on outside benchmarks.
  • It needs a procedural generator with explicit settings; the authors name tasks without one as open (R, §6).
  • The 500-step runs add a small KL term and asymmetric clipping for every method (R, Table 5 note). The learning-rate check covers one seed and two baselines (R, Appendix G.2).
  • Spread is wide in places: Dice 71.8 ± 6.2; the mutation-only ablation 47.1 ± 10.8 (R, Table 2).
  • Models from 3B to 7B; tasks are puzzles, simple maths and grids.
