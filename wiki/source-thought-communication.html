Thought Communication in Multiagent Collaboration

source created 2026-09-10 updated 2026-09-10 ai-agents multi-agent latent-communication identifiability sparse-autoencoders llm-reasoning 2025

Authors: Yujia Zheng, Zhuokai Zhao, Zijian Li, Yaqi Xie, Mingze Gao, Lizhu Zhang, Kun Zhang · arXiv: 2510.20733v1 · Date: 2025-10-23

Summary

Most LLM multi-agent systems pass text. This paper argues text is a lossy channel and proposes passing latent state instead — the authors call it thought communication and reach for a telepathy analogy. The formal move: treat each agent's hidden state as H = f(Z) for unknown invertible f over latent "thoughts" Z, then prove that with an ℓ₀ sparsity constraint on the Jacobian (the matrix of partial derivatives saying which thought touches which state dimension) you can recover, up to permutation, both the thoughts any pair of agents share and each agent's private ones — and recover the sharing structure itself, up to relabelling. The system built on that theory, ThoughtComm, trains a sparsity-regularized autoencoder over concatenated agent states, reweights the recovered latents per agent by how many agents depend on them, and injects the result as prefix vectors before each agent's tokens.

Key claims

  • Identifiability without auxiliary information. Three theorems: shared thoughts between any agent pair, private thoughts, and the global sharing structure are all recoverable in a nonparametric setting. The load-bearing assumption is that the generating function varies enough across the population for Jacobians to span their support subspaces.
  • Synthetic recovery works and the regularizer is what does it. With Laplacian latents pushed through random invertible maps, the sparsity-regularized model recovers shared and private components (measured by R² against ground truth); the same model without the regularizer fails.
  • Reported accuracy gains on math benchmarks. Three agents, two debate rounds, 500 train / 500 eval examples on MATH and GSM8K. Qwen-3-1.7B on MATH: 93.0% for ThoughtComm against 75.8% for Multiagent Finetuning and 43.6% for a single answer. Llama-3-8B-Instruct on MATH: 45.6% / 39.68% / 36.2%. Average +67.23% relative over a single agent and +19.06% over Multiagent Finetuning.
  • Holds up under ablation. Accuracy is stable within 5% across prefix lengths 1–16, and gains persist over 2–6 debate rounds while the Multiagent Finetuning baseline degrades as rounds increase.
  • Admitted limits. The method needs access to internal model states, which the authors say is unavailable for closed-source or API-restricted models. Their suggested substitute — context-aware embeddings of observational data — is not tested. The claim that the paradigm extends to all modalities is theoretical, not demonstrated. And the pipeline still trains an autoencoder plus an adapter, so it is cheaper than full finetuning rather than free.

Relevance

  • Core concept-ai-agents material: it attacks the assumption behind nearly every agent framework in the wiki — that agents coordinate through natural language.
  • Sharp conceptual echo of concept-global-workspace: a small shared latent channel broadcast to many consumers, except here the workspace spans separate models rather than living inside one. The J-space work reads a broadcast hub; this builds one.
  • The extraction machinery is the sparse-autoencoder toolkit from source-sae-monosemanticity-2023 repurposed from interpretability to communication — worth flagging under concept-mechanistic-interpretability as a case where an interp method becomes a system component.
  • Direct tension with experiment-edge-federation: that project's stated ambition is transfer across mutually untrusted devices, and ThoughtComm's requirement of raw internal states is exactly the access such a setting cannot assume. Useful as a negative constraint on federation design.
  • For concept-llm-reasoning, treat the numbers with care: a 1.7B model reaching 93% on MATH is a very large jump, measured on a 500-example evaluation subset with a trained adapter in the loop. The theory is the durable part; the benchmark deltas want independent replication.
