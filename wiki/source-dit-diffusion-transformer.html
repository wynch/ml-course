DiT — Diffusion Transformer

source created 2026-05-07 updated 2026-05-07 diffusion transformer generative-models architecture 2022

Peebles & Xie — arXiv 2212.09748, ICCV 2023 (UC Berkeley / NYU)

Replaces the U-Net backbone of source-latent-diffusion-stable-diffusion with a pure transformer operating on patches of the latent. DiT shows that diffusion models inherit the same clean scaling-law behaviour as transformer language models (source-kaplan-scaling-laws, source-chinchilla-compute-optimal) — bigger transformer, more compute, monotonically better FID — and that the inductive biases of the U-Net (convolution, multiscale skip connections) are not load-bearing once you have enough scale.

Method

  • Tokenise: take VAE latents (e.g. 32×32×4 from a Stable-Diffusion-style autoencoder), partition into patches, linearly project. Add positional embeddings. Identical to ViT.
  • Transformer blocks (12, 24, 28 layers in S/B/L/XL configs). Each block conditions on the diffusion timestep t and the class/text embedding c via adaLN-Zero — modulating LayerNorm scales and shifts. This is DiT's main empirical win over earlier conditioning recipes (in-context tokens, cross-attention only).
  • Output head produces noise and (optionally) variance predictions, identical to DDPM training.
  • Variants: DiT-S/B/L/XL at 33M / 130M / 458M / 675M parameters.

Key numbers

  • DiT-XL/2 on class-conditional ImageNet 256×256: FID 2.27, beating prior U-Net-based latent diffusion (LDM 3.60) and pixel-space ADM (FID 4.59).
  • Clean scaling law: FID decreases monotonically with Gflops; the relationship is well-fit by a power law in compute, mirroring LLMs.
  • Outperforms U-Nets at every model size and compute budget tested.

Why it matters

DiT is the architectural rosetta stone that unifies language and image generation under the transformer. Three consequences:

  1. Generative scaling laws are universal. The same compute / parameters / data curves that govern LLMs also govern image diffusion, if the architecture is a transformer. U-Nets have ceilings; transformers don't (yet). This is the missing piece that motivated frontier labs to bet on transformer diffusion.
  2. It is the architecture of the modern frontier: OpenAI's Sora (video), Stable Diffusion 3 (image), Flux, Hunyuan, Sora-2, Veo all use DiT or close variants (with modifications like MMDiT for joint text–image attention). Imagen-3 likewise. Within ~18 months of publication, DiT displaced U-Net almost entirely at the frontier.
  3. adaLN-Zero as conditioning primitive. The "modulate LayerNorm with conditioning" pattern reappears across modalities — it's a more parameter-efficient and stable way to inject t and c than cross-attention everywhere.

DiT also sets up productive tension with one-step / few-step alternatives: standard DiT still needs 20–50 denoising steps, which is why source-flow-map-language-models and consistency-model / flow-matching lines of work aim to collapse this into a single function evaluation. DiT establishes the backbone; flow-matching and shortcut models change the trajectory.

The deeper claim DiT operationalises: inductive bias is a crutch you can shed at scale. Just as transformers killed convolutional bias for vision (ViT) and recurrence for sequences (source-attention-is-all-you-need), they killed the U-Net's multiscale skip-connection bias for diffusion. The pattern keeps repeating across the wiki.
