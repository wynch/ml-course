MeBP: backpropagation on a phone

Summary

Fine-tuning needs far more memory than running a model, so on phones people have used zeroth-order methods (MeZO): estimate the gradient from two forward passes with a random nudge, no backward pass. That fits in memory but needs 10× to 100× more steps (R, Abstract, §2). MeBP keeps true backpropagation and makes it fit instead (R, §3).

How (R, §3, Algorithm 1):

  • Train only LoRA adapters (rank 16); the base weights stay frozen, stored at 4 bits.
  • Gradient checkpointing: cut the model into blocks (one per transformer layer, plus the final linear layer and the loss), keep only each block's input, and recompute inside the block during the backward pass.
  • Keep almost nothing in RAM: base weights are memory-mapped and decompressed one block at a time, when needed; the saved block inputs are memory-mapped to storage too. Only the LoRA weights and their gradients, tens of megabytes, stay resident.
  • Each block's forward and backward graphs are compiled ahead of time and run on the phone from Swift.

  • A MeBP step takes 43% to 94% longer than a MeZO step and up to 20% more memory (R, §4.2).
  • But the authors find MeZO far behind per step: in server-side runs on WikiText-2, backpropagation improves loss and next-token accuracy within 100 steps; MeZO after 100,000 steps still ends worse (R, §4.1, Figure 1). Three improved zeroth-order methods also stay behind (R, Appendix A).
  • Memory is about 10× below an earlier phone implementation, PocketLLM's MeZO, which used 6.5 GB for OPT-1.3B (R, §2, §4.2).
  • Decompressing weights takes 32–43% of forward time and 13–24% of backward time (R, Table 2).
  • Cost grows with sequence length: for Qwen2.5 1.5B, 6.92 s and 405 MB at 128 tokens, 34.40 s and 994 MB at 1,024 (R, Table 3). The authors point to short texts: messages, brief emails, prompts (R, §4.2).
  • The bottleneck in memory and time is the final linear layer and the loss, where the vocabulary-sized product sits (R, §4.2, Figure 2).

Caveats

  • "All tested LLMs ... within 1GB" (R, §4.2): their own Table 1 gives 1029.49 MB for Gemma3 4B, just over.
  • The accuracy evidence is server-side simulation, shown only in figures (lost in this capture); on the phone the paper measures time and memory per step, not training to convergence (R, §4.1, §4.2).
  • One task (language modelling on 2,048 WikiText-2 samples), one phone, iOS only, A17 Pro or newer (R, §4.1, Limitations).
  • Background training on a phone faces OS memory limits and interruptions; the paper cites them but does not test training in the background (R, §1).
