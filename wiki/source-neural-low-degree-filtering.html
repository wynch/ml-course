Deep Learning as Neural Low-Degree Filtering

source created 2026-05-14 updated 2026-05-14 deep-learning-theory representation-learning feature-learning spectral-methods generalization low-degree 2026

Dandi, Vilucchio, Arnaboldi, Tabanelli & Krzakala — arXiv 2605.13612v1, May 2026

This paper proposes Neural Low-Degree Filtering as a theoretical model of hierarchical feature learning. The central move is to treat deep learning as an iterative spectral filtering process: each layer receives the current representation, then selects directions that have strong low-degree correlation with the label and are accessible from that representation.

The paper is useful for the wiki because it gives a concrete, spectral account of how depth can build features rather than merely scale a fixed random-feature map.

Core Claim

Neural networks do not only fit labels in a fixed feature space. In the paper's stylized limit, each layer changes what features are available to the next layer:

current representation -> accessible low-degree correlations -> selected features -> next representation

That makes hierarchical feature learning explicit. The layer sequence becomes a chain of feature filters, where later layers can discover compositions that were not directly visible at the input.

Why "Low-Degree" Matters

Low-degree structure is a common bridge between statistical learning theory and practical deep learning:

  • low-degree correlations are often the first learnable signal under finite samples
  • higher-order concepts may become accessible only after earlier features have been constructed
  • depth can progressively expose compositional structure instead of forcing one shallow kernel to represent everything at once

The paper's contribution is to turn this into an iterative layer-by-layer mechanism rather than a static approximation claim.

Contrast With Lazy or Kernel Regimes

The theory is positioned against lazy random-feature baselines. In a lazy regime, the representation is mostly fixed and learning happens mainly in the final linear readout. Neural Low-Degree Filtering instead gives a tractable proxy for feature learning beyond the lazy regime: each layer can change the feature basis available to the next layer.

This makes it adjacent to source-litman-generalization-theory, but with a different emphasis:

  • source-litman-generalization-theory emphasizes signal/noise routing under optimization.
  • This paper emphasizes spectral selection and low-degree compositionality across layers.

Together, they add a 2026 theory thread: deep learning may be explainable as structured routing/filtering of signal, not only as scale plus optimization.

Fit With The Wavelet-Substrate Thread

The connection to idea-wavelet-substrate-for-edge-intelligence is conceptual, not direct.

The wavelet-substrate idea asks whether early perception can start from fixed mathematical filters and reserve learning for residuals, composition, and uncertainty. Neural Low-Degree Filtering asks how learned networks may discover useful filters layer by layer through spectral access to label correlations.
