Stéphane Mallat

entity created 2026-05-14 updated 2026-05-14 researcher wavelets scattering-transform signal-processing representation-learning data-science

Stéphane Mallat is an applied mathematician and signal-processing researcher whose work connects wavelets, multiresolution analysis, harmonic analysis, and modern machine learning. He holds the Data Science chair at the Collège de France and is associated with École normale supérieure. In 2025 he received the CNRS Gold Medal for work at the intersection of mathematics, signal processing, and artificial intelligence.

For this wiki, Mallat is important because he offers a non-fashion-based route into deep learning: start from mathematically structured signal operators, then ask what neural networks learn beyond those operators.

Key Contributions

  • Multiresolution and wavelets: formal tools for separating signal variation by scale.
  • Wavelet scattering transform: a deep convolution-like cascade using fixed wavelet filters and modulus nonlinearities rather than learned early filters.
  • Invariant and stable representations: scattering builds translation invariance and stability to small deformations into the representation.
  • Scattering spectra and phase harmonics: later work uses structured wavelet statistics to model non-Gaussian fields, physical processes, and geometry beyond ordinary power spectra.
  • Moment-based generative modeling: 2026 work uses scattering moments as constraints or feature maps for maximum-entropy generation and training-free stochastic interpolants.
  • Mathematics of neural networks: Mallat's research program repeatedly asks what convolutional networks are doing in signal-processing terms.

Why This Matters Here

The current author research line asks whether edge intelligence can reserve learning capacity for composition, residuals, and control rather than relearning generic low-level filters. Mallat's scattering program is a direct precedent: it makes the early part of a deep network mostly fixed, stable, and interpretable.

Open Questions For The Wiki

  • When does fixed multiscale structure beat learned early layers on sample efficiency, robustness, and on-device adaptation?
  • When does scattering's useful stability erase information needed for identity, geometry, causality, or control?
  • Can a higher-level rule feed back into a fixed wavelet substrate by changing scale, orientation, sparsity, or residual routing, without turning the whole system into a generic learned model?
