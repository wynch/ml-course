Next-Latent Prediction (NextLat)

source created 2026-06-18 updated 2026-06-18 world-models self-supervised-learning representation-learning transformers llm-reasoning test-time-compute

Authors: Jayden Teoh, Manan Tomar, Kwangjun Ahn, Edward S. Hu, Tim Pearce, Pratyusha Sharma, Akshay Krishnamurthy, Riashat Islam, Alex Lamb, John Langford Date: 2025-11-08 arXiv: 2511.05963

Summary

NextLat adds a self-supervised next-latent prediction objective on top of ordinary next-token training: the transformer learns to predict its own next latent state given the next token. The motivation is that next-token prediction gives a transformer no incentive to compress history into compact latent states with consistent transition rules — so it can learn solutions that generalize poorly. The auxiliary latents provably converge toward belief states (the compressed history sufficient to predict the future), injecting a recurrent inductive bias while leaving the architecture, parallel-training efficiency, and inference unchanged.

Key Ideas

  • The objective: predict next latent given next token — a cheap auxiliary loss layered onto next-token prediction.
  • Belief states. The learned latents provably trend toward sufficient statistics of history; the transformer ends up carrying a compact internal world model rather than re-deriving state from the full context each step.
  • Architecture-preserving. A recurrent/state-tracking inductive bias without giving up the transformer's parallel training or inference characteristics.
  • Broad gains. Improvements over next-token prediction and other baselines across world modeling, reasoning, planning, and language modeling — in downstream accuracy, representation compression, and lookahead planning.
  • Self-speculative decoding. Predictable latents enable variable-length self-speculative decoding, up to 3.3× faster inference in language modeling.
