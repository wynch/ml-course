Mallat 2026 Moment-Based Generative Models

source created 2026-05-14 updated 2026-05-14 stephane-mallat generative-models diffusion stochastic-interpolants maximum-entropy wavelet-scattering representation-learning

Two recent 2026 papers with Stéphane Mallat connect wavelet/scattering moments to generative modeling rather than directly to edge classification:

  • MGD: Moment Guided Diffusion for Maximum Entropy Generation — Lempereur, Cuvelle-Magar, Coeurdoux, Mallat, Vanden-Eijnden; arXiv 2602.17211, submitted 2026-02-19.
  • Training-Free Generative Modeling via Kernelized Stochastic Interpolants — Coeurdoux, Lempereur, Cuvelle-Magar, Eboli, Mallat, Borovykh, Vanden-Eijnden; arXiv 2602.20070, submitted 2026-02-23 and revised 2026-02-25.

Main Ideas

Moment Guided Diffusion combines maximum-entropy moment constraints with stochastic interpolants. It aims to sample distributions from limited moment information without the slow mixing of classical maximum-entropy sampling in high dimensions. The paper applies this to financial time series, turbulent flows, and cosmological fields using wavelet scattering moments.

Training-Free Generative Modeling via Kernelized Stochastic Interpolants replaces neural-network training with linear systems in the stochastic-interpolant framework. It explicitly allows feature maps such as scattering transforms, supporting training-free generation and model combination.

Why It Matters Here

These papers push the wavelet-substrate idea beyond classification:

  • scattering moments can be treated as constraints on a generated world, not just features for a classifier
  • a mathematical feature map can guide a generative process
  • "learn only what remains" can become "generate only what satisfies structured moment constraints"

For experiment-wavelet-substrate-network, this is not a first milestone. It is a later direction for residual prediction:

signal -> wavelet/scattering moments -> residual surprise -> constrained generation or prediction

Connection To Strange Loops

The strange-loop angle is indirect but useful. If higher-level learned structure can impose moment constraints on lower-level generated or predicted signals, then interpretation feeds back into perception:

low-level scattering state -> high-level moment model -> generated/predicted signal -> residual against low-level state

That loop is more precise than vague self-reference, but it is also harder to benchmark.

Caveat

These are generative/statistical papers, not edge-device learning papers. They should influence the residual-learning and prediction branch, not the first classifier benchmark.
