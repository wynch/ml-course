Jason Wei

entity created 2026-05-07 updated 2026-05-07 researcher author google-brain openai llm-reasoning chain-of-thought emergence scaling

LLM-capabilities researcher; Google Brain era (chain-of-thought, emergent abilities, instruction tuning) then later at OpenAI. In this wiki he occupies a rare double position: he is first author of both the canonical chain-of-thought paper and the emergent-abilities paper that frames one side of the wiki's central scaling-laws / emergence debate.

Sources in the wiki

  • source-chain-of-thought-foundations — first author of "Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models" (Wei, Wang, Schuurmans, Bosma, Ichter, Xia, Chi, Le, Zhou; Jan 2022, arXiv 2201.11903). Founded the test-time-reasoning era; the few-shot prompt with intermediate reasoning steps that the entire downstream concept-test-time-compute / o1-style stack is descended from.
  • source-emergence-debate — first author of "Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models" (Wei, Tay, Bommasani, Raffel, Zoph, Borgeaud, Yogatama, Bosma, Zhou, Metzler, Chi, Hashimoto, Vinyals, Liang, Dean, Fedus; TMLR 2022, arXiv 2206.07682). The pro-emergence half of the debate that the Schaeffer rebuttal attacks on metric-choice grounds.

Themes

Wei's wiki footprint is a two-paper, same-year double: in 2022 he establishes both the empirical claim ("scale produces qualitatively new capabilities") and the methodological technique ("you elicit those capabilities by prompting for intermediate reasoning") that together define the post-GPT-3 LLM reasoning paradigm. Then, crucially, Schaeffer et al. (2023) target the metric assumptions behind the first paper using tasks largely drawn from the second. That makes Wei the wiki's clearest example of an author on both sides of a methodological debate at once — pro-emergence on the claim side, and (via CoT) the source of many of the benchmarks the debate happens on. Beyond the wiki, his line continues into instruction tuning and the o1 / reasoning-model era at OpenAI.
