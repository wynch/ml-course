Summary

A looped language model runs the same stack of layers several times per token: Ouro 1.4B is 24 layers run 4 times, Ouro 2.6B is 48 layers run 4 times (R, §3.1, §4.3.1, Table 2). The extra depth costs compute, not parameters. The authors pretrain these models on 7.7T tokens, far beyond earlier looped models, and ask whether looping pays at that scale (R, §1, §4).

  • An exit gate per loop. After each loop a small gate gives the chance of stopping. Training weights each loop's loss by that chance and adds an entropy bonus, so the gate neither always stops late nor always early (R, §3.2–3.3). A second stage, with the model frozen, trains the gate to stop when one more loop no longer lowers the loss by more than 0.005 (R, §3.4). At inference, a threshold on the summed stopping chance sets the trade between compute and accuracy (R, §3.2).
  • Training. Eight loops at first caused loss spikes; they cut to four. The 2.6B model was made from the 1.4B by duplicating its 24 layers (R, §4.3). Data are open datasets only (R, §4.2).
  • RL failed. RL with checkable rewards (DAPO, GRPO) gave no gain over the fine-tuned model, partly because fast rollout engines assume a fixed depth (R, §4.5).

The gains sit on reasoning-heavy benchmarks; on MMLU, which leans on stored knowledge, both trail Qwen3 (R, §5.1, Appendix B.4).

  • Loops past training hurt. Trained at four loops, benchmark scores peak at four and fall at five to eight; at one loop the 1.4B scores 41.21 on MMLU against 67.45 at four (R, §5.3, Table 10). The Thinking models score near zero at one loop (AIME 2024: 0.00 and 3.00) and decline past five (R, Tables 12–13). Only the safety score keeps improving past four loops (R, §7.1, Figure 8a).
  • Looping adds no storage. In synthetic biography tests, looped and plain GPT-2-style models from 1M to 40M parameters both hold about 2 bits of facts per parameter (R, §6.1, Figure 6). On a modular-arithmetic task that composes facts, looped models beat plain models with the same parameters and often those with the same compute: at expression length 24, 3 layers looped 4 times score 92.2% against 34.8% for 12 plain layers (R, §6.2, Figure 6). The authors' reading: looping helps use knowledge, not store it.
  • Early exit. On MMLU, the trained gate gives about 66% at an average of 2.5 loops, against about 64% for the gate from pretraining alone; a simple rule (stop when the hidden state changes little) comes within 1–2 points (R, §5.4.1, Figure 5; read from a plot).
  • KV cache. Keeping only the last loop's cache during decoding cuts cache memory 4× for 78.85 against 78.92 on GSM8K; keeping only the first loop's drops it to 18.73. Prefill needs all four caches (R, §5.4.2, Table 14). Agreement, 2026-10-23: this paper itself reports that reusing caches while reading the prompt degrades performance by more than 10 points on GSM8K (R, §5.4.2, "Prefilling Phase"). BeNice! (Prairie et al.) shares the last loop's cache during prompt processing and finds Ouro 1.4B's Core score falling from 54.57 to 25.17, and 2.6B's from 59.78 to 26.58 (raw/arxiv-2610-31633-benice-closing-looping-s-inference-memory.md, Table 1, Appendix C), which confirms that finding; source-benice-kv-sharing-looped.
  • Answers change across loops. On 1,000 Quora question pairs, only 36.1% of loop-2 answers match loop 4; a probe predicts Qwen3-4B-Thinking's final answer from before its written reasoning with ROC AUC 0.99 (R, §7.2, Figure 9). The authors read this as faithful latent reasoning.

Caveats

  • Ouro runs 4 passes through its layers per token, so "1.4B matches 4B" compares parameters, not compute (R, §1). In the authors' own small-scale runs (53M to 1.36B, 20B tokens), a plain model with the same depth, and so more parameters, always scores higher than the looped one (R, Appendix D.1, Table 18).
  • Contradiction, 2026-10-20: an independent harness scores the released Thinking models lower than this paper does. source-loopcd-decoding-looped-transformers gets AIME 2024 pass@1 of 50.83 (1.4B) and 61.88 (2.6B), OlympiadBench 61.27 and 64.05 (raw/arxiv-2610-02185-decoding-looped-transformers-better-for-almost.md, Table 1a); this paper reports 65.0 and 64.7, and 71.6 and 76.4 (R, Table 9). This paper grades with an LLM judge at temperature 1.0 (R, §5.2); LoopCD uses 16 samples, integer matching for AIME and symbolic equivalence on the 674-problem English maths subset of OlympiadBench (same LoopCD file, Appendix B.2).
  • Comparisons are against other labs' base models trained on different data and token counts, from 2T to 36T (R, Tables 7–8).
  • The faithfulness claim rests on answers changing between loops and on probes, not on an intervention; the authors say they cannot manipulate the latent process (R, §7.2).
  • "Up to 12B" (R, Abstract) holds on some benchmarks: on Table 8, Gemma3 12B leads on ARC-C, HellaSwag and Winogrande.
