Exploring LLMs with MLX and the M5 Neural Accelerators

Every claim below cites the raw file as R, with its section.

Summary

MLX is Apple's open-source array framework for Apple silicon, with a NumPy-like API, automatic differentiation and one memory pool shared by CPU and GPU (R, "What is MLX"). MLX LM, built on it, runs and fine-tunes most language models on Hugging Face and quantises a model in seconds (R, "Running LLMs on Apple Silicon").

The post's news: the M5 chip puts "Neural Accelerators" inside the GPU, units for matrix multiplication, and MLX uses them through the TensorOps and Metal Performance Primitives of Metal 4 (R, "Inference Performance on M5 with MLX"). It needs macOS 26.2 or later (R, footnote 1). These are GPU units; the post never mentions the separate Neural Engine (ANE).

Key claims

  • Set-up. A MacBook Pro with M5 and 24 GB against a similar M4 machine; prompts of 4,096 tokens, then 128 generated tokens, through mlx_lm.generate (R, "Inference Performance on M5 with MLX").
  • First token, compute-bound. The M5 gets the first token in under 10 s for a dense 14B model and under 3 s for a 30B MoE (R, "Time to First Token"). Speed-ups over the M4 run from 3.33× (gpt-oss-20b) to 4.06× (Qwen3-14B, 4-bit) (R, Table 1).
  • Later tokens, bandwidth-bound. Generation is 19–27% faster, in line with memory bandwidth: 120 GB/s on the M4, 153 GB/s on the M5, 28% more (R, "Time to First Token"; Table 1 gives 1.19–1.27×).
  • Memory. All six models fit in 24 GB; Qwen3-8B in BF16 takes 17.46 GB, Qwen3-30B-A3B at 4 bits 17.31 GB, Qwen3-14B at 4 bits 9.16 GB (R, Table 1).
  • Images. A 1024×1024 image from FLUX-dev at 4 bits (12B parameters) is "more than 3.8x faster" on the M5 (R, closing paragraph).

Caveats

  • Apple measures its own hardware. One prompt length, one generation length, batch of one; no energy or heat figures.
  • Inference only. The post says MLX can fine-tune, but gives no training numbers on the M5.
  • The seconds behind "under 10 s" and "under 3 s" sit in Figure 1, which this capture lost; the wiki has only the two bounds and the ratios.
  • A blog post, not a paper; no named authors.
