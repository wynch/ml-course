Multimodal Models

concept created 2026-05-06 updated 2026-07-04 multimodal vision-language clip llava flamingo jepa

Models that handle multiple input modalities — typically vision + text, sometimes audio, video, robot actions, or 3D. This page also covers multimodal reasoning (reasoning across modalities specifically — see section below).

This page is the hub. The lineage of modern vision-language models clusters into three programmes that disagree on the right interface between vision and language:

  1. Contrastive alignment — push paired (image, text) embeddings together in a shared space. CLIP is the foundational reference.
  2. Token-space VLMs — feed visual features into a (frozen or jointly trained) LLM and let it autoregressively generate text. Flamingo and LLaVA are the canonical references.
  3. Embedding-space prediction — predict embeddings of target text rather than generate tokens. The JEPA programme's vision-language move.

A fourth thread — pure-vision SSL, source-dino-lineage — sits adjacent: it argues you do not need any text supervision to learn good visual features, and matches CLIP on most visual benchmarks without it.

Three programmes for vision-language

Contrastive image-text alignment

The foundational paper is source-clip-radford (Radford 2021, OpenAI). Dual encoders — image ViT and text Transformer — trained on 400M (image, text) pairs from the web with a symmetric InfoNCE loss. Zero-shot ImageNet matches a fully supervised ResNet-50.

CLIP became the default vision encoder for the entire post-2021 multimodal-LLM stack (most VLMs plug a CLIP-style ViT into a frozen or jointly-trained LLM).

Successors and challengers:

  • SigLIP, OpenCLIP, Perception Encoder (incremental improvements; same recipe).
  • source-dino-lineage (Oquab 2023) — matches CLIP on most visual benchmarks without text supervision, undermining the "you need language to learn vision" reading of CLIP.
  • source-vl-jepa (Chen 2025) — beats CLIP, SigLIP2, and Perception Encoder on the average of 16 video benchmarks via embedding-space prediction.

Vision encoders grafted onto LLMs (token-space VLMs)

The dominant pattern for instruction-following multimodal assistants:

  • source-flamingo (Alayrac 2022, DeepMind) — frozen LM (Chinchilla) + frozen vision encoder + gated cross-attention layers + Perceiver Resampler. First VLM with strong few-shot in-context learning across modalities. SOTA on 16 multimodal benchmarks with only a handful of examples per task.
  • BLIP-2 (Li 2023) — replaces Flamingo's heavy adapter with a smaller Q-Former that lives between vision and language.
  • source-llava-visual-instruction (Liu 2023) — radical simplification: a single linear projection maps CLIP-ViT features into LLaMA's word-embedding space; instruction-tune on GPT-4-generated multimodal data. Open-source counterpart to GPT-4V.
  • GPT-4V, Claude vision, Gemini vision — closed-source frontier VLMs; reported architectures align with this pattern.

The trajectory is one of adapter simplification: Flamingo's gated cross-attention → BLIP-2's Q-Former → LLaVA's linear layer. The lesson: vision tokens can simply live in the LLM's input space if you have decent training data.

Embedding-space prediction (JEPA programme)

The JEPA family (concept-jepa) argues prediction-in-embedding-space beats both pixel-level generation and token-level generation:

  • source-ijepa-self-supervised-learning — image-only JEPA; predict embeddings of masked patches.
  • source-vjepa-family — extends to video, becomes a world model for zero-shot robot control.
  • source-vl-jepa (Dec 2025) — applies the JEPA move to vision-language: predict the embedding of the target text, not its tokens. ~50% fewer trainable params than a token-space VLM trained on the same data, with stronger average performance. Beats CLIP / SigLIP2 / Perception Encoder on 16 video benchmarks.

The deep argument: token-space generation forces the model to commit to surface form (exact wording, punctuation, register) that no VQA / classification / retrieval task cares about. Embedding-space prediction abstracts that away — the language analogue of I-JEPA's "pixel reconstruction wastes capacity" argument.

Pure-vision SSL (the orthogonal thread)

source-dino-lineage is the major datapoint that vision can be learned without language supervision:

  • Self-distillation + masked-image-modeling + curated 142M-image dataset (LVD-142M).
  • Matches or beats CLIP on linear-probe ImageNet, dense tasks, OOD robustness, domain transfer.
  • Validates the source-lecun-path-autonomous-intelligence thesis: vision representations don't need text.

This makes the choice between CLIP and DINOv2 backbones a real one for downstream VLMs — and informs the JEPA programme's confidence that text-aware vision pretraining isn't the only path.

Multimodal Reasoning (formerly its own concept page)

Reasoning across multiple modalities — text, images, video, audio. Distinct from the encoding/alignment question above: here the focus is whether a model can integrate evidence and infer over time, causality, and structure that span modalities.

  • source-clevrer-video-reasoning — diagnostic video dataset testing temporal and causal reasoning across question types; models excel at perception but fail at causal/counterfactual questions.

Tensions / open questions

  • Tokens or embeddings? Token-space VLMs (LLaVA, Flamingo, GPT-4V) generate text autoregressively. Embedding-space VLMs (VL-JEPA) predict semantic embeddings directly. The right interface depends on whether your downstream task cares about surface form. Generation tasks need tokens; classification / retrieval / VQA arguably don't.
  • Text supervision or not? CLIP says vision needs language to learn good features at scale. DINOv2 says it doesn't. DINOv3 (source-dino-lineage) extends the SSL programme further. Many production VLMs still use CLIP-style encoders for the alignment, but DINOv2 backbones increasingly compete.
  • Adapter complexity? Flamingo's gated cross-attention vs LLaVA's linear projection — both work, latter is simpler. The "minimum viable adapter" question is open.
  • Multi-image / interleaved inputs. Flamingo handles arbitrarily interleaved sequences via its Perceiver Resampler; LLaVA's single-image setup is limited. Modern open VLMs (IDEFICS, OBELICS-trained) re-import Flamingo-style interleaving.
