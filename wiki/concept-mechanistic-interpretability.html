Mechanistic Interpretability

concept created 2026-04-05 updated 2026-04-13 interpretability features circuits sparse-autoencoders neural-symbolic

The study of neural network internals — understanding what models learn, how they represent knowledge, and how information flows through layers. The goal is to reverse-engineer learned algorithms into human-understandable components. Viewed through this wiki's neural-symbolic thesis (concept-neural-symbolic-bridge), mech interp is the readout side of the bridge: extracting symbolic structure from weights.

Key tension

The field is split between:

  1. External analysis — SAEs, probing, activation patching, attribution graphs (scalable, potentially miss emergent properties).
  2. Self-explanation — introspective interpretability, predictive concept decoders, self-training for explanations (natural language but may be unfaithful).
  3. Data-side control — patterning (steers behavior without understanding internals).
  4. Decompilation — RASP extraction, weights-to-code, formal methods (exact but requires cooperative substrates).

Open questions

  • Can SAEs capture everything, or is there "dark matter" in activations?
  • Is introspective interpretability achievable without training specifically for it?
  • How do we validate that interpretations are faithful, not just plausible?
  • Can decompilation techniques reach frontier-scale stock models, or do they require architectural cooperation?
  • What's the minimum primitive set for a "language" of features — composition, types, quantifiers?
