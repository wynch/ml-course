AdaptiveEmbed — Per-Sample Capacity in Multi-Vector Retrieval

source created 2026-09-10 updated 2026-09-10 retrieval multimodal representation-learning embeddings contrastive-learning

Authors: Xinze Liu, Lei Yang, Dayan Wu et al. (Institute of Information Engineering, CAS) · arXiv: 2608.25412v1 · Date: 2026-08-26

Summary

Multi-vector retrieval represents one item with several embeddings instead of one, so fine detail survives compression. Every such system so far fixes the count: every image gets 8 vectors, every caption gets 4. This paper asks whether that count should be a per-sample decision, names the setting SAMVR, and builds one system for it. Stage I learns a pool of eight tokens per sample with a Q-Former (a small module that squeezes encoder states into a few learned query vectors), organised as two ordered four-token groups trained Matryoshka-style — every prefix of a group is itself a usable representation. Stage II decides how many of those tokens to switch on: a small Token Selection Transformer looks at how the currently selected tokens rank items in a fixed reference bank, and either adds the next token or stops. It is trained on the measured gain in retrieval AP from each possible addition, with every admissible action's utility enumerated rather than sampled. Matching uses SetSim: an exact maximum-weight one-to-one assignment between two equal-size sets, which unlike MaxSim (every query vector takes its own best match, reuse allowed) is symmetric, so one embedding set serves both retrieval directions.

Key claims

  • Fewer vectors, slightly better numbers. COCO text-to-image / image-to-text mAP 63.45 / 61.14 at 2.1 average active tokens, against MetaEmbed(2/4) at 62.31 / 60.48 with 3 tokens and MetaEmbed(16/64) at 62.27 / 59.12 with 40. Best on 6 of 8 image–text directions; loses Flickr30K I2T (75.72 vs 77.29) and ADE20K I2T (47.27 vs 47.88).
  • Fixed capacity does not buy accuracy. Going from 3 to 40 tokens does not consistently improve any of the four image–text benchmarks. This is the paper's most useful negative result and it is measured, not argued.
  • The gain comes from allocation, not from a better encoder. Holding the trained embedding set fixed and varying only the selection, adaptive allocation beats every fixed-k operating point at lower average capacity.
  • Generalises across modality pairs. Best multi-vector result on 7 of 8 video–text and audio–text directions, at 1.9 average tokens on ActivityNet, with only the encoder swapped.
  • Both components earn their place. Removing multi-group training costs most on audio (Clotho T2A 32.56 → 30.16); removing the bank-feedback signal costs accuracy and raises average capacity to 2.5 tokens.
  • A concrete argument against MaxSim at scale. As MetaEmbed grows from 2/4 to 16/64 tokens, the fraction of query tokens matching distinct database tokens falls from 100.0% to 80.8% — extra vectors pile onto the same targets. SetSim's bijection rules this out by construction.
  • Cheap at query time. ~2 active tokens keeps set matching far under high-capacity late-interaction cost; SetSim is solved exactly by a batched dynamic program for k ≤ 8.

Limitations

  • No limitations section; the conclusion calls AdaptiveEmbed "an initial framework" and SAMVR "a starting point", which is the honest reading of the numbers.
  • The oracle gap is the real story. A non-deployable oracle picking the best representation per sample scores 69.51 / 67.85 on COCO at 2.2 tokens, against the learned policy's 63.45 / 61.14. The learned selector captures a small slice of what the setting allows; most of the claimed potential is unrealised.
  • Accuracy gains over the best fixed baseline are around one point in-domain. The defensible claim is same accuracy at a third to a twentieth of the vectors, not a large retrieval win.
  • Encoders (Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B, WAVE-7B) are frozen; image–text training uses COCO only. Allocation needs a reference bank at inference, which is extra machinery the fixed-capacity baselines do not carry.

Relevance

  • source-bundle-retrieval-and-rag — a new axis for that cluster: not which representation but how much of it, decided per item.
  • source-colbert — MaxSim late interaction is precisely what SetSim replaces, and the 100.0% → 80.8% coverage measurement is the first number in the wiki that quantifies a cost of MaxSim as vector counts grow.
  • source-bge-m3-embeddings — M3 said don't choose between dense, sparse and late interaction; this says don't choose a fixed capacity either. Same instinct, applied to budget rather than to paradigm.
  • concept-multimodal — the same allocation policy holds across image, video and audio with only an encoder swap, which is weak evidence that capacity demand is a property of content rather than of modality.
  • concept-representation-learning — Matryoshka-style nested prefixes reappear here as the substrate that makes per-sample truncation coherent at all.
  • Gap: the wiki has no page on multi-vector retrieval theory (checked for arXiv 2608.21494 — absent), so the capacity question currently has no conceptual home beyond the retrieval bundle.
