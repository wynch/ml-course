LoRA Without Regret

Summary

LoRA trains a small low-rank update beside each frozen weight matrix in place of the matrix itself (source-lora-low-rank-adaptation). The post asks when that matches full fine-tuning (FullFT). Setup: ranks 1 to 512, a learning-rate sweep for every condition, constant schedule; Llama 3 and Qwen3 models, one of them a mixture of experts; supervised runs on Tulu3 (instruction following) and OpenThoughts3 (reasoning), scored by log loss; RL runs on maths with answer correctness as reward (R, "Methods and results").

It finds a "low-regret regime" covering most post-training: LoRA matches FullFT when it is on all layers and has more trainable parameters than the information it must learn (R, "What matters for LoRA", "Discussion").

Key results

  • Capacity. High-rank LoRA and FullFT follow the same curve, loss falling linearly in the log of steps; lower ranks leave that curve at a point that depends on rank, when the adapter runs out of room (R, "LoRA rank").
  • Learning rate. The best learning rate for LoRA is about 10× that of FullFT, in supervised learning and RL alike; across ranks 4 to 512 it changes by less than 2× (R, "LoRA rank", "Optimal learning rates for LoRA vs. FullFT"). For runs under about 100 steps, about 15× (R, "Learning rates in short and long runs").
  • Batch size. In some settings LoRA pays more than FullFT as the batch grows, whatever the rank; the gap is smaller at batch 32. Both do best at small batches (R, "Batch size effects").
  • Which layers. Attention-only LoRA underperforms MLP-only LoRA, even at equal parameter count (attention rank 256, 0.25B, against MLP rank 128, 0.24B, on Llama-3.1-8B); this holds for a dense model and an MoE (R, "Layers Where LoRA Is Applied").
  • RL needs little capacity. LoRA matches FullFT for policy-gradient RL "even with ranks as low as 1" (R, "Reinforcement learning"). The argument: an episode with one reward at the end teaches about 1 bit, so 10,000 MATH problems × 32 samples is about 320,000 bits, against 3M parameters in a rank-1 LoRA on Llama-3.1-8B (same section). Larger runs on DeepMath with Qwen3-8B-Base agree (same).
  • Compute. A LoRA forward-backward pass costs slightly more than two-thirds of FullFT's FLOPs (R, "Compute efficiency advantage of LoRA").

Caveats

  • A company blog, not peer-reviewed; the company sells LoRA fine-tuning through Tinker.
  • Supervised results are log loss, not task scores; the authors chose this for clean curves (R, "What matters for LoRA").
  • The RL bits argument is an estimate; the authors say they cannot yet forecast exactly when LoRA matches FullFT (R, "Open questions").
  • LoRA can still lose to FullFT on datasets larger than its capacity, which is the pretraining-like regime (R, opening, "What matters for LoRA").
