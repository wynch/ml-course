Active Inference

concept created 2026-04-05 updated 2026-10-24 active-inference free-energy-principle neuroscience robotics hierarchical-generative-models

A neuroscience-inspired framework where agents act to minimise surprise (free energy) by updating both their beliefs and their environment. Rooted in Karl Friston's Free Energy Principle, gaining traction as an alternative to reward-based RL.

In the Wiki

Foundations

  • source-active-inference-foundations — Friston et al. 2017 (process theory, Neural Computation; expected free energy split into epistemic and pragmatic value) and Parr, Pezzulo & Friston 2022 (MIT Press textbook).

Hierarchical generative models

  • source-schema-based-active-inference — Pezzulo et al.'s S-HAI: hierarchical active inference with schemas, reproduces rodent mPFC neural codes (goal-progress, conjunctive, place-like cells) and behavioural schema generalisation (arXiv 2601.18946).
  • source-why-learn-if-you-can-infer-active-inference — VERSES: arm reaching beats an RL baseline as joints are added (NeurIPS 2024 workshop); on Habitat household tasks, hand-tuned active-inference skills reach 66.5% success against 54.7% for RL (arXiv 2507.17338).
  • AXIOM (Heins et al., arXiv 2505.24784; not in raw) runs active inference with object-centric mixture models that grow as needed. On the Gameworld 10k games it matches or beats BBF and DreamerV3 in average reward with 0.3–1.6M parameters against 6.47M and 420M (§3, Table 2). Vendor figures quoted elsewhere (up to 60% better, 39× faster) are not in the paper and were not checked.

Cognitive applications

  • source-active-inference-empathy — empathy integrated into active inference for socially adaptive robot behaviour. Agents empathise with others to reduce collective free energy.

Predictive coding as a learning rule

  • source-pc-alm-augmented-lagrangian-predictive-coding — predictive coding, which began as a model of cortex (Rao and Ballard, 1999; Friston and Kiebel, 2009), used to train deep networks without backprop. Plain predictive coding loses credit with depth; adding a Lagrange multiplier per layer matches backprop on residual MLPs up to depth 128 (raw/arxiv-2605-31022-augmented-lagrangian-predictive-coding.md, §2, §5.3). The paper frames it as a penalty method, not a probabilistic model (raw/arxiv-2605-31022-augmented-lagrangian-predictive-coding.md, Appendix H).

Themes

  1. AIF as RL alternative for robotics — when the generative model is hand-tuned, AIF agents can outperform RL on Habitat-scale household tasks (source-why-learn-if-you-can-infer-active-inference). This reframes the active inference debate: not "AIF vs. RL on equal footing" but "AIF when you can specify generative structure."
  2. Hierarchy is load-bearing — S-HAI and the VERSES work both rely on hierarchical structure. S-HAI shows it's also a useful model of mPFC; the engineering and neuroscience converge on the same architectural prior.
  3. Connection to neuroscience — S-HAI's reproduction of rodent neural codes makes AIF unusually well-positioned in the concept-llm-reasoning / biology-as-evidence stack.
  4. Bridge to world models — AIF is structurally identical to model-based RL (predict-then-plan over a generative model). The source-dreamerv3 vs source-why-learn-if-you-can-infer-active-inference contrast is the clearest data point: same engine, opposite epistemology — Dreamer learns the generative model across 150+ tasks, VERSES specifies a kinematic one by hand. See concept-world-models for the full lineage.

Broader Context

Active inference unifies perception, action, and learning under a single objective (free energy minimisation). Tension (2026-10-24): Byrnes argues that learning to predict the senses cannot by itself choose between actions, since "I can predict that I'll sing, and then I sing, and my prediction was correct"; he holds that choice needs reward signals from fixed circuits (raw/web-2022-byrnes-brain-like-agi-safety-04-short-term-predictor.md, §4.7; source-bundle-byrnes-brain-like-agi-safety). He aims this at prediction of the senses, not at expected free energy; he links a 2023 post against the free energy principle that is not in raw. The wiki's reading: expected free energy has a pragmatic term, the expected log-probability of preferred outcomes (source-active-inference-foundations), and those prior preferences are fixed priors, much like Byrnes's fixed steering signals, so the gap is smaller than his argument suggests. The field is still much smaller in coverage than RL/RLHF here. See open questions in overview.
