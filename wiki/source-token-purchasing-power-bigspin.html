The Decline of Token-Level Purchasing Power (Bigspin AI)

source created 2026-07-04 updated 2026-07-04 ai-economics token-pricing coding-agents developer-tools inference

Publisher: Bigspin AI Authors: Moritz Sudhof (CEO), Chris Potts (Chief Scientist) Date: 2026-06-21

Summary

Bigspin AI measures "token-level purchasing power" — how much productive engineering output a single token buys — and finds it fell sharply over early 2026, a phenomenon they call "tokenflation". Analyzing 5,851 real Claude Opus 4.6 coding sessions (Feb 5 – Apr 15, 2026) with a "consumer price index" over five engineering deliverables (PRs shipped, files touched, lines of code, documentation, knowledge captures), they report that a token in mid-April 2026 purchased roughly $0.23 of output versus $1.00 in early February — a ~77% drop in just over two months, even though the sticker price per token did not rise. The point is subtle and important: the unit of AI cost (the token) can become dramatically more expensive in real terms while the nominal price list stays flat, because the model spends more tokens (especially on "thinking") to accomplish the same amount of shipped work.

Key Ideas

  • Tokenflation, quantified. Same model, same nominal price, but per-token output collapses. In a 10K-token session, February drafted 630 code lines vs April's 91; February completed 0.30 PRs per 10K tokens vs April's 0.16.
  • Quality gains didn't offset the loss. Code survival rate rose 90% → 95% across the window — better code, but nowhere near enough to compensate for the efficiency drop.
  • Three candidate causes. (1) Job-mix shift — the authors' strongest evidence — token allocation moved from 97% tool use (code generation) in February to 61% by mid-April, spread across code, text explanations, and thinking (1% → 18%); (2) model calibration — ongoing behavior tuning during product improvements; (3) harder tasks — users tackling more complex problems needing extended collaboration. None fully explains it alone.
  • Budgeting implication. Organizations forecasting AI-coding spend should expect materially higher costs: "producing engineering goods with AI has gotten considerably more expensive." Teams that measure agent-drafted code are hit harder than teams that measure shipped PRs.
  • A new economic lens. Introduces a demand-side metric — output per token — distinct from the usual supply-side headline ($/token). The real cost of AI engineering can rise even as published prices fall.
