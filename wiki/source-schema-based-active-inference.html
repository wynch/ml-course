Schema-based Hierarchical Active Inference (S-HAI)

source created 2026-05-06 updated 2026-05-06 active-inference neuroscience hierarchical-models generalization prefrontal-cortex schema 2026

Toon Van de Maele, Tim Verbelen, Dileep George, Giovanni Pezzulo — arXiv 2601.18946, Jan 2026

A computational model that couples active inference with schemas — abstract relational structures that capture commonalities across experiences. The result reproduces both the behavioral signatures of rapid schema-based generalization and the neural codes seen in rodent medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) during schema-dependent navigation.

Architecture

Two-level hierarchical generative model:

  • Top level: abstract task structure (schemas — e.g. "go-to-goal-type-X" templates)
  • Bottom level: spatial navigation in a concrete environment
  • Grounding likelihood: maps abstract goals at the top onto physical locations at the bottom; this is what gets re-bound when a familiar schema is dropped into a novel context

Inference at the top selects which schema is active; inference at the bottom plans physical action. Both levels are updated to minimize free energy.

Behavioral results

S-HAI captures the human/animal repertoire of schema-based generalization:

  • Flexible remapping of an abstract schema onto a new context.
  • Goal ambiguity resolution when multiple schemas could fit the observations.
  • Reuse vs accommodation tradeoff — when to fall back on existing structure vs grow new mappings.

Neural correspondence

Crucially, the same model reproduces three classes of cells reported in rodent mPFC during schema-dependent tasks:

  • Task-invariant goal-progress cells.
  • Goal-and-spatially conjunctive cells.
  • Place-like codes at the lower level.

This gives a mechanistic, computational story for how mPFC might implement abstract relational coding.

Relevance

A concrete example of concept-active-inference used not just as a control framework but as a theory of cortical coding. The authors are an interesting cross-section: Pezzulo (active inference / cognitive neuroscience), Verbelen (deep active inference, also on the VERSES robot control work), and Dileep George (Vicarion / schema networks). Connects abstraction-and-grounding to the same hierarchical-generative-model substrate.

Sits alongside source-active-inference-empathy in the active inference cluster, but the focus here is structural generalization rather than social cognition. Worth comparing to source-clevrer-video-reasoning as another attempt to study compositional / schema-like reasoning, though from the deep-learning side.
