Summary

Anthropic published a new constitution for Claude: a long document on the values and behaviour it wants, written "primarily for Claude" (R, "What is Claude's Constitution?"). It replaces the May 2023 constitution, which was "a list of standalone principles" (R, "Our new approach"). The new one explains its reasons, so that the model can judge new cases instead of following rules by rote (same).

Key claims

  • Four properties, in priority order when they seem to conflict: broadly safe (not undermining proper human oversight of AI "during the current phase of development"), broadly ethical, compliant with Anthropic's guidelines, genuinely helpful (R, "A brief summary of the new constitution").
  • Safety above ethics, for now. Not because safety matters more in the end, but because current models can err through mistaken beliefs or flawed values, so humans must keep the power to correct them (R, same section, "Being broadly safe").
  • Few hard rules. "Hard constraints" cover a short list of high-stakes acts, such as giving serious uplift to a bioweapons attack; the rest is judgement (R, "Our new approach", "Claude's ethics").
  • Final authority. Any other training or instruction should agree with both its letter and its spirit (R, "What is Claude's Constitution?").
  • Used in training. It is used at several training stages, growing out of Constitutional AI (2023), and Claude itself uses it to make synthetic training data: conversations, responses and rankings of responses (R, same section).
  • A section on Claude's nature, stating Anthropic's uncertainty about whether Claude has some form of consciousness or moral status (R, summary list).
  • Released under CC0, free for anyone to use (R, opening).
  • Scope: written for mainline, general-access models; some specialised models "don't fully fit" it (R, "Conclusion").

Caveats

  • Intention, not measured behaviour. Anthropic says outputs "might not always adhere" to it and points to its system cards for gaps (R, opening, "Conclusion").
  • The raw copy is the announcement; claims about the constitution's own text rest on Anthropic's summary of it.
  • A rigid-rule example in a footnote ("Always recommend professional help…") is Anthropic's own illustration of why it avoids rules, not an evaluated result (R, footnote 2).
