From RLVR to RLSVR — Self-Verifiable Rewards by Task Transformation

source created 2026-09-10 updated 2026-09-10 reinforcement-learning self-play self-improvement rlhf-alternatives reward-design llm-reasoning

Authors: Qinsi Wang et al. (Duke, Adobe, Penn State, Oregon State, NUS, Amazon) · arXiv: 2607.23802v2 · Date: 2026-07-31 (v1 2026-07-26) Code: github.com/wangqinsi1/RLSVR

Summary

RLVR — reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, i.e. training against an automatic answer checker — works where a checker exists: math, code. Open-ended work (summaries, stories) has no checker, so the field substitutes a reward model or an LLM judge, which caps the policy at the judge's competence. This paper's move is to stop approximating quality and instead rewrite the task into a game whose answer is known by construction. The environment picks a hidden variable, has the model do the real task under it, then asks a question about the hidden variable that can only be answered from the outputs. The instantiation, SpyRL, is the party game Who Is the Spy?: five players get the same input, one ("the spy") gets a version with a 20–40% span cut out, all write the summary/story/math problem, then all vote on who was working from damaged input. The vote is checkable — the environment assigned the spy. A player is rewarded for attracting few votes, which is a proxy for having written the better output. Training alternates: performers update while detectors are frozen, then swap.

Key claims

  • Summarization improves on all five test sets. Qwen3-4B GovReport ROUGE-L (overlap with a reference summary) 30.2 → 36.7; Qwen3-8B 29.0 → 34.1. Beats R-Zero and Absolute Zero, the two self-play baselines, on every set.
  • Creative writing wins the A/B. GPT-4o pairwise win rate of SpyRL against its own untrained backbone: 81.3% overall on WritingPrompts, 75.1% on WritingBench (Qwen3-4B). Against the baselines it also stays above 70%.
  • Verifiable tasks improve too, which was not required. Qwen3-4B seven-benchmark average 41.4 → 50.4; AIME 25 6.7 → 20.0; GPQA-Diamond 26.3 → 41.3.
  • The proxy tracks quality. Over 100 games, players receiving more suspicion votes get worse GPT-4o quality ranks. This is a measured correlation, not a proof that votes equal quality.
  • Role-advantage estimation is load-bearing. The spy is handicapped, so its raw reward must be re-centred on its own role. Without that correction the seven-benchmark average is 37.5 — below the 41.4 untrained backbone. The method actively harms the model when the calibration is removed.
  • Insensitive to the one hand-designed part. Masking 20% vs 40% of the input is nearly indistinguishable across five benchmarks. Group size 5 is the knee; 6 and 8 flatten out.
  • Transfer is asymmetric. Summarization and creative writing help each other; the math-trained checkpoint falls below parity on both writing tasks.

Limitations

  • No limitations section in the paper. The following are read off the setup.
  • Only Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B. No claim about larger or non-Qwen models.
  • The judge is removed from training, not from measurement: the headline open-ended numbers are GPT-4o A/B win rates. Human evaluation covers creative writing only.
  • The reward remains a proxy. "Whose output looks information-starved" is correlated with quality on 100 games, and nothing rules out the policy learning to look un-spy-like rather than to write well.

Relevance

  • concept-self-improvement — the sharpest version of the paper's thesis: verifiability is a property you can engineer into a task, not one a task either has or lacks. That reframes what counts as a self-improvable domain.
  • concept-rlhf-alternatives — sits beside source-dpo-direct-preference-optimization and source-intuitor-reasoning-without-rewards: all three remove a learned evaluator from the loop, but this one removes it by changing the task rather than by changing the objective.
  • concept-self-supervised-learning — the stated ancestor. The latent spy identity plays the role of the masked token: a label the data-generating process already knows.
  • concept-llm-reasoning — the reasoning gains (AIME 25 6.7 → 20.0) come from a game about writing, not from more math supervision.
  • Local echo: the crystallizer experiment (../ml-lab/crystallizer/RESULTS_V2.md) picked self-verifiable tasks as a selection rule — it studied string-transformation rules precisely because an answer can be checked exactly. RLSVR is the opposite direction on the same axis: keep the task you want and manufacture the checker. Honest caveat: the crystallizer never trained weights, so the two share a design principle and nothing else.
