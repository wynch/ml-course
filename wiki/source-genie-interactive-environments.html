Genie — Generative Interactive Environments

source created 2026-05-06 updated 2026-05-06 world-models foundation-models generative-environments latent-actions video deepmind 2024

Jake Bruce, Michael Dennis, Ashley Edwards, Jack Parker-Holder, Yuge (Jimmy) Shi, et al. — DeepMind, arXiv 2402.15391, Feb 2024 (ICML 2024 Best Paper).

The first foundation model for playable, controllable environments trained from unlabeled internet video. 11B parameters, ~200,000 hours of 2D platformer gameplay video — no action labels, no reward labels, no resets. The result is a model that takes a single image and generates an environment a human (or agent) can step through frame-by-frame using a discrete action.

Three-component architecture

  1. Latent Action Model (LAM): a VQ-VAE-style module trained to predict the next frame from the current frame plus a learned latent action token taken from a small codebook (8 codes in the released model). The bottleneck of an 8-way categorical forces the LAM to discover the world's actual controllable degrees of freedom — jump, move-left, move-right etc. emerge unsupervised from video. This is the load-bearing trick: Genie unlocks "playability" without ever seeing a controller signal.
  2. Video Tokenizer: a separate VQ-VAE compressing video frames to a grid of discrete tokens.
  3. Dynamics Model: an ST-Transformer (spatial-temporal factorised attention) that autoregressively predicts the next frame's tokens conditioned on past tokens and the latent action of that timestep.

At inference: encode the seed image, pick a latent action (via a UI mapped from the 8 codes), let the dynamics model predict next-frame tokens, decode back to pixels. Repeat. The user is interacting with a learned generative model whose action interface was discovered by the LAM.

Results

  • Generates coherent, controllable 2D environments from any seed image, including hand-drawn sketches and real-world photos transferred to platformer dynamics.
  • A behavioural-cloning agent trained on Genie's latent actions in held-out video transfers competitively to held-out environments — evidence that latent actions are recoverable from any video, not memorised.
  • Establishes a "Chinchilla-style" scaling-law analysis for video world models: smooth losses with model size up to 11B.

Relevance

Genie is the inflection point for concept-world-models from "MDP-shaped generative dynamics in latent space" (source-ha-schmidhuber-world-models → source-dreamerv3) to foundation-scale generative environments learned from internet video without labels. The latent-action discovery is mechanistically related to JEPA's prediction-in-representation idea, and the "controllability emerges from a discrete bottleneck" thread connects to VQ-VAE work in concept-representation-learning (cf. source-drifting-model-representation).

It also sets up the central concept-world-models tension that crystallises in 2024–25: generative world models (Genie, Genie 2) that synthesise pixels and afford direct play, vs. predictive world models (V-JEPA 2, TD-MPC2's reward-only latent) that predict in representation and use it for control without ever generating frames.
