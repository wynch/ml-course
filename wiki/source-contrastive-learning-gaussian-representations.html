Why Contrastive Learning Naturally Produces Gaussian Representations

source created 2026-04-13 updated 2026-04-13 representation-learning contrastive-learning geometry iclr-2026

Roy Betser (Technion) with Eyal Gofer, Meir Yossef Levi, Guy Gilboa — Medium blog summary of ICLR 2026 Oral (arXiv 2602.24012)

Argues that contrastive learning objectives inevitably produce representations with Gaussian structure, as a consequence of the objective operating in high-dimensional space rather than by accident.

Key arguments

1. Spherical uniformity as the primary mechanism. Contrastive objectives balance alignment (pulling similar examples together) against uniformity (pushing dissimilar ones apart). The uniformity term drives representations toward even distribution across all directions on the sphere.

2. The Maxwell-Poincaré connection. A classical result: if a point is sampled uniformly from a high-dimensional sphere, its projection onto any fixed direction is approximately Gaussian. This bridges geometric uniformity to the observed Gaussian marginals.

3. Alignment saturation hypothesis. Alignment improves until it hits a ceiling set by augmentation strength; after that, uniformity continues driving the directional distribution, so Gaussianity is the late-training attractor.

4. Regularization route. Under a regularized objective with entropy and norm penalties, the uniform-on-sphere distribution is optimal even when uniformity would hurt alignment — a second, independent path to the same conclusion.

Relevance

Gives a clean geometric account of why representations from SimCLR/MoCo/CLIP-style training look Gaussian — a long-standing empirical observation. Relates to the Platonic representation debate (source-platonic-representation-aristotelian) and to the broader question of whether trained embeddings have universal structure (concept-representation-learning). Also connects to source-nonlinear-feature-representations's argument that linear directions are the right unit in high-dimensional nets: if the marginals are Gaussian along every direction, linear features are not just convenient but statistically inevitable.
