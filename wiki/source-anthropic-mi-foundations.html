Anthropic Mech Interp Foundations

source created 2026-05-07 updated 2026-05-07 interpretability circuits transformers induction-heads superposition features in-context-learning phase-transition foundational anthropic

The three papers from Anthropic's transformer-circuits programme (late 2021 to late 2022) that established the conceptual scaffolding the rest of mech interp still runs on: (1) an algebraic rewrite of the transformer into readable paths, (2) the existence proof that ICL is implemented by a specific circuit that forms in a phase transition, and (3) the theoretical explanation for why neurons fail as the unit of analysis. Cite them as a unit: they collectively answer "what's the right grammar for reading an LLM."

A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits — Elhage, Nanda, Olsson, Henighan, ..., Olah 2021

Anthropic / transformer-circuits.pub, December 2021.

Rewrites attention-only transformers as a sum of interpretable paths over QK circuits (which attend) and OV circuits (which write to the residual stream), decomposing a forward pass into additive contributions per head and per composition. Under this algebra, a two-layer attention-only transformer is literally a sum of zero-, one-, and two-head paths — each of which can be inspected independently.

Empirical centerpiece: the discovery and naming of induction heads — a two-head composition that implements "look up the previous occurrence of the current token and copy what followed it." This is an algorithm for in-context copying, and the paper argues it is the mechanism behind a large fraction of in-context learning in transformers.

Distinctive contribution: the grammar of mech interp. When later papers say "this is a name mover head" or "this is composition through the residual stream," they are writing in this paper's notation. The QK/OV/path-decomposition vocabulary is now the standard. Gives an algebraic rewrite of the transformer into paths you can read like a program.

Toy Models of Superposition — Elhage, Hume, Olsson, ..., Olah 2022

Anthropic / transformer-circuits.pub, September 2022. arXiv 2209.10652.

Builds a minimal MLP that provably stores more sparse features than it has dimensions by packing them as non-orthogonal directions — "superposition." Maps out a phase diagram: as feature sparsity and importance vary, stored features snap into regular geometric arrangements (digons, triangles, pentagons, higher uniform polytopes). Links superposition to polysemanticity (one neuron firing for many features), adversarial examples, and grokking dynamics.

Key findings

  • Neurons are the wrong unit of analysis when features outnumber dimensions.
  • Superposition is a predictable phenomenon: feature importance × sparsity determines whether a feature gets its own direction, shares one, or is dropped entirely.
  • The correct symbolic unit is a linear direction in activation space, not a neuron.

Distinctive contribution: explains why the "one neuron = one concept" dream fails and reframes the symbolic unit of extraction. This is the theoretical foundation that licenses sparse-autoencoder readouts: a concept is still a discrete object, just not axis-aligned. Directly motivates the entire SAE dictionary-learning program (source-sae-monosemanticity-2023 and source-sae-scaling-trilogy).

In-Context Learning and Induction Heads — Olsson, Elhage, Nanda, Joseph, DasSarma, Henighan, ..., Olah 2022

Anthropic / transformer-circuits.pub, March 2022 (no arXiv).

Promotes induction heads from "neat circuit found in 2-layer attention-only models" (the math-framework paper) to "the mechanistic substrate of in-context learning across the entire model family."

The claim

An induction head is a two-head composition implementing the algorithm: find the previous occurrence of the current token A, look at the token B that followed it, and copy B as the prediction. In notation: [A][B]…[A] → [B]. The previous-token head writes "the token before me was X" into position-i+1's residual stream; the induction head's QK circuit then matches that signature when it next sees X, and its OV circuit copies X's continuation.

The 2022 paper argues this circuit is responsible for a large fraction of all in-context learning (ICL) — not just literal copying, but the broader ability of LLMs to use earlier context to predict later tokens better. ICL is operationalized as the loss difference between token 50 and token 500 in a long sequence: how much does the model improve by reading more context.

Six arguments (the paper's spine)

Six progressively stronger forms of evidence connecting induction-head formation to ICL:

  1. Macroscopic co-occurrence — across 34 transformers studied during training, induction heads form in a sharp phase transition that coincides with the bend in the ICL-vs-tokens curve. Both happen in the same narrow training window.
  2. Macroscopic co-perturbation — architectural changes that shift when the phase transition happens (e.g., disabling parts of the attention pattern) shift the ICL bend by the same amount.
  3. Direct ablation — ablating induction heads in small models cuts ICL substantially; the effect is concentrated in heads that look like induction heads.
  4. Generality — in larger models, induction heads do far more than literal copying: translation, fuzzy matching, abstract pattern continuation. The mechanism generalizes.
  5. Mechanistic plausibility — the prefix-match-then-copy operation is a natural primitive for ICL writ large; harder ICL tasks can be built compositionally on top of it.
  6. Continuity small→large — the small-model story (where the heads can be fully read out as in the 2021 math-framework paper) extends smoothly into 13B-parameter models.

The argument structure is itself notable: the paper deliberately mixes mechanistic (small-model) and behavioural (large-model) evidence to bridge the scale gap that haunts mech interp.

Phase transition

The phase transition is the paper's signature empirical finding. Models go from "no induction-head signature, ~no ICL" to "induction-head signature, full ICL" over a small fraction of training. This makes induction-head formation a discrete computational milestone rather than gradient-descent gradually polishing a continuous capability — closer to grokking (source-nanda-2023-grokking-modular-addition) than to standard scaling-law smoothness.

Distinctive contribution: operationalised "circuit findings can scale" for the Anthropic mech interp programme. Everything later — IOI (source-wang-2022-ioi-circuit-gpt2), ACDC (source-conmy-2023-acdc-automated-circuit-discovery), the SAE wave (source-sae-monosemanticity-2023 → source-sae-scaling-trilogy) — implicitly inherits the framing that real LLM behaviours are produced by identifiable circuits. Induction heads are the existence proof. Also gives the field a concrete answer to "where does ICL come from?", which had been treated as emergent magic since source-gpt3-few-shot-learners.

Why this grouping matters

These three papers form the vocabulary, the existence proof, and the unit-of-analysis correction for the entire Anthropic mech interp programme — all in roughly 12 months.

  • The 2021 framework gives the grammar (paths, QK/OV, composition).
  • The induction-heads 2022 paper gives the existence proof that real behaviours (ICL) decompose into named, manipulable circuits — and that they form via discrete phase transitions, not smooth scaling.
  • Toy models of superposition gives the corrected ontology: features are linear directions, not neurons. Without this, SAEs make no sense.

Read together, they license everything downstream in concept-mechanistic-interpretability: IOI-style circuit discovery, SAE dictionary learning, attribution graphs, model biology. The neural↔symbolic bridge is most concrete here: pieces of the LLM's algorithm — pattern-match plus copy — extracted from weights and named; the phase transition gives a sharp empirical hook between training dynamics and computational structure.
