Why Learn If You Can Infer

source created 2026-05-06 updated 2026-05-06 active-inference robotics hierarchical-models planning control verses 2024

VERSES Research blog (2026), summarizing "Why learn if you can infer? Robot arm control with Hierarchical Active Inference" — Mazzaglia, Verbelen et al., NeuroAI @ NeurIPS 2024

A position-and-results post arguing that control should be cast as inference, not as a long offline learning problem. The technical work behind it is a hierarchical active inference controller for n-DOF robot arms; the blog also reports follow-on results on the Habitat household-task benchmark.

Core claim

Don't learn what you can infer.

Brute-force RL needs huge offline budgets to learn policies that are then brittle on unseen layouts. Active inference sidesteps this by treating control as probabilistic inference under a hand-shaped generative model: the agent produces motor commands that minimize expected free energy under a model of its own kinematics and the world. Most "learning" turns into Bayesian model identification of physical parameters (link lengths, link velocities, etc.) at run time.

Hierarchical architecture

Two-tier (with a mid-tier coordination layer in the household setup):

  • Top level — symbolic: discrete planning states like "go to the kitchen", "Pick", "Move", "Place". Reasoning over task structure and scene context.
  • Bottom level — continuous sensorimotor: predicts joint velocities and proprioceptive feedback; resolves inverse kinematics implicitly because the generative model mirrors the kinematic chain.

By designing the generative model to mirror the robot's kinematic hierarchy, kinematic inversion falls out as a consequence of inference — no analytic IK or learned IK net needed. The agent simultaneously infers motor commands and its own morphological parameters.

Results

  • Robot-arm reaching (NeurIPS 2024): outperforms an RL baseline as DOFs increase, with collision avoidance and link-length estimation done online during execution. Path lengths are shorter on average even when reach time is somewhat longer.
  • Habitat household tasks (blog): ~66.5% success on tidy-the-room style tasks vs ~54.7% for RL competitors that needed 1.3B training iterations. The active-inference agent's "training" is hand-tuning each skill over a handful of episodes; evaluation is on unseen layouts.

Relevance

A concrete, embodied counter-thesis to the "scale up RL with massive offline data" approach. Sits naturally next to:

  • source-active-inference-empathy — active inference applied to social cognition.
  • source-schema-based-active-inference — same technical family (hierarchical generative model) used as a theory of cortical coding in rodents; one of the authors (Verbelen) overlaps.
  • concept-active-inference — this is a real-world systems-level instantiation of the framework.

Also a useful counterpoint to the dominant LLM-agent / RL-from-rewards thread in concept-ai-agents and concept-rlhf-alternatives: here the "agent" doesn't have language at all, and the alternative to reward isn't internal confidence (source-intuitor-reasoning-without-rewards) but expected free energy under a generative model.
