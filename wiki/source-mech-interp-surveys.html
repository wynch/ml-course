Mech Interp Surveys

The pair of survey papers the wiki uses as authoritative entry points to mechanistic interpretability. Rai et al. zoom out across all of MI; Shu et al. zoom in on SAEs specifically. Together they are the "what to read first" reference pair for concept-mechanistic-interpretability.

A Practical Review of Mechanistic Interpretability for Transformer-Based LMs — Rai, Zhou, Feng, Saparov, Yao 2024

arXiv:2407.02646, July 2024 (latest revision Oct 2025).

A 61-page, 15-figure survey aimed at researchers entering the field. (Originally indexed in the wiki as "Bereska & Gavves" — that is a different mech-interp survey, also 2024, focused on AI safety motivations. This page covers Rai et al.'s practical review, which is more comprehensive and task-centric.)

Its distinctive move is the task-centric organisation: rather than carve up MI by methodology (probes / patching / SAEs / circuits), it groups research around the questions being asked.

Coverage map

The review covers the full method stack. Roughly:

  • Probing — what information is linearly recoverable from a representation; classifier probes vs. structural probes; failure modes (probes succeeding doesn't mean the model uses the information).
  • Activation patching / causal mediation — substitute a clean activation with a corrupted one (or vice versa) and measure downstream effect; the workhorse of circuit discovery from source-wang-2022-ioi-circuit-gpt2 onward.
  • Attribution — gradient × activation, integrated gradients, attribution patching; quantifies how much each component matters for a specific prediction.
  • Circuit analysis — going from "this head matters" to "this head plus that MLP plus this composition implements algorithm X." The IOI / induction-head / grokking lineage. Includes source-conmy-2023-acdc-automated-circuit-discovery-style automation and source-circuit-tracing-methods-anthropic attribution graphs.
  • Sparse autoencoders — feature decomposition of the residual stream / FFN activations into a sparse, monosemantic basis. Covers the source-sae-monosemanticity-2023 → source-sae-scaling-trilogy arc and architectural variants.
  • Causal interventions — broader category encompassing patching, ablation, steering, and feature-level interventions like the ones in source-conditional-activation-steering-cast.

Task-centric framing

The novel contribution is organising findings by what question the technique answers:

  • "What does this component represent?"
  • "What is this component computing?"
  • "How does this behaviour arise mechanistically?"
  • "How can we steer or correct this behaviour?"
  • "How do we evaluate whether an explanation is correct?"

Makes the review usable as a lookup table: a practitioner with a specific question can find the relevant techniques without learning the whole method taxonomy first.

Snapshot of the field (mid-2024)

  • The circuits foundation — Olah, Elhage, induction heads, IOI, grokking (source-anthropic-mi-foundations).
  • The superposition + SAE turn — Anthropic toy models → Bricken → Cunningham → Templeton; the field's reorientation around features-as-directions.
  • The automation push — ACDC, attribution patching, transcoders, attribution graphs.
  • The frontier-application turn — Biology-of-Claude-3.5-Haiku (source-lindsey-2025-biology-of-llm), large-scale SAE deployments.
  • The evaluation problem — how to tell whether an explanation is faithful, including the chain-of-thought-monitorability question (source-measuring-cot-monitorability).

Identified gaps: scalability of circuit-level analysis, faithfulness benchmarks, the gap between "we can name features" and "we can predict behaviour from features."

Distinctive contribution: the survey-of-surveys reference for mech interp — the page to cite when something needs an authoritative, recent, comprehensive overview rather than a single primary source.

A Survey on Sparse Autoencoders — Shu, Wu, Zhao, Rai, Yao, Liu, Du 2025

EMNLP 2025 Findings. arXiv:2503.05613, March 2025.

A focused survey of the SAE-for-interpretability subfield. Where Rai et al. zoom out across all of MI, this one zooms in on SAEs specifically: what they are, how to train them, how to evaluate them, what to do with them.

Four pillars

1. Technical framework — architectures and training. SAE variants covered:

  • Standard / vanilla SAEs — encoder–decoder pair with L1-penalised hidden activations; the source-sae-monosemanticity-2023 starting point.
  • TopK SAEs — replace L1 with a hard top-k constraint; OpenAI's contribution.
  • Batch-TopK — efficient variant for large activation spaces.
  • JumpReLU / Gated SAEs — DeepMind variants, used in the Gemma Scope release.
  • Matryoshka SAEs (source-sae-scaling-trilogy) — nested dictionaries of increasing size, giving a feature hierarchy rather than a flat code.
  • Crosscoders (source-crosscoder-model-diffing) — SAEs that span multiple models or multiple layers, supporting model-diffing and cross-layer analysis.
  • Transcoders — SAEs trained on input/output pairs of an MLP block rather than residual-stream activations; basis for source-circuit-tracing-methods-anthropic and the Gemma Scope full-stack release (source-sae-scaling-trilogy).

Training-side topics: sparsity coefficient scheduling, dead-neuron mitigation (resampling, ghost grads), feature collapse avoidance, dictionary-learning perspectives, scaling laws for SAE training compute.

2. Feature explanation. Two families of methods for explaining what an SAE feature represents:

  • Input-based — top-activating examples, automated explanation via an LLM scoring activation patterns over text.
  • Output-based — what the feature's decoder column does to the residual stream / next-token distribution; logit lens views; intervention-based readouts.

Connects naturally to the introspective / self-explanation thread (source-self-explanation-transluce, source-introspective-interpretability).

3. Evaluation. Distinguishes:

  • Structural metrics — reconstruction loss, sparsity (L0), feature-density distributions, dead-feature rate, dictionary similarity.
  • Functional metrics — does intervening on the feature change behaviour as predicted? Does using SAE features improve probing? Does steering work? This is where the Golden-Gate-Claude-style demos in source-sae-scaling-trilogy live.

Faithfulness — whether an explanation reflects the actual computation — is identified as the central open problem.

4. Applications.

  • Steering and control — feature-level activation steering (source-conditional-activation-steering-cast).
  • Behaviour analysis / model biology — source-lindsey-2025-biology-of-llm case studies.
  • Model diffing — Crosscoders (source-crosscoder-model-diffing).
  • Layer replacement — using SAE features as a stand-in for the layer (source-complete-replacement-lm-layers).
  • Safety auditing — finding deception-relevant or refusal-relevant features.

Open questions surfaced

  • Faithfulness — how to validate that SAE features describe what the model is doing, not just plausible decompositions of activations.
  • Dark matter — what fraction of model behaviour does the SAE basis miss? Does the residual capture something fundamental, or just noise?
  • Scaling — SAE training cost grows with dictionary size and base-model size; can this be made tractable for 10x larger frontier models?
  • Non-linear / dictionary structure — see source-nonlinear-feature-representations for the theoretical constraints angle.

Distinctive contribution: the canonical reference for the SAE branch of concept-mechanistic-interpretability as of mid-2025. Explicitly cites and synthesises essentially every SAE source in this wiki.

Why this grouping matters

The two surveys are complementary in scope, not redundant:

  • Rai et al. → mech interp as a whole field with SAEs as one branch; task-centric so you can look up "what technique answers my question."
  • Shu et al. → SAEs in isolation with the technical depth (architectures, training, evaluation, faithfulness) the broader survey can't afford.

Use Rai for orientation across MI; use Shu when you've decided SAEs are the tool and need to pick a variant or design an evaluation. Together they identify the same central open problem: faithfulness — does the explanation actually describe what the model is computing?
