Think Before You Speak: Pause Tokens

Summary

The plainest version of "give the model tokens to compute in". A transformer that has read K tokens computes K vectors per layer before its next token; the paper asks what happens if it gets K + M (R, §1). It appends M copies of one learned <pause> token, outside the normal vocabulary, and ignores the model's outputs until the last pause (R, §3.1). The pauses carry no information and add one embedding, 1,024 numbers on a 1B model (R, §4.1). Nothing supervises what happens at the pause positions; the loss skips them, and only the next real tokens are scored (R, §3.1, Eq. 3).

In pretraining, pauses go in at random places, 10% of the positions of each 2,048-token sequence; in finetuning and inference, M pauses are appended after the prompt (R, §3.1, §4.1).

Key results

Models: 1B and 130M decoder-only, pretrained on C4 for 200B tokens; nine downstream tasks; 5 finetuning runs each; M = 10 or 50, best of the two reported (R, §4.1-4.2).

  • Pause in pretraining and finetuning beats standard training on eight of nine tasks for the 1B model (all but HellaSwag) and six for the 130M model. SQuAD exact match +18%, CommonSenseQA up to +8%, GSM8k 7.5% → 8.5% (R, §1, §4.3, Figure 3).
  • Pause in finetuning only gives smaller gains on about five tasks and hurts GSM8k (R, §4.3). This is the setting a practitioner could try on an existing model; the authors call its effect "lukewarm".
  • Pause in pretraining only, no pauses downstream: clear gains on two tasks only (CoQA, PhysicalIQA), so most of the gain comes from using the pauses at inference (R, §4.3).
  • Periods instead of pauses on a normally trained model: no gain, matching Lanham et al. (R, §4.3, Figure 4a).
  • How many: each task has a best M; on GSM8k 10 is best and 50 falls back to baseline (R, §5).
  • Robustness: a model finetuned with 10 pauses stays above baseline with half as many at inference, but with zero pauses the fully pause-trained model breaks "spectacularly" (R, §5).
  • Theory: pauses add width, not depth. With M pauses the model does K + M parallel computations per layer; chain-of-thought instead adds about M × L sequential steps because each written token is fed back (R, §6, Appendix I.2). An informal theorem gives tasks a 2-layer transformer can do only with pauses, when the attention layer has far more parameters than the input has tokens (R, §6, Theorem 6.1, Appendix J).

Caveats

  • Gains depend on pretraining with pauses, which is costly; only two model sizes, both small (R, §8).
  • The smaller model gains on fewer tasks, the opposite of the authors' first guess (R, §6).
  • The paper's figures give the per-task scores; the text quotes only the headline gains, so this page does too.
  • M is fixed per task, not chosen per input (R, §7 "Adaptive Compute Transformers").
