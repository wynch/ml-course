Switch Transformer

source created 2026-05-06 updated 2026-05-06 moe mixture-of-experts sparsity scaling architecture foundational 2021

William Fedus, Barret Zoph, Noam Shazeer — arXiv 2101.03961, JMLR 2022 (Google Brain)

The paper that turned mixture-of-experts (MoE) from a research curiosity into a production-grade scaling lever. Simplifies the source-shazeer-outrageously-large-moe sparsely-gated layer to top-1 routing ("switch routing") — every token goes to exactly one expert — and shows this routes around the instability and complexity that had previously made MoE hard to train, scaling cleanly to a 1.6T-parameter model.

Method

  • Switch layer: replaces each transformer FFN with N experts (each itself an FFN) and a learned router. The router computes softmax(W_r · x) and dispatches each token to its single argmax expert — top-1 instead of top-2/top-k as in Shazeer 2017.
  • Capacity factor: each expert can process at most (tokens / experts) · capacity_factor tokens per batch; overflow tokens skip the layer (residual passes through). Trades load balance vs. dropped tokens.
  • Auxiliary load-balancing loss: encourages uniform routing (penalises mass on any single expert).
  • Selective precision: keep router computations in float32 for stability while the rest of the model is bf16/fp16 — fixes most prior MoE training instabilities.
  • Expert parallelism in the mesh: experts shard across devices, tokens are all-to-all'd to their assigned expert, computed, and shuffled back.

Key numbers

  • Switch-Base (~7B activated, 26B total) matches T5-Base in 1/7 the training time.
  • Switch-XXL outperforms T5-XXL with 4× faster pre-training at matched FLOPs.
  • Switch-C: 1.6T parameters (2048 experts), the first openly reported trillion-param language model. Ran on TPUv3 pods with bf16.
  • 4–7× wall-clock pre-training speedup at fixed quality budget vs. the dense T5 baseline.

Why it matters

Switch made MoE the default route to "more parameters at fixed FLOPs" — every modern frontier MoE (Mixtral, DeepSeek-V2/V3, Qwen-MoE, GPT-4-class systems) descends from this template, even those that re-add top-k routing. The two key insights — simplify to top-1, and stabilise the router with float32 + load-balancing loss — are still standard.

In wiki context, Switch is the direct successor of source-shazeer-outrageously-large-moe (the 2017 paper that introduced sparsely-gated MoE) and the architectural counterpoint to source-engram-deepseek, which contrasts MoE-style learned conditional compute against a deterministic O(1) N-gram memory and finds a U-shaped scaling law trading the two off. It's also the philosophical ancestor of encoder-MoE work like source-openai-privacy-filter (1.5B / 50M-active sparse MoE encoder for PII), which retrofits the Switch-style top-k routing into a bidirectional classifier.
