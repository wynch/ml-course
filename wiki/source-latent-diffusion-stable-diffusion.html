Latent Diffusion Models / Stable Diffusion

source created 2026-05-07 updated 2026-05-07 diffusion generative-models latent-space foundational 2021

Rombach, Blattmann, Lorenz, Esser, Ommer — arXiv 2112.10752, CVPR 2022 (Ludwig Maximilian University Munich / Runway / Stability AI)

The paper that made high-resolution diffusion tractable on a single GPU and underpinned Stable Diffusion, the first widely deployable open-weights image generator. The core move: instead of running the diffusion process in pixel space (expensive at 512×512+), compress images into a learned low-dimensional latent space via a VQ/VAE autoencoder, then run the diffusion U-Net on those latents.

Method

Two-stage system, trained separately:

  1. Perceptual autoencoder. A VQ-regularised or KL-regularised autoencoder compresses 512×512×3 images into ~64×64×4 latents (≈48× reduction). Trained with reconstruction loss + a perceptual loss + a patch-GAN discriminator to keep textures sharp. The encoder/decoder are frozen for stage 2.
  2. Latent diffusion U-Net. A time-conditioned U-Net learns to denoise these latents under standard DDPM/DDIM dynamics. Cross-attention layers inject text conditioning (CLIP / BERT text embeddings) at multiple U-Net resolutions, enabling flexible conditioning (text, layout, segmentation, etc.).

At inference: sample noise in latent space → run iterative denoising (20–50 steps with DDIM/PLMS/DPM-Solver) → decode through the frozen autoencoder.

Key numbers

  • 48× less compute at training than pixel-space diffusion (Imagen, GLIDE) for comparable quality.
  • 256×256 ImageNet class-conditional FID 3.6 (SOTA at the time).
  • Stable Diffusion v1.x: 860M U-Net + 123M text encoder, trained on LAION-5B subsets, runs at <4 GB VRAM — the threshold that triggered the consumer-side explosion.
  • Sample speed: a 512×512 image in ~3–5 seconds on a single consumer GPU.

Why it matters

Latent Diffusion is the canonical instance of a recurring research move: compress first, then generate in the compressed space. The autoencoder strips away perceptually irrelevant high-frequency detail; the diffusion model only has to model the semantically meaningful part of the distribution. Compute savings are quadratic in resolution.

Three lines of influence in the wiki:

  1. Architecture lineage. LDM is the U-Net backbone; source-dit-diffusion-transformer replaces the U-Net with a pure transformer over patches in the same latent space, and this DiT design now dominates (Sora, Stable Diffusion 3, Flux). LDM is the bridge from pixel diffusion to transformer diffusion.
  2. Modality generalisation. The latent-space recipe transfers to video (Sora, latent video diffusion), 3D (source-3d-generation-foundations, source-3d-generation-foundations), molecules, audio. The autoencoder need not even be reconstruction-trained — any well-behaved latent works.
  3. Cross-modal conditioning. The cross-attention text-injection pattern is what made conditional generation flexible: swap in CLIP image embeddings for image-to-image, depth maps for ControlNet, etc. Anchor for the broader concept-multimodal story.

Sits in productive tension with concept-diffusion-language-models (text-side analogue, slower to catch on) and with continuous-flow alternatives like source-flow-map-language-models which collapse iterative denoising into one-step generation. LDM's "iterative refinement in a learned latent" is also conceptually close to the concept-representation-learning thesis: the autoencoder produces the substrate on which all the interesting structure lives.

Practical legacy: Stable Diffusion is the most-downloaded open-weights generative model in history and the reason most diffusion infrastructure (Diffusers, ComfyUI, AUTOMATIC1111) exists.
