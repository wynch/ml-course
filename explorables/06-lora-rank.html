Low rank: what LoRA actually trains

Module 06 · Transformers/LLMs ← All explorables

LoRA's whole bet is one empirical claim: fine-tuning's update ΔW has low intrinsic rank (it uses few independent directions), so you can store it as two skinny matrices ΔW = (α/r)·B·A and train only those. Drag the rank slider below and watch a 48×48 matrix get rebuilt from its top-r singular directions. Then flip between two matrices from the same layer of SmolLM2-360M: the real frozen weight of q_proj and the real trained ΔW that module 06's rank-32 adapter adds to it. To get within 20%, the frozen weight needs 15 of its 48 directions; the trained update needs 7.

2 · Where the adapter attaches

Module 06 targets all seven linear projections in every one of SmolLM2-360M's 32 blocks. W₀ stays frozen; each gets its own A ∈ ℝ^{r×d_in} and B ∈ ℝ^{d_out×r}. A starts random, B starts at zero, so at step 0 the adapter is a no-op and the model is the base.

3 · And it works — the module's real run

200 LoRA-SFT steps on ~512 smoltalk / everyday-conversations examples, SmolLM2-360M on the M5's MPS backend. Exercise (a) reran the first 120 steps at r=4 and r=32: 8× the trainable parameters, essentially the same loss.

sft-lab r=16 · loss 1.2516 → 0.5957 in 200 steps r=4 final 0.6549 r=32 final 0.6553 train runtime 728 s on MPS PyTorch/MPS ~479 tok/s MLX ~3,500 tok/s (~7.3×)

What to notice

  • Compare the two at r = 8. The frozen W₀ keeps 35% error (88% of its energy); the trained ΔW is down to 17% (97% of its energy). Its top direction alone holds 57% of the energy, against 42% for W₀. The update is more concentrated than the weight it changes.
  • Exactly 0 at r = 32 proves nothing. Set ΔW to r = 32 and the error hits exactly 0, but only because the adapter's shape forces it: B·A passes through 32 dimensions, so its rank cannot exceed 32. The honest evidence for low rank is how fast the energy falls before that cap.
  • Break-even is at r = 24 here. Full matrix = 48·48 = 2,304 numbers; the factors cost r·(48+48) = 96r. Above r=24 the "compression" costs more than the matrix. Real LoRA never gets near that: at d=960 and r=16, A+B is 3.3% of one W₀.
  • The spectrum tells you how cheap a factorization can be. The structured pattern's singular values drop to zero after 3; ΔW's fall fast; W₀'s fall more slowly. The flatter the spectrum, the more directions a good approximation needs.
  • More rank did not mean lower loss here. r=4 trains 2.17M params (0.60%), r=32 trains 17.4M (4.58%), and their loss curves in panel 3 sit on top of each other. But the test is not clean: both runs keep α = 32, so the scale α/r is 8 at r=4 and 1 at r=32, and r=32 takes much smaller steps. Try α = 2r, or rsLoRA's α/√r, before concluding the task needs only rank 4.
