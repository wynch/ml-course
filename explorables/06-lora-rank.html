Low rank: what LoRA actually trains

Module 06 · Transformers/LLMs ← All explorables

LoRA's whole bet is one empirical claim: fine-tuning's update ΔW has very low intrinsic rank, so you can store it as two skinny matrices ΔW = (α/r)·B·A and train only those. Drag the rank slider below and watch a 48×48 matrix get rebuilt from its top-r singular directions. Then flip between a real frozen weight from module 04's tiny GPT and the real trained ΔW from module 06's rank-32 adapter. To get within 20%, the frozen weight needs 28 of its 48 directions; the trained update needs 7. That gap is the entire trick.

2 · Where the adapter attaches

Module 06 targets all seven linear projections in every one of SmolLM2-360M's 32 blocks. W₀ stays frozen; each gets its own A ∈ ℝ^{r×d_in} and B ∈ ℝ^{d_out×r}. A starts random, B starts at zero, so at step 0 the adapter is a no-op and the model is the base.

3 · And it works — the module's real run

200 LoRA-SFT steps on ~512 smoltalk / everyday-conversations examples, SmolLM2-360M on the M5's MPS backend. Exercise (a) reran the first 120 steps at r=4 and r=32: 8× the trainable parameters, essentially the same loss.

What to notice

  • A frozen weight is not low rank. Pick W₀ — real frozen weight and set r=16: the error is still 41% and the residual is nearly as bright as the target. A trained transformer packs information into every direction it has.
  • The trained update is. Pick ΔW — real trained LoRA update: r=8 already gets error down to 17%, and at r=32 the error hits exactly 0 — because that ΔW really was produced by a rank-32 adapter. You are watching LoRA's premise reproduced from the adapter file on disk.
  • Break-even is at r = 24 here. Full matrix = 48·48 = 2,304 numbers; the factors cost r·(48+48) = 96r. Above r=24 the "compression" costs more than the matrix. Real LoRA never gets near that: at d=960 and r=16, A+B is 3.3% of one W₀.
  • The spectrum tells you the rank before you train. The structured pattern's singular values drop to zero after 3; ΔW's decay smoothly; W₀'s form a near-flat plateau. Flat spectrum ⇒ no cheap factorization exists.
  • More rank ≠ more learning. r=4 trains 2.17M params (0.60%), r=32 trains 17.4M (4.58%) — and their loss curves in panel 3 sit on top of each other. Everyday-conversations genuinely has low intrinsic rank, so the extra capacity has nothing to do.
