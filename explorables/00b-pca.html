Principal components, by hand

Module 00b · Origins ← All explorables

Power iteration is the oldest way to find an eigenvector and the easiest to believe: multiply a random arrow by the covariance matrix, renormalise, repeat. The largest eigenvalue wins because it is amplified fastest. Here you can watch that race happen, one step at a time, on a cloud you control.

Try this: press Step a few times and watch the orange arrow snap onto the long axis of the cloud — usually in three or four steps. Then drag the elongation slider towards 1.0 so the cloud becomes a circle, restart the iteration, and watch it crawl: with λ₂/λ₁ ≈ 1 there is no dominant direction left to find. Panel 2 is the same algorithm's real output on 784-dimensional FashionMNIST, from modules/00b-bayes-knn-pca.

What to notice

  • Power iteration converges geometrically, at rate λ₂/λ₁. At the default elongation the arrow is within a degree of PC1 after 3 steps. Push the elongation slider to 1.00, hit Restart v, and the iteration limps — every direction is equally good, so there is nothing to converge to. That single ratio is the whole story of why the module's 784-dimensional run needed 22 iterations for one component and 3,390 for another.
  • PC1 is not a fit line. Slide projection onto PC1 to 100% and watch the grey residual segments: they meet the axis at right angles. Least squares minimises vertical distance to a line; PCA minimises perpendicular distance, which is why it has no notion of which variable is the "output".
  • The eigenvalue is the variance. λ₁ and λ₂ in the readout are literally the variances along the two axes, and they always sum to the total variance of the cloud, no matter how you rotate it. Drag a single outlier far away and watch λ₁ jump — PCA is a second-moment method, so one distant point can steer the whole axis.
  • Sign is meaningless. Press Restart v a few times. The arrow sometimes settles pointing the other way — v and −v are the same eigenvector. That is why the module's comparison against numpy.linalg.eigh aligns signs first (pca.py:_fix_signs); without it the "disagreement" between two correct answers would look like 180°.
  • Real data is mostly empty directions. In panel 2, three components carry half of FashionMNIST's pixel variance and 83 carry 90% — of 784. The scree bars fall off a cliff after about the fifth. That cliff is why low-rank tricks work at all, from these eigen-clothes to LoRA in module 06.
  • Reconstruction error is the variance you discarded. Sweep k and watch the MSE readout fall along the tail of the spectrum. At k = 24 — 3% of the dimensions — the sneaker, the trouser and the boot are unmistakable; the bag's texture is the first thing to go, because texture lives in the small eigenvalues.
