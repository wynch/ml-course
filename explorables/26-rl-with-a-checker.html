RL with a checker

Module 26 · Acting and planning ← All explorables

A program checks each answer: 1 if the sum is right, 0 if not. Reinforcement learning then makes the replies that scored 1 more likely. This page shows the two choices every such method makes: how to turn a group's rewards into advantages (how much to push each reply up or down), and how to spread that push over a reply's characters. Then it replays what the module's runs did to a small model that adds 3-digit numbers: pass@1 (right in one try) against pass@k (right at least once in k tries).

1 · One group, three advantages

One problem, a group of replies sampled from the model. Click a reply to flip it between right and wrong; long replies are 28 characters (worked), short ones 4 (direct). The bars show each reply's advantage under the three rules, and the push each of its characters gets once the loss spreads it over the reply: GRPO divides by the reply's own length, Dr. GRPO and RLOO by the same 32 for every reply.

2 · What training did, step by step

The module's runs, mean of 3 seeds: 300 steps of 32 sums × 8 replies. Pick what to plot. The base model writes worked replies a quarter of the time, and those are right far more often.

3 · pass@k, before and after

Each of the 500 test sums got 128 samples from the base and from each trained model (seed 0). Slide k: pass@k is the chance that at least one of k tries is right, estimated from the 128. If training only narrowed the model onto fewer answers, the base would catch up, or pass it, at large k.
