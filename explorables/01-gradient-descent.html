Gradient descent, and the graph that computes the gradient

Module 01 · Foundations ← All explorables

Two halves of one idea. On the left, what a gradient is for: a ball rolls downhill on a non-convex loss surface, one p ← p − lr·∇f step at a time. Below it, where the gradient comes from: the exact computation graph from module 01, o = tanh(x1·w1 + x2·w2 + b), stepped forward then backward.

Try this: push the learning rate past 0.35 and watch the ball stop settling; past 1.3 and watch it leave the chart entirely. Add momentum and it can roll straight through a shallow local minimum. Then click through the graph panel one step at a time and check the arithmetic — the numbers are the module's real ones.

What to notice

  • The gradient only knows the slope under the ball. Drop it in the upper-left basin at lr = 0.08, momentum = 0 and it settles into the local minimum at f = −0.750 — never learning that f = −1.097 exists two ridges away.
  • Momentum is a velocity, not a bigger step. With momentum = 0.9 the same run carries speed over the ridge and lands somewhere else entirely. It is the same v ← μv − lr·g; p ← p + v rule as module 02's SGD(momentum=0.9).
  • Too-high learning rates fail in two distinct ways. Around lr ≈ 0.35–1.2 the ball oscillates across a basin forever (the step overshoots the curvature, |1 − lr·λ| > 1); above lr ≈ 1.3 it leaves the chart and the loss runs away to infinity.
  • The decay schedule buys you both. Module 01 starts at lr = 1.0 and ends at 0.1: big early steps to travel, small late steps to settle. Turn the checkbox on with a high starting rate and watch the trail tighten as k grows.
  • Backward is one rule applied everywhere. In panel 2 every backward step multiplies the gradient arriving from above by a local derivative: ×1 for a sum (it just routes), ×(the other input) for a product, ×(1 − o²) for tanh. That is why w2.grad = 0 — its partner x2 is 0, so nothing the weight does matters here.
  • Reverse topological order is not optional. Step through backward and watch x1w1+x2w2 receive its gradient before it passes anything to x1·w1. Visit a node early and you would hand on an incomplete sum.
