Prompts as parameters

Module 34 · Using models well ← All explorables

A frozen Qwen3-1.7B labels questions by the kind of thing they ask for. Its weights never change; only the instruction in front of each question does. Four optimisers rewrite that instruction against a fixed score on 60 training questions, then meet questions they never saw. Every number here comes from the module's own runs (README).

1 · The idea in five slides

The prompt is the parameter

A network's output depends on its weights θ and its input x. Here θ stays fixed and we tune the text p placed before x:

p* = argmaxp accuracy( fθ(p, x), y ) over the training questions

The score is fixed: the label whose first token the model rates highest. No sampling, so the same instruction always gets the same score.

The search space is every string. There is no gradient with respect to p: we can only score strings the optimiser writes.

Three sets, one budget

  • train: 60 questions, 10 per label. The optimisers see their scores.
  • held-out: 300 more from the same file, 50 per label. Never seen. Train minus held-out is overfitting.
  • test: TREC's own 500 test questions, in their own label mix.
  • budget: training questions scored per run, the same for every optimiser.

Four optimisers

  • APE: show the optimiser six labelled questions, ask what the instruction was; keep the best; reword the top four.
  • OPRO: show it the twenty best instructions so far with their scores; ask for a better one.
  • TextGrad-style: a critique of the current errors is the "gradient"; a rewrite that follows it is the "step"; undo a step that lowers the score.
  • GEPA-style: reflect on six answers with feedback; keep the child only if it wins those six; draw parents from those best on some question.

What is like gradient descent, and what is not

Like it: a loss on training data; a step that tries to lower it; a held-out set to catch overfitting; a budget.

Not like it: no derivative exists. A "text gradient" is a paragraph of advice, not a direction in a space with a metric. Its step size is unknown and its sign is often wrong: most edits do not help.

Every method here is a search: propose, score, keep. The language model makes the proposals far better than random strings.
