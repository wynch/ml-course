Diffusion on a spiral

Module 09 · Breadth ← All explorables

Destroying structure is free; learning to reverse the destruction is generative modelling. Below, a 2-D spiral dissolves into pure Gaussian noise as you drag the timestep — that half is closed-form algebra, nothing learned. Then the real 50,178-parameter MLP trained by module 09 runs live in your browser, 200 denoising steps, turning noise back into the spiral. Scrub the timeline, watch the learned vector field sharpen as t drops, then hit play on the reverse process.

What to notice

  • The forward half is boring on purpose. Drag t and the colour bands scramble; by t≈199, √ᾱ_t has fallen to 2.5e-4 and the cloud is indistinguishable from N(0, I). No network was involved — this is one line of algebra.
  • Cosine vs linear is not cosmetic. Flip the schedule and read √ᾱ_t at t = 199: cosine reaches 0.0002 — the data is gone — but the original linear schedule stalls at 0.364, still 36% signal, with the spiral plainly visible. Linear's β range (1e-4 → 0.02) was tuned for T = 1000; at this module's T = 200 it never finishes the job, so a sampler starting from N(0, I) would begin somewhere the model never saw. That is why the cosine schedule is the default here — and why swapping it is exercise (b).
  • The vector field is the whole learned object. At high t the arrows point broadly inward, toward the data's centre of mass — the net only knows "you are too far out". Slide t down and the field sharpens into the spiral's shape, arrows pointing at the nearest arm. That is −ε̂ ∝ ∇log p(x_t), the score.
  • The reverse process is stochastic. Each Sample again starts from different noise and injects fresh noise at every step, so no two runs trace the same path — yet they all land on the same spiral. Turn on trails to see the paths.
  • A loss of 0.37 is a success. The target is unit-variance noise, so predicting ε perfectly is impossible — much of it is irreducible. What matters is that the residual structure the net did learn is enough to reassemble the spiral.
