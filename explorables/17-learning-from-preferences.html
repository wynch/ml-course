Learning from preferences

Module 17 · Other recipes ← All explorables

A judge never says how good a reply is; it says which of two replies it prefers. The Bradley-Terry model turns such choices into scores: item i beats item j with probability σ(si − sj). DPO uses the same model with one twist: a reply's score is β times how much more likely the trained model makes it than the frozen reference does. Below: drag scores and watch the probabilities, move β and watch one pair's loss, then read what the module measured.

1 · Bradley-Terry: drag the scores

Six items on a score line. Drag a dot; the table gives P(row beats column) = σ(srow − scol). Only differences matter: slide every dot by the same amount and nothing changes. The button simulates a judge that follows these probabilities, then fits scores to its choices alone (hollow rings), as section 1 of the module does.

Press “Simulate and refit” to fit scores from simulated choices.

2 · One pair's DPO loss as β moves

For a prompt with a chosen reply yw and a rejected one yl, each log-ratio is log π(y|x) − log πref(y|x): how many nats more likely the trained model makes the reply than the reference. The implicit reward is β times the log-ratio, and the loss is Bradley-Terry's: −log σ(rw − rl). The starting values are one held-out pair under a random, untrained LoRA adapter (module section 2).
